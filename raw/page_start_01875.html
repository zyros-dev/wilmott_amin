Friends, yesterday I shared my initial thoughts about fitting the new model to data. But I feel that it was a strategy that lacked simplicity. At least at the start when we are not familiar with everything in the new method, we have to discover algorithms that are very simple and easy to use. 
I have been thinking of very simple and easy to use methods today and want to share some ideas with friends. 
I am copying relevant evolution equation of a random variable from a previous post.  I have slightly modified the equation as

[$] \, X_{t_1}(Z,Z_0) \, = \, X_{t_0}(Z_0) \, + \, h_0(Z_0) \, + \, h_1(Z_0) Z \,  + \, h_2(Z_0) \, (Z^2\, - \, 1) \, \,[$]         Equation(1)

Suppose we want to find the dynamics of weekly evolution of a stock. And we want to find the coefficients of various Z-series in above equation so that moments of weekly evolution of the random variable over the training data match with the moments given by the above equation.

First we deal with the moments of simple returns/innovations as 

[$] \, X_{t_1}(Z,Z_0) \, - \, X_{t_0}(Z_0) \, = \, h_0(Z_0) \, + \, h_1(Z_0) Z \,  + \, h_2(Z_0) \, (Z^2\, - \, 1) \, \,[$]
[$]\, dX_t \, =  \, h_0(Z_0) \, + \, h_1(Z_0) Z \,  + \, h_2(Z_0) \, (Z^2\, - \, 1) \, \,[$]                                                Equation(2)

We want to exogeneously specify the functions that would later be replaced by appropriate Z-series and try to match the unconditional moments given the conditional evolution form we have specified. So we specify a conditional evolution form as

[$]\, dX_t \, =  a_0 \, f_0(X_t) \, +\, a_1 \, f_1(X_t) Z \,  +\, a_2 \, f_2(X_t) \, (Z^2\, - \, 1) \, \,[$]                        Equation(3)

where [$] f_0(X_t)[$] , [$]f_1(X_t) [$] , [$]f_2(X_t) [$] are exogenous functions specified by us and these can easily be evaluated at the start of each weekly time interval. We want to find out the coefficients [$]\, a_0 \,[$] , [$]\, a_1 \,[$] , [$]\, a_2 \,[$] so that moments of above equation calculated over the training interval of T weeks match with the unconditional moments of evolution of weekly simple returns.

These moments of  unconditional weekly simple returns can be found over a training period of T weeks as

[$] \, RMu(p) \, = \, \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \, {dX(t)}^p \, /T\, = \, \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \, {(X_{t+1} \, - \, X{t})}^p \,/T [$]   Equation(4)

Since we have exogeneously specified the form of functions  [$] f_0(X_t)[$] , [$]f_1(X_t) [$] , [$]f_2(X_t) [$]  in Equation(3), some function forms will get a better fit to data while other functions would give a poorer fit to moments so we would have to play to see which is the best function form so that best match of moments are is found with the data.
Please note that when finding the moments of Equation(3) for matching them with empirical unconditional moments, we would have to find all the cross moments between functions of various orders till eight order of all the functions involved. These functions involved are given by  [$] f_0(X_t)[$] , [$]f_1(X_t) [$] , [$]f_2(X_t) [$] . All these cross moments of various orders between functions can easily be found from the data. Like [$]\, \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \, {f_0(X_{t})}^{p0} \,  {f_1(X{t})}^{p1} \,/T[$] 
So given a set of exogeneous functions specified by us, we would have to find the best values of coefficients [$]\, a_0 \,[$] , [$]\, a_1 \,[$] , [$]\, a_2 \,[$] so that moments of the Equation(3) calculated over the training interval of T weeks match with the unconditional moments of evolution of weekly simple returns given by Equation(4). These coefficients can have interpretations as drift parameter, volatility of first hermite and volatility of second hermite polynomial respectively. Again since the match to unconditional moments of all order might not be perfect, we would want to try various forms of functions [$] f_0(X_t)[$] , [$]f_1(X_t) [$] , [$]f_2(X_t) [$]  so that best match to unconditional moments of equation(4) is achieved.
Once we have calculated analytic dynamics of evolution over a week, we would have to extend the analysis to evolution of random variable between one week and two weeks since weekly dynamics cannot be linearly added/interpolated(by matching/addition of weekly moments with appropriate analytics) due to mean reversion in the random variables/stocks over time. So we would also gain a better understanding of mean reversion as we work with the method.

Though I am not perfectly sure, another possible caveat is that for random variables with heavy tailed distributions, the Equation(1) may not be enough and might have to be replaced by another equation of the form

[$] \, X_{t_1}(Z,Z_0) \, = \, X_{t_0}(Z_0) \, + \, h_0(Z_0) \, + \, h_1(Z_0) Z \,  + \, h_2(Z_0) \, (Z^2\, - \, 1) \,\,  + \, h_3(Z_0) \, (Z^3\, - \, 3 \, Z) \, \,[$]         Equation(5)
This is because first two hermites might not be able to generate a heavy tailed distribution and we might have to add the third hermite in order to add heavy tails.

Friends, if we want to calculate the parameters of an actual SDE from data, things are not as simple as I mentioned in previous post. This is because there are not three simple coefficients as I mentioned in previous post. volatility and drift parameters and there products are spread in each of the terms. And if we include third order terms, there are all sort of products of volatilities and drift parameters. Let me do some simple analysis to explain that.

Suppose we have a general SDE in original coordinates. The SDE is given as

[$] \, dX(t) \, = \, \mu(X(t)) \, dt \,+\, \sigma(X(t)) \, dZ(t) [$]            Equation(1)

We know that a second order expansion of the above SDE is given as (using the method we have previously learnt on this forum)

[$] \, X_{t_1} \, = \, X(t_0) \, + \, \mu(X(t_0)) \Delta t \, + \, \sigma(X(t_0)) Z \sqrt{\Delta t} \\ + \, \frac{\partial \mu(X(t_0))}{\partial X} \, \mu(X(t_0)) \, \frac{{\Delta t}^2}{2} \, + \, \frac{\partial \mu(X(t_0))}{\partial X} \, \sigma(X(t_0)) \, (1 \, - \, \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} ) \, Z \, {\Delta t}^{1.5} \\ +\, .5  \, \frac{\partial^2 \mu(X(t_0))}{\partial X^2} \, {\sigma(X(t_0))}^2 \, \frac{{\Delta t}^2}{2} \, \\ + \, \frac{\partial \sigma(X(t_0))}{\partial X} \, \mu(X(t_0)) \,  (\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} ) \, Z \, {\Delta t}^{1.5} \, + \, \frac{\partial \sigma(X(t_0))}{\partial X} \, \sigma(X(t_0)) \, (Z^2\, - \, 1) \, \frac{\Delta t}{2} \\ +\, .5  \, \frac{\partial^2 \sigma(X(t_0))}{\partial X^2} \, {\sigma(X(t_0))}^2 \, \,  (\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} ) \, Z \, {\Delta t}^{1.5} \,[$]         Equation(2)

suppose 
[$]\mu(X(t)) \, = \, \mu_1 \, {X(t)}^{\beta_1} \, + \, \mu_2 \, {X(t)}^{\beta_2} [$]
and 
[$]\sigma(X(t)) \, = \, \sigma_0 \, {X(t)}^{\gamma}[$]  

When we substitute the above functions in previous equation and collect for zeroth hermite, first hermite and 2nd hrmite terms, we do not get three constants as I mentioned in previous post. Instead the parameters [$]\mu_1[$] , [$]\mu_2 [$] and [$]\sigma_0[$] and their all sort of products appear in the two hermites equation. If we want to find only [$]\mu_1[$] , [$]\mu_2 [$] and [$]\sigma_0[$] from the moment matching exercise as mentioned in previous post, this is still very doable but we will have to calculate a lot of terms that are multiplied by these parameters again and again during optimization, But it should not be very difficult at all. 
Also instead of specifying functions, we can also find the powers [$]\beta_1[$], [$]\beta_2[$] and [$] \gamma[$] from optimization(so that moments of the (two)hermites equation match the moments of data perfectly well)  on top of three parameters mentioned above.
However, the important point is that if we are trying to find the parameters of SDE equation from some starting point, we do not need Z-series as coefficients in two hermites equation, rather we can easily use the functions used in the SDE and their moments instead of the Z-series.
And we can extend the above method to evaluation of stochastic volatility models by including the product with stochastic volatility function from other SDE. 
I will try to take an arbitrary SDE and generate data using monte carlo and then write a numerical algorithm that fits the parameters of the SDE to data generated by the monte carlo of the SDE itself. All this exercise would be based on moment matching and optimization. But writing such a program can take a few days.

Friends, I have tried to play with standardized moments of difference of successive realizations (what I have been calling innovations) of stochastic processes represented by SDEs. And it turns out to be very interesting. I will come back with a more detailed post tomorrow but several common sense observations are here. Higher moments increase quite fast as power exponent gamma in volatility term increases. For a given data, if you roughly know your volatility, it could be possible to use two benchmarks for example gamma =.95 and gamma=.5, and list the standardized moments of benchmarks using monte carlo with large number of paths. And then see where the standardized moments of the given realization of stochastic process fall in between.
Similarly in bessel coordinates, a little experience and playing with the SDE models, helps establish how standardized moments change with gamma. In general in bessel coordinates, these moments are generally relatively closer to moments of standard normal in an identifiable way within a reasonably broad range.
For SDEs with mean reversion,as mean reversion gets larger, higher standardized moments continue to become quite relatively smaller when compared to cases with no mean reversion or smaller mean reversion with the same gamma both in bessel and original coordinates. So higher standardized moments(especially even) when compared with no mean reversion benchmarks are a great way to tell about the possibility of mean reversion in the data.
Again these are not moments of cross-sectional distribution of the SDE, rather these are moments of distribution of innovations of the SDE process though some properties might possibly be similar in both.
Earlier when I was playing with mean reversion, I was able to quite accurately back out the input volatility from data generated by mean reversion SDEs and there did not seem to be any decrease in volatility or variance. Though I might be wrong, It seems that this is a property of SDE innovations that backed out volatility remains constant and it must not be confused with decreasing volatility in cross-sectional distribution. However, as opposed to variance, higher standardized moments of SDE innovations for mean reverting SDEs were significantly smaller than  standardized moments of similar SDEs without mean reversion with eighth standardized moment showing the most pronounced decrease. 
In quite many cases, there may not be a need to standardize and we can compare moments directly but standardization of moments helps us understand and compare with normal moments and also to keep a better idea about relative magnitude of moments.
I am sure there can be very fruitful research in this area. And I will continue to explore further in coming days.

This program generates an SDE data with monte carlo simulation using just one path i.e one realization of SDE stochastic process for N Time steps. Then given the SDE random increments, we match moments/second moments of SDE increments with analytic moments/variance of the SDE given the SDE starting value at each point.
In this program I just match second moments since it is quick and effective for volatility. But you can alter the moment match criteria in function 
CalculateMomentsMatchObjFunction0 to include higher moments in the criteria of fit.
We iterate over volatility coefficients to find the best fit of 2nd moment/moments i.e where average analytic moments of SDE increments
match best with the data moments of SDE increments.
The program also iterates over drift coefficients but that is ineffective and their matching has not much effect. It can only be used to find
volatility coefficients if structure of the SDE volatility is known. SDE is
 dXt = (mu1 Xt^beta1 + mu2 Xt^beta2) dt + sigma0 * Xt^ gamma * dZt
 We suppose that gamma is known to us and this program does not calculate  gamma. It calculates sigma0 only given the data.
