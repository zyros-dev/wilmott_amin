Friends, in this post I want to explain the ideas behind hermite Z-series regressions. Even though though these ideas are not difficult at all, I will keep things simple and first explain one variable hermite Z-series regression, and then generalize it to multivariate hermite Z-series regressions. 

In the first step of the regression, we have to find a Z-series representation of the marginal density of explained variable and explanatory variable. This would usually be done by calculating moments of the explained and explanatory random variable and then converting them into Z-series using method (and posted matlab program) I have previously suggested on this forum. When we know that analytics between explained variable and explanatory variable are more involved, we can easily fit up to eight moments to find Z-series if data set is large enough (and then run a hermite regression to 7th order polynomial). In my initial experiments with hermite regressions, I just used first four moments. First three moments were fixed at their observed values and fourth moment was fixed at positivity condition (Read here 
about first derivative positivity condition: https://forum.wilmott.com/viewtopic.php?f=4&t=99702&start=1710#p874464) of first derivative dXdZ so that we could get a valid density.
Once we find a Z-series representation by fitting first four moment, it would be a third order polynomial. First the Z-Series of regressand
[$]Y=a_0 + a_1 \, Z_1 + a_2 \, Z_1^2 + a_3 \, Z_1^3 [$]  Eq(A)
Then we find Z-series of regressor
[$]X=b_0 + b_1 \, Z_2 + b_2 \, Z_2^2 + b_3 \, Z_2^3 [$]  Eq(B)


We want to remind the friends that for every Z-series in powers of Z, there is an equivalent Z-series in hermite polynomials given as

[$]Y=ah_0 + ah_1 \, Z_1 + ah_2 \, (Z_1^2-1) + ah_{3} \, (Z_1^3-3 \, Z_1) [$]
Then we find Z-series of regressor
[$]X=bh_0 + bh_1 \, Z_2 + bh_2 \, (Z_2^2 \, -\, 1) + bh_3 \, (Z_2^3 \, - \, 3 \, Z_2)[$]  

Once we have found the Z-series, the first step is to find Z-values of regressors and regressand data with respect to their marginal density. For each data point for every variable, we find out corresponding value of Z by inverting the Z-series relationship given in equations A and B. This is non-linear standardization of data as opposed to linear standardization used as ([$]Z=\frac{(X \, - \, \mu)}{\sigma}[$]). Again we are standardizing the data by finding inverse of higher degree polynomial as opposed to linear standardization resulting from inverse of first degree polynomial. In what follows, I will call this non-linear standardized values of observed data points as Z-values of data. These standardized Z-values are found from observed data points by Newton root-finding algorithm after giving an intelligent starting point.(This is included in my matlab program earlier posted)

Suppose we have a total of P (as in total paths of joint evolution of both stochastic processes)  joint observations of stochastic processes [$]X[$] and Y. Here 
[$] Z_{1,p} [$] denotes our standardized Z-value corresponding to pth observation of data of Y and  [$] Z_{2,p} [$] denotes our standardized Z-value corresponding to pth observation of data of explanatory variable [$]X[$]
For Finding Correlation between first hermite polynomial components, we write the equation for the correlation between first hermites of one regressor and regressand as

[$]\rho_{XY,1} \, = \,  \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{P} ah_1 \, Z_{1,p} \, bh_1 \, Z_{2,p} \,}{\sqrt{\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{P} ah_1^2 \, {Z_{1,p}}^2 \,\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{P} \, bh_1^2 \, {Z_{2,p}}^2 \,}}[$]

Again, Please notice that for calculation of above equation, we first have to find the specific values of [$] Z_{1,p}[$] and [$]Z_{2,p}[$] that are associated with particular pth observation of [$]X_p[$] and [$]Y_p[$] by some inversion method.
For Finding Correlation between second hermite polynomial components, we write the equation for the correlation between second hermite polynomial of observation Z-values of one regressor and regressand as

[$]\rho_{XY,2} \, = \,  \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{P} ah_2 \, ({Z_{1,p}}^2-1) \, bh_2 \, ({Z_{2,p}}^2-1) \,}{\sqrt{\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{P} ah_2^2 \, {(Z_{1,p}^2-1)}^2 \,\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{P} \, bh_2^2 \, {({Z_{2,p}}^2-1})^2 \,}}[$]

similarly for nth hermite polynaomial correlations, we would write as

[$]\rho_{XY,n} \, = \,  \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{P} ah_n \, H_n(Z_1) \, bh_n \, H_n(Z_2) \,}{\sqrt{\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{P} ah_n^2 \, {H_n(Z_1)}^2 \,\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{P} \, bh_n^2 \, {H_n(Z_2)}^2 \,}}[$]

Please note that correlations are in joint products of hermite polynomials associated with observation Z-values. Their coefficients are only required for covariances between hermite polynomials.

We know from simple regression theory that once we know correlation between two variables, we can write the regression equation between them as

[$]Y \, = \, \mu_Y \, + \, \rho_{XY} \frac{s_Y}{s_X} \, (X\, - \, \mu_X) [$]

So we could write a variant of the above traditional formula in our new case (please notice that correlated contribution from Z-values of regressors is being scaled by SD/volatility of marginal density of regressand) with hermite correlations as

[$]Y \, = \, ah_0 \, + \, \rho_{XY,1} \, ah_1 \, Z_1 \, + \, \rho_{XY,2} \, ah_2 \, ({Z_1}^2 -1) \, + \, \rho_{XY,3} \, ah_3 \, ({Z_1}^3 -3 Z_1) + ...+\, \rho_{XY,n} \, ah_n \, H_n(Z_1)[$]

Friends, I am trying to work with interesting examples of hermite orthogonal regressions. But I still continue to run into some problems with earlier work. For example we have two different methods to construct Z-series of a random variable. 1. Through fitting its first few moments.  2. Through mapping the density of random variable on standard normal density and extracting hermite coefficients after inner product with hermite polynomials of various order.
Problem is that both of these methods do not necessarily satisfy the positivity condition associated with dXdZ > 0 all over the density domain. When we worked with these methods, positivity condition of first derivative was not in our mind and I only realized later that it is also important. 
When I try playing with data, this problem with earlier written algorithms continue to come up again and again since they do not ensure positivity condition of first derivative   dXdZ > 0. I want to spend next two to three days to re-write my earlier algorithms written with both methods through fitting of moments and through extraction of hermite coefficients and embed the positivity condition in the algorithms so that we get a nice smooth density with very good tails that we could use in analytics everywhere.
