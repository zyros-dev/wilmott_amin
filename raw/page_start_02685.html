Friends, as I have stated somewhere here earlier that mean reversion can be measured from a ratio of second moment and fourth moment. We just have to look at standardized fourth moment for knowledge of mean reversion as standardized fourth moment is already fourth moment divided by square of second moment. 

We know that standard normal has a standardized fourth moment of three. This is reference standardized kurtosis. Above it, the financial assets diffusion is more trending with more positive auto-correlations and below it the diffusion is more mean reverting with more negative auto-correlation. 

In my trading scenarios, when local standardized kurtosis was more than four, I could safely assume that market is mostly trending with positive correlations. Similarly, we can  find a number below three at which we can safely assume that market is mean reverting. 

If you have a strategy with trending market i.e. with positive correlations, you would like to avoid trading when standardized kurtosis is not safely towards your side i.e. it is not more than four or any other appropriate limit you have found from your experiments. 

Similarly for trending market and there is a grey area between both sides when market is not decisively trending or mean reverting where it would be difficult to know how to take sides.

I have not found any other indicator so powerful when trying to know whether the market is trending or mean reverting.

Friends, I have been playing with the old transition probabilities program from time to time when I could be free. 

I think it is difficult to find precise transition probabilities according to an arbitrary stochastic data generating program. The reason is that there is usually no unique transition probability between two marginal distributions. And transition probabilities could be generated in so many ways that they fit two marginal distributions. We have more information when we are given joint pairs of starting and ending distribution but I still think that we still need to specify some extra structure or conditions on the transition probabilities calculation program for it to be able to match transition probabilities with data generation program.

My Transition probabilities generation program seems to work well with lognormal data but for CEV type SDEs, it has some slack. I will give graph examples to explain this. My method to generate transition probabilities goes as follows. I take an SDE with starting point one and take data observations at starting future point  Ts and ending future point Tz. This data is used to find transition probabilities between input data at Ts and output data at Tz. With this analytic formula, we can project transition probabilities at any time point Tz while being projected from delta source anywhere on Ts. Then we check for how good these analytic calculations are by using the original SDE to numerically generate transition probabilities at time Tz from various delta sources at Ts.

We use a general SDE of the form.
                                                      X(Tz) = X(Ts) + kappa * (theta - X)  dt + epsilon * X^gamma *dz

Friends, before I post the graphs of comparison of analytic and numerical transition probabilities of SDEs, I want to tell the friends about the methodology with which these graphs were made.

Our SDE is
                                       dV(t) = kappa * ( theta - V(t) ) dt +sigma * V(t)^gamma dZ(t)
or in latex form

 \[ dV(t) = \kappa  \,( \theta - V )\, dt\, +\, \sigma V^{\gamma} \, dZ(t) \]

\[V(TT1)\,=X\]
\[V(TT2)\,=Y\]
where both TT1 and TT2 are greater than zero and TT2 > TT1.

First I chose parameters of an SDE and simulated it. I took the values of paths of SDE at 18th step(TT1) (and called them X) and also the values of paths of SDE at 36th step (TT2) (and called them Y). We are now given pairs of X and Y from the same paths. Using this joint data, we construct transition probabilities of  P(Y|X=X0) or more precisely P(Y(TT2)|X(TT1)=X0) from any delta point X0 on the distribution of X. This is done both analytically from our algorithm and also numerically from the SDE.  For numerically finding the transition probabilities, we simply take the SDE and start it at delta origin X=X0 and simulate it for time (TT2 - TT1). Analytic calculation does not know the SDE at all but we do use the value of gamma parameter of of the SDE  in our calculations but this gamma parameter can be numerically estimated from the data points. In fact, we believe that in mean reverting SDEs, our use of the value of gamma directly from SDE is suboptimal since effective value of gamma changes due to strong mean reversion and our results might improve if we directly estimate gamma from the SDE data.

Friends, first I want to describe the analytical procedure for the calculation of transition probabilities  (Y|X=X0) where Y=V(TT2) and X=V(TT1) and V is a stochastic process.

1. First we find a general two dimensional distribution in the form of 2D Hermite series of Y|X from the least squares regression as we had earlier described. This 2D Hermite series is given as

\begin{align}

\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \,&=\, \Big[\, cH_{0,0} \, + \, cH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, cH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{1,0} \, + \, cH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, cH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{2,0} \, + \, cH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, cH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

2. We are also given a 1D Hermite series of X from its univariate distribution. We invert this distribution to find \(Z_{X_0}\) corresponding to \(X_0\) which is the delta source from where we want to calculate transition probabilities.
if Hermite series of X is given as

\[\,X(Z_x)\,=\, ah_0 \, +\, ah_1 \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, ah_2 \,H_2(Z_x) \,+\, ah_3 \,H_3(Z_x) \,+\,\ldots\]

then \(Z_{X_0}\) is the value of \(Z_x\) for which the following equation holds

\[\,X_0\,=\, ah_0 \, +\, ah_1 \,H_1(Z_{X_0}) \,+\, ah_2 \,H_2(Z_{X_0}) \,+\, ah_3 \,H_3(Z_{X_0}) \,+\,\ldots\]

The above inversion to find \(Z_{X_0}\)  corresponding to \(X_0\) is usually done by some iterative or Newton search algorithm.

3. Now we collapse the above two dimensional Hermite series of \(\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\) by substituting the numerical value of   \(Z_{X_0}\) in this equation by equating \(Z_x\,=\, Z_{X_0}\) in the 2D Hermite series equation. This will give us one dimensional conditional Hermite series in terms of \(Z_y\). This conditional Hermite series can be written as

\[\,Y(Z_y)\,=\, bh_0 \, +\, bh_1 \,H_1(Z_y) \,+\, bh_2 \,H_2(Z_y) \,+\, bh_3 \,H_3(Z_y) \,+\,\ldots\]

Please recall that above one dimensional Hermite series is obtained by collapsing the 2D conditional Hermite series of \(\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\) by substituting the numerical value of   \(Z_x\,=\,Z_{X_0}\).

4.  To find a proper conditional distribution corresponding to delta source at \(X=X_0\) as in transition probabilities, We have to divide the above univariate Hermite series by two terms. First term is \({E[X]}^{\gamma}\). Second term is \({X_0}^{(1-\gamma)}\).  So we write

\[\,Y(Z_y)\,| \left(X=X_0\right)\, =\frac{\, bh_0 \, +\, bh_1 \,H_1(Z_y) \,+\, bh_2 \,H_2(Z_y) \,+\, bh_3 \,H_3(Z_y) \,+\,\ldots}{{E[X]}^{\gamma} \,{X_0}^{(1-\gamma)} } \]

 5. In the last step, we have to replace the mean of above conditional series by conditional mean of \(E[Y|(X=X_0)]\). This conditional mean is easily found by regressing one dimensional unconditional univariate Z-series of Y on one dimensional Z-series of X found by a univariate Z-series least squares regression. If we represent the numerical value of  conditional mean as \(E[Y|(X=X_0)]\), our final expression for the Hermite series of conditional transition probabilities becomes 

\[\,Y(Z_y)\,| \left(X=X_0\right)\, =\, E[Y|(X=X_0)] + \frac{\, bh_1 \,H_1(Z_y) \,+\, bh_2 \,H_2(Z_y) \,+\, bh_3 \,H_3(Z_y) \,+\,\ldots}{{E[X]}^{\gamma} \,{X_0}^{(1-\gamma)} } \]

In other words, we can now write the Hermite series of conditional transition probabilities as

\[\,\,Y(Z_y)\,| \left(X=X_0\right)\, \,=\, ch_0 \, +\, ch_1 \,H_1(Z_y) \,+\, ch_2 \,H_2(Z_y) \,+\, ch_3 \,H_3(Z_y) \,+\,\ldots\]

where 
\[ch_0\,=\, E[Y|(X=X_0)]\,=\, E[Y|(Z_x=Z_{X_0})]\, \]
\[ch_1\,=\, \frac{\, bh_1 }{{E[X]}^{\gamma} \,{X_0}^{(1-\gamma)} } \]
\[ch_2\,=\, \frac{\, bh_2 }{{E[X]}^{\gamma} \,{X_0}^{(1-\gamma)} } \]
\[ch_3\,=\, \frac{\, bh_3 }{{E[X]}^{\gamma} \,{X_0}^{(1-\gamma)} } \]
and so on for nth coefficient.

Please note that \(E[X]\,=\,ah_0\) because in a hermite series constant term is equal to mean of the random variable, so for nth coefficient, we have equivalently

\[ch_n\,=\, \frac{\, bh_n }{{ah_0}^{\gamma} \,{X_0}^{(1-\gamma)} } \]


In all of the above calculations, we did not use any knowledge of parameters of the SDE except for parameter gamma \((\gamma)\). This gamma can easily be numerically calculated from the distribution of X and we believe that for strong mean-reverting SDEs and other difficult SDEs, it will be more accurate to calculate this gamma from the distribution itself.

Friends, first I want to describe the analytical procedure for the calculation of transition probabilities  (Y|X=X0) where Y=V(TT2) and X=V(TT1) and V is a stochastic process.

1. First we find a general two dimensional distribution in the form of 2D Hermite series of Y|X from the least squares regression as we had earlier described. This 2D Hermite series is given as

\begin{align}

\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \,&=\, \Big[\, cH_{0,0} \, + \, cH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, cH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{1,0} \, + \, cH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, cH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{2,0} \, + \, cH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, cH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

2. We are also given a 1D Hermite series of X from its univariate distribution. We invert this distribution to find \(Z_{X_0}\) corresponding to \(X_0\) which is the delta source from where we want to calculate transition probabilities.
if Hermite series of X is given as

\[\,X(Z_x)\,=\, ah_0 \, +\, ah_1 \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, ah_2 \,H_2(Z_x) \,+\, ah_3 \,H_3(Z_x) \,+\,\ldots\]

then \(Z_{X_0}\) is the value of \(Z_x\) for which the following equation holds

\[\,X_0\,=\, ah_0 \, +\, ah_1 \,H_1(Z_{X_0}) \,+\, ah_2 \,H_2(Z_{X_0}) \,+\, ah_3 \,H_3(Z_{X_0}) \,+\,\ldots\]

The above inversion to find \(Z_{X_0}\)  corresponding to \(X_0\) is usually done by some iterative or Newton search algorithm.

3. Now we collapse the above two dimensional Hermite series of \(\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\) by substituting the numerical value of   \(Z_{X_0}\) in this equation by equating \(Z_x\,=\, Z_{X_0}\) in the 2D Hermite series equation. This will give us one dimensional conditional Hermite series in terms of \(Z_y\). This conditional Hermite series can be written as

\[\,Y(Z_y)\,=\, bh_0 \, +\, bh_1 \,H_1(Z_y) \,+\, bh_2 \,H_2(Z_y) \,+\, bh_3 \,H_3(Z_y) \,+\,\ldots\]

Please recall that above one dimensional Hermite series is obtained by collapsing the 2D conditional Hermite series of \(\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\) by substituting the numerical value of   \(Z_x\,=\,Z_{X_0}\).

4.  To find a proper conditional distribution corresponding to delta source at \(X=X_0\) as in transition probabilities, We have to divide the above univariate Hermite series by two terms. First term is \({E[X]}^{\gamma}\). Second term is \({X_0}^{(1-\gamma)}\).  So we write

\[\,Y(Z_y)\,| \left(X=X_0\right)\, =\frac{\, bh_0 \, +\, bh_1 \,H_1(Z_y) \,+\, bh_2 \,H_2(Z_y) \,+\, bh_3 \,H_3(Z_y) \,+\,\ldots}{{E[X]}^{\gamma} \,{X_0}^{(1-\gamma)} } \]

 5. In the last step, we have to replace the mean of above conditional series by conditional mean of \(E[Y|(X=X_0)]\). This conditional mean is easily found by regressing one dimensional unconditional univariate Z-series of Y on one dimensional Z-series of X found by a univariate Z-series least squares regression. If we represent the numerical value of  conditional mean as \(E[Y|(X=X_0)]\), our final expression for the Hermite series of conditional transition probabilities becomes 

\[\,Y(Z_y)\,| \left(X=X_0\right)\, =\, E[Y|(X=X_0)] + \frac{\, bh_1 \,H_1(Z_y) \,+\, bh_2 \,H_2(Z_y) \,+\, bh_3 \,H_3(Z_y) \,+\,\ldots}{{E[X]}^{\gamma} \,{X_0}^{(1-\gamma)} } \]

In other words, we can now write the Hermite series of conditional transition probabilities as

\[\,\,Y(Z_y)\,| \left(X=X_0\right)\, \,=\, ch_0 \, +\, ch_1 \,H_1(Z_y) \,+\, ch_2 \,H_2(Z_y) \,+\, ch_3 \,H_3(Z_y) \,+\,\ldots\]

where 
\[ch_0\,=\, E[Y|(X=X_0)]\,=\, E[Y|(Z_x=Z_{X_0})]\, \]
\[ch_1\,=\, \frac{\, bh_1 }{{E[X]}^{\gamma} \,{X_0}^{(1-\gamma)} } \]
\[ch_2\,=\, \frac{\, bh_2 }{{E[X]}^{\gamma} \,{X_0}^{(1-\gamma)} } \]
\[ch_3\,=\, \frac{\, bh_3 }{{E[X]}^{\gamma} \,{X_0}^{(1-\gamma)} } \]
and so on for nth coefficient.

Please note that \(E[X]\,=\,ah_0\) because in a hermite series constant term is equal to mean of the random variable, so for nth coefficient, we have equivalently

\[ch_n\,=\, \frac{\, bh_n }{{ah_0}^{\gamma} \,{X_0}^{(1-\gamma)} } \]


In all of the above calculations, we did not use any knowledge of parameters of the SDE except for parameter gamma \((\gamma)\). This gamma can easily be numerically calculated from the distribution of X and we believe that for strong mean-reverting SDEs and other difficult SDEs, it will be more accurate to calculate this gamma from the distribution itself.
