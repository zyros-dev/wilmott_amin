Friends, here I want to present the equations for the correction in the semi-analytic method that matches the shape of our analytic density with monte carlo density but first I will present some other equations for friends to be oriented with the problem.
The solution of one dimensional Fokker-Planck Equation was given as

[$] \frac{\partial w_b}{\partial t}=\mu_w \,  + .5 {\sigma_w}^2 \big[ Z_1  \, {(\frac{\partial Z_1}{\partial w})} \, +  \, {(\frac{\partial Z_1}{\partial w})}^2  \frac{\partial^2 w}{\partial {Z_1}^2} \, \big]  \,[$]

In my new implementation for 2D Fokker-Planck PDE, I have bypassed full probability mass conservation equation and have just used a 2D version of the above equation for one dimensional FPE. Though I may be wrong, In my basic exploratory analysis, I found that after mean-correction applied to 2D version of one dimensional FPE, a full-fledged probability mass conservation equation in two dimensional FP PDE is much slower and does not have any special advantages over the 2D version of one dimensional FPE. Both equations yield similar results but a full-fledged probability mass conservation implementation is relatively slower. I used the shape correction on 2D version of one dimensional FPE And you can easily use it for full-fledged probability mass conservation solution with very similar improvement results.
Here I write the 2D version of one dimensional FPE solution as
[$] \frac{\partial y_b(n,m)}{\partial t} =  \mu_y(n,m)  \, +\, .5 \, {\sigma_y}^2 \, {V(n)}^{2 \gamma_V}   \, \big[ Z_2(m) \, {(\frac{\partial Z_2}{\partial y})} + {(\frac{\partial Z_2}{\partial y})}^2 \frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial {Z_2}^2} \big]  [$]

The above solution is valid for each of the 1D asset PDEs on any nth node of the SV SDE.
When shape of the density did not perfectly match with monte carlo density, I tried several possibilities. One thing I could think of was that there might be some slight amount of diffusion in asset PDE arising from SV coordinates. I tried that by adding the following term in previous solution as

[$] -\, .5 \, {\sigma_y}^2 \, {V(n)}^{2 \gamma_V}   \, \big[  {(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w})}^2 \frac{\partial^2 w}{\partial {Z}^2} \big]  [$]
In the above equation Z is the normal random variable associated with the 1D stochastic volatility PDE solution. The addition of above term in the previous solution fixed the mismatch and brought the new semi-analytic density in line with monte carlo density. Interestingly the contribution from this above term is very small and its maximum value per time step at the start of the simulation is roughly one thousandth of a basis point (1.0 * 10^-7) while the maximum value of above term per time step increases to roughly one fourth of a basis point (2.5 * 10^-5) after two years for the typical equations I was trying. So this is a very small correction but over a longer time frame helps to fix the mismatch of our analytic density with the true monte carlo density.

So after adding the new term our full solution would be 
[$] \frac{\partial y_b(n,m)}{\partial t} =  \mu_y(n,m)  \, +\, .5 \, {\sigma_y}^2 \, {V(n)}^{2 \gamma_V}   \, \big[ Z_2(m) \, {(\frac{\partial Z_2}{\partial y})} + {(\frac{\partial Z_2}{\partial y})}^2 \frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial {Z_2}^2} \big]  [$]
[$] -\, .5 \, {\sigma_y}^2 \, {V(n)}^{2 \gamma_V}   \, \big[  {(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w})}^2 \frac{\partial^2 w}{\partial {Z}^2} \big]  [$]

You can make the same correction in full probability mass conservation framework but you might have to slightly alter the last equation by multiplying it with      [$]\frac{\partial Z_2}{\partial y}[$] as I suspect.
Again I have no formal proof for this correction and I tried it while experimenting and it worked very well. So a formal proof for this new equation is an open problem.
One thing I want to add again is that I have added mean-correction on every 1D asset PDE on nth SV PDE node(in 2D version of 1D formal solution) and it is only after mean-correction that I find it to be comparable with full probability mass conservation solution in two dimensions.

[$]X(t)=a_0  \,+ a_1 \, Z + a_2 \, Z^2 + a_3 \, Z^3 +  a_4 \, Z^4 +\, ... [$]

[$]\frac{\partial X(t)}{\partial Z}= a_1 \, + 2 \,a_2 \, Z +3 \, a_3 \, Z^2 +4 \,  a_4 \, Z^3 +\, ... [$] 
[$]\frac{\partial^2 X(t)}{\partial Z^2}=  2 \,a_2 \, +6 \, a_3 \, Z +12 \,  a_4 \, Z^2 +\, ... [$]

[$]X(t+\Delta t)=c_0  \,+ c_1 \, Z + c_2 \, Z^2 + c_3 \, Z^3 +  c_4 \, Z^4 +\, ... [$]

All the coefficients c's at next time step variable [$]X(t+\Delta t)[$] are supposedly unknown and these are the ones we want to find out in our recursions.

For our analytics, we would have to also represent the time dependent drift as a series in Z 

[$]\mu(X(t))=b_0  \,+ b_1 \, Z + b_2 \, Z^2 + b_3 \, Z^3 +  b_4 \, Z^4 +\, ... [$]

We do not want to use finite differences anywhere so in order to find the coefficients [$]b_0, \, b_1, \, b_2[$] and so on we use 

[$]b_0 = \mu(X(Z_0))[$]  where [$]Z_0[$] means calculating at  Z=0.

[$]b_1 = \frac{\partial \mu}{\partial X} \, \frac{\partial X}{\partial Z}=\frac{\partial \mu}{\partial X} \, [a_0  \,+ a_1 \, Z + a_2 \, Z^2 + a_3 \, Z^3 +  a_4 \, Z^4 ] |^{Z=0} [$]

Later we can see if we can get a reasonably fast algorithm in two dimensions.

First we suppose that we can express the solution of X(t) where X is the SDE/PDE variable as 

[$]X(t)=a_0  \,+ a_1 \, Z + a_2 \, Z^2 + a_3 \, Z^3 +  a_4 \, Z^4 +\, ... [$]

[$]\frac{\partial X(t)}{\partial Z}= a_1 \, + 2 \,a_2 \, Z +3 \, a_3 \, Z^2 +4 \,  a_4 \, Z^3 +\, ... [$] 
[$]\frac{\partial^2 X(t)}{\partial Z^2}=  2 \,a_2 \, +6 \, a_3 \, Z +12 \,  a_4 \, Z^2 +\, ... [$]

[$]X(t+\Delta t)=c_0  \,+ c_1 \, Z + c_2 \, Z^2 + c_3 \, Z^3 +  c_4 \, Z^4 +\, ... [$]

All the coefficients c's at next time step variable [$]X(t+\Delta t)[$] are supposedly unknown and these are the ones we want to find out in our recursions.

For our analytics, we would have to also represent the time dependent drift as a series in Z 

[$]\mu(X(t))=b_0  \,+ b_1 \, Z + b_2 \, Z^2 + b_3 \, Z^3 +  b_4 \, Z^4 +\, ... [$]

We do not want to use finite differences anywhere so in order to find the coefficients [$]b_0, \, b_1, \, b_2[$] and so on we use 

[$]b_0 = \mu(X(Z_0))[$]  where [$]Z_0[$] means calculating at  Z=0.

and so we can analytically find the derivatives of drift to any order analytically since derivatives with respect to X are found by analytic differentiation.

Friends, I am working with the initial versions of the proposed series solution in matlab but it is not precise and I believe that lack of precision is due to lack of order in time in our series solution as it is hardly first order accurate in time. I was able to work out more accurate solution in time by using "method of iterated integrals"-like technique. This more accurate solution can also be used in finite difference solutions(that we have been working on since past several months) for a longer stepping in time though I have still not implemented that on my computer. Here are the details for higher order solution to constant CDF lines of SDEs.

We know that in bessel coordinates the solution to constant CDF lines of SDEs is given as

[$]\frac{dw}{dt}=\mu(w) \, + \, .5 {\sigma}^2 Z \frac{\partial Z}{\partial w} - \, .5 {\sigma}^2 \frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} [\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w}]^{-1}[$]

I have changed the third term to a slightly different but equivalent form since we have to take derivatives with respect to w. Here in the above equation, for the two term drift SDEs that we have been working with, the drift term in bessel coordinates is given as
[$]\mu(w) \,= \mu_1 [(1-\gamma)w]^{\frac{\beta_1-\gamma}{1-\gamma}} + \mu_2 [(1-\gamma)w]^{\frac{\beta_2-\gamma}{1-\gamma}} -.5 {\sigma}^2 \gamma [(1-\gamma)w]^{-1}[$]

Now we do an iterated integral like expansion of first equation to find the second order solution in time for the evolution of constant CDF lines of the SDE as

[$]w(t_1)=w(t_0)+ \mu(w(t_0)) \, \int_{t_0}^{t_1} dt + \, .5 {\sigma}^2 Z \frac{\partial Z}{\partial w} \, \int_{t_0}^{t_1} dt + \, \, .5 {\sigma}^2 \,  \frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} [\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w}]^{-1}  \, \int_{t_0}^{t_1} dt[$]
[$]+\frac{\partial \mu(w(t_0))}{\partial w} \frac{dw}{dt} \, \int_{t_0}^{t_1}\int_{t_0}^{t} ds dt+\, \, .5 {\sigma}^2 \frac{\partial}{\partial w}[Z \frac{\partial Z}{\partial w}]  \,\frac{dw}{dt} \, \int_{t_0}^{t_1}\int_{t_0}^{t} ds dt+\, \, .5 {\sigma}^2 \frac{\partial}{\partial w} \big[ \frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} {(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w})}^{-1} \big]  \,\frac{dw}{dt} \, \int_{t_0}^{t_1}\int_{t_0}^{t} ds dt[$] 

All the terms outside the integrals are evaluated at starting time [$]t_0[$] and are constants for the time interval and therefore have been taken out of the time dependent integrals.
Now we explain the constant terms outside the double integrals.
[$]\frac{\partial \mu(w(t_0))}{\partial w} \frac{dw}{dt}=\frac{\partial \mu(w(t_0))}{\partial w} \, \big[\mu(w(t_0)) \, + \, .5 {\sigma}^2 Z \frac{\partial Z}{\partial w} - \, .5 {\sigma}^2 \frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} [\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w}]^{-1} \big] [$] 
Please note that all values and derivatives in previous expression are evaluated at time [$]t_0[$]
Now coming towards second of the terms with double integrals
[$] .5 {\sigma}^2 \frac{\partial}{\partial w}[Z \frac{\partial Z}{\partial w}]  \,\frac{dw}{dt}=.5 {\sigma}^2 \big[{(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w})}^2+Z\frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} \big]\, \big[\mu(w(t_0)) \, + \, .5 {\sigma}^2 Z \frac{\partial Z}{\partial w} - \, .5 {\sigma}^2 \frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} [\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w}]^{-1} \big][$]
Now coming towards third and last term with double integrals
[$]\, .5 {\sigma}^2 \frac{\partial}{\partial w} \big[ \frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} {(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w})}^{-1} \big]  \,\frac{dw}{dt}[$]
[$]=\, .5 {\sigma}^2 \Big[\frac{\partial^3 Z}{\partial w^3} {(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w})}^{-1} - {(\frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2})}^2 {(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w})}^{-2} \Big] \, \big[\mu(w(t_0)) \, + \, .5 {\sigma}^2 Z \frac{\partial Z}{\partial w} - \, .5 {\sigma}^2 \frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} [\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w}]^{-1} \big][$]

which gives us the complete expanded solution as
[$]w(t_1)=w(t_0)+ \mu(w(t_0)) \, \int_{t_0}^{t_1} dt + \, .5 {\sigma}^2 Z \frac{\partial Z}{\partial w} \, \int_{t_0}^{t_1} dt + \, \, .5 {\sigma}^2 \,  \frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} [\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w}]^{-1}  \, \int_{t_0}^{t_1} dt[$]
[$]+\Big[\frac{\partial \mu(w(t_0))}{\partial w} \, \big[\mu(w(t_0)) \, + \, .5 {\sigma}^2 Z \frac{\partial Z}{\partial w} - \, .5 {\sigma}^2 \frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} [\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w}]^{-1} \big] \Big] \, \int_{t_0}^{t_1}\int_{t_0}^{t} ds dt[$]
[$]+\Big[.5 {\sigma}^2 \big[{(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w})}^2+Z\frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} \big]\, \big[\mu(w(t_0)) \, + \, .5 {\sigma}^2 Z \frac{\partial Z}{\partial w} - \, .5 {\sigma}^2 \frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} [\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w}]^{-1} \big] \Big] \, \int_{t_0}^{t_1}\int_{t_0}^{t} ds dt[$]
[$]+\, .5 {\sigma}^2 \Big[\frac{\partial^3 Z}{\partial w^3} {(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w})}^{-1} - {(\frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2})}^2 {(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w})}^{-2} \Big] \, \big[\mu(w(t_0)) \, + \, .5 {\sigma}^2 Z \frac{\partial Z}{\partial w} - \, .5 {\sigma}^2 \frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial w^2} [\frac{\partial Z}{\partial w}]^{-1} \big] \int_{t_0}^{t_1}\int_{t_0}^{t} ds dt[$] 
Please note that all terms and derivatives outside of the integrals are evaluated at start of the simulation time step.
The above is the raw solution that should be second order accurate in time. Since I have not implemented it, there can possibly be stability issues but they would have to be worked out once we have a better understanding of the higher order solution of the constant CDF lines of the SDE density.
