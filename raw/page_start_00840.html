[$]dX(t)=\mu(X) dt + \sigma(X) dz(t)[$]

Dear friends, the new update on the work is that I have worked out the part of calculation of arithmetic path integrals of functions of SDE on the transition probabilities grid. The logic is simple: There are four parts of the calculations.
1. we calculate the value of functions in time along the evolution of the SDE and propagate the caculated contributions with the weights of transition probabilities. This is very simple since the function values can easily be calculated on a static grid.
2. On each time step, we add the evolved "Ito noise" associated with that function to the arithmetic sum contribution added at that step. I have shown earlier how to evolve the functions of SDE on a fixed grid. By "Ito noise", I mean the noise associated with the Ito evolution of the particular function from time zero to the forward time where contribution to the arithmetic sum or arithmetic path integral is being calculated. This specifies the forward variance.
3. For mean-reverting SDEs, we have to specify the correlations of contribution to "arithmetic path integral" at forward time with contributions at all previous time intervals and then add a correlation weighted version of the "Ito-noise" to account for the covariances(or extra variance coming from correlations). This can be calculated very easily on the same fixed grid.
4. Some running variances are added again in the form of "Ito-noise".

This is very simple once you would become familiar with the simple underlying ideas. I would be posting first interim version of my program tomorrow (28th Dec in London). Description of the program.
This program calculates densities of arithmetic weighted sum of functions of SDE variable or arithmetic weighted path integrals of  functions of SDE on transition probabilities grid . The restriction in this first program is that you can find path integrals for only those SDEs that do not have any significant correlation across time. you cannot calculate path integrals of  Mean reverting type SDEs with this program. you can specify any function like fy=theta1*yy(t)^omega1; here theta1 is the weight for arithmetic sum and you could use theta1=dt or (delta t) which is time step size. In the program, I have used the term fy for the function and fyPI for its path integral. %processes takes more time than simulating the original process.
%For both the Ito processes, the value omega1 has to be greater than .75 
%when gamma is smaller than .65;
%when gamma is grerater than .75, you can choose omega1 close to .5.
%for gamma=.95 and close, you can choose any value of omega1 since the 
%process does not reach zero.

%Copyright Ahsan Amin. Infiniti derivatives Technologies.
%Please fell free to connect on linkedin: linkedin.com/in/ahsan-amin-0a53334 
%or skype ahsan.amin2999
%I create a fixed grid of size Nn. The grid is based on bessel/lamperti
%version of the process.Bessel process very approximately remains closer to
%a gaussian process so it also helps in calculating a grid that is 
%relatively uniform in Z which is the driving noise of the SDE. I calculate
%the drift and volatility of the SDE and then calculate a Zt(driving noise) which links
%the starting point of the SDE to neighboring grid points. 
%In the program the state dependent drift is called wMu0dt. if w1 is
%starting point on grid and w2 is final point and state dependent
%volatility(defined here as coefficient of Z in expansion of SDE)is dw0(w1), 
%then Zt(w1,w2)=(w2-w1-wMu0dt)/dw0(w1);This is the Zt(w1,w2) which will be used for

%w is the bessel process and yy is the process in the original coordinates
%so Zt remains the same across original and bessel coordinates.
%For the SDE probabilities calculation, I calculate their evolutions from 
%point w1 at time to to w2 at time t+dt as
%P(w2)=Sum(over w1) Pt(w1,w2)*P(w1);

%and not raw probabilities. Transition probabilities and occupation
%probabilities are calculated to be integrated probability mass over the
%appropriate grid intervals.
%Calculating the drift from bessel version is very accurate but very closer to 
%zero the bessel version is very inaccurate and relatively unstable so
%there I use the original coordinates to determine Zt and associted transition
%probabilities. Transition probabilities only depend on Zt (the driving noise).
%below(and also in the program code) I have used yy as the SDE process in original coordinates.

%When evolving the Ito functionals of the diffusion, we do not evolve their
%probabilities but evolve thier expectations for the appropriate grid
%cells. Since the correct expectation is known at every grid cell, and the
%probability is known from the original process, we can calculate the 
%appropriate value for the Ito-process at the terminal time by dividing the
%expectaation at that cell by integrated probability mass in that cell.
%I have not calculated arbitrary payoffs along the process path since they
%require a specification of forward variance at each cell in forward point
%so you still cannot do swaps or arithmetic addition along the process. You
%can only evolve a proper Ito process starting at initial time.

% REference for the method for path-integrals/dt-integral I used in my
%program is my paper on
% SSRN titled "Solution of Stochastic Volatility Models Using Variance 
%Transition Probabilities and Path Integrals"
%https://ssrn.com/abstract=2149231 or 
%http://dx.doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2149231
%For diffusions going into zero, you may see instabilities, I have not
%corrected for that yet in this initial version.


dt=.125/8;   % Simulation time interval.%For diffusions close to zero
             %decrease dt for accuracy.
Tt=8*8;     % Number of simulation levels. Terminal time= Tt*dt; //.125/8*8*8=1 year; 
OrderA=2;  %For monte carlo order, Keep at two.
OrderM=2;  %For monte corlo order,Keep at two.OrderM has to be equal to OrderA 
            %is the true constraint otherwise you can change the order upto 4. 

x0=1.0;   % starting value of SDE in original coordinates
beta1=0.0;   % the first drift term power.
beta2=1.0;   % Second drift term power.
gamma=.950;   % volatility power.
kappa=0.0;%1.450;   %mean reversion parameter.
theta=0.0;   %mean reversion target

%SDE across different times. A single positive drift term easily 
%qualifies for SDE with negligible correlations. You can also put both
%terms to zero to calculate path integrals of CEV type SDEs.

%choose power exponent for drift beta2 between 0 and 1.

mu2=.25;    % Second drift coefficient.
sigma0=.850;%Volatility value

%We calculate arithmetic summation(path integral) of theta1*yy(t)^omega1;
omega1=.650; %power on yy(t) for fy and fyPI. 
%I have tested powers between .65 and 1;
theta1=dt;%This is the weight on arithmetic sum in the path integral.
%This is also the multiplier for the function fy. Since calculation of
%fyPI borrows from calculation of fy, this has to be the same for both fy
%and fyPI.

        %Do not change above alpha.
tic

w0T=w0;
for tt=1:Tt
    w0T=w0T+CalculateDrift(mu1,mu2,beta1,beta2,gamma,sigma0,w0T,1,1,dt);
end

%coordinates at the terminal time.

%grid which is kept at 3.5 standard normal deviations below the projected 
%median. The argument is that bessel standard deviations are roughly close
%to normal standard deviations.Obviously this does not apply to
%mean-reverting type processes and appropriate integrated volatility could
%be used.
wS2=(w0-sigma0*sqrt(Tt*dt)*4.0);%wS2 second candidate for the starting point of the grid.
if(wS1<wS2)
    wS0=wS1;
else
    wS0=wS2;
end
%Above wS0 is the true lower starting point of the grid chosen from the
%lesser of wS1 or wS2.
if(wS0<0)
    wS0=.001;
end

wE2=(w0+sigma0*sqrt(Tt*dt)*4.5);
if(wE1>wE2)
    wE0=wE1;
else
    wE0=wE2;
end

wS(1:Nn)=w(1:Nn)-.5*dNn;%Grid starts;
wE(1:Nn)=w(1:Nn)+.5*dNn;%Grid Ends.

%transition probabilities calculation for the entire grid but only from
%below 5.5 SDs to positive 5.5 SDs.
%w0
Nn0=round(1+(w0-wS0)/dNn);%This is the cell associated with delta origin 
%that will have occupation probability one at the start.

b2(1:Nn)=w(1:Nn);

%For Ito Process of f=theta1*yy(t)^omega1;
[fMu0dt,dfz0,df2z0]=CalculateFunctionDriftAndVol(theta1,omega1,mu1,mu2,beta1,beta2,gamma,sigma0,w,wnStart,Nn,dt);
df2z0(1)=0;
dfz0(1)=dfz0(2);

       else              
           c1N(mm)=1;
       end

       %point mm on the first grid.This is 5.5 SDs less than w(mm)

           c2N(mm)=mm+cN;
       else
           c2N(mm)=Nn-1;
       end

Friends, here is the new report of  progress of the research work on densities of arithmetic path integrals of a general possibly mean reverting SDE. Please first let me describe something about the dynamics of the fixed grid. We update the expectations of sum of functions along the evolution of the SDE process. But there is a crucial difference with monte carlo. All the functions calculated midway along the evolution of the SDE process are realized at a different probability distribution but calculated finally with the terminal probability distribution of the SDE. Being more concrete, let us consider a four step SDE process with times t0, t1, t2, t3, t4. at these times functions of the SDE are evaluated with probability distribution p(t1), p(t2), p(t3) and p(t4) but the arithmetic sum is assigned a single probability p(t4) which is the terminal probability of the SDE. Please note that variance of p(t1), p(t2), p(t3) and p(t4) all are generally different and usually increasing. This way a function evaluated at t1, when transported to time t4, its variance roughly decreases by var(t1)/var(t4). Similarly a function evaluated at t2, sees its variance decrease by var(t2)/var(t4) when it reaches the terminal time. And a function evaluated at time t3, sees its variance decrease by var(t3)/var(t4) roughly with very slight additional adjustments. a function evaluated at t4, has its variance unchanged since it was realized at p(t4) and also calculated at p(t4). All the covariances also decrease by the respective variance ratios as I mentioned. In the previous matlab program for the linear SDEs, I made up for the decreasing variances by adding additional noise. But for a general SDE, the adjustment is slightly difficult. To overcome this difficulty, I designed a "quadratic variations generator" which evolves the squared noise process. This is a very little variance process and its mean at any time is equal to variance of the SDE. So we can easily make the variance adjustment (by scaling the noise or adding the noise) by simply calculating the present variance of the SDE and one step ahead variance of the SDE. So that the process keeps the true variance at every step of the SDE due to variance adjustments.

Considering a simpler version of  the above setup, I will try to explain a bit further in more detail. First of all, we are evolving the expected values of arithmetic sum of function values along the evolution of SDE. Expected value of function evaluated at time t1 is approximately given, for each cell, as E[f(X(t1))]=f(X(t1))*P(X(t1)). where f(X(t1)) is the value of function at a particular grid cell and P(X(t1)) is the integrated probability mass in that particular grid cell. Problem is that we have to find the conditional value of evolved function at the terminal time which is calculated by a division by the terminal probability mass. Imagine if there were only two time steps and if there were a single payoff at time t1. When we calculate its conditional value at time t1, it would have to be calculated with probability mass at time t1. Now we divide forward transported version of E[f(X(t1))]=f(X(t1))*P(X(t1)) by P(X(t2)). The true conditional value relevant for the arithmetic sum should be  f(X(t1)) with a variance that is associated with P(X(t1)). Now doing the division by P(X(t2)) at terminal time scales the variance by Var(X(t1))/Var(X(t2)). In doing one step forward transport, the f(X(t1)) value also gained some additional variance of sigma^2*dt. So a noise scaling of   (Var(X(t2))/Var(X(t1))-sigma^2*dt) should be able to alter the variance so that we could retrieve the right variance of f(X(t1)) when divided by the forward terminal density P(x(t2)) despite that the expectation at time step t1 that evolved forward to time step 2 was calculated with density P(X(t1)).
This should not be a problem for non-linear densities where variance evolution could be different in different parts of the density. We have to consider that probability particles or paths that carry the expectation of functions forward on average move along CDF subdivisions or standard deviation lines. A particle located at .25 CDF probability mass at time t1 would on average move to .25 CDF on the next time step t2 or, in other words, move along Z-standard deviation lines. What we can easily do is to calculate dX(t1)/dZ and also calculate dX(t2)/dZ (this is the same calculation that we do for change of probability with standard normal) and then multiply E[f(X(t1))] by [dX(t2)/dZ]/[dX(t1)/dZ] for corresponding values of Z-standard deviation values(we would have to associate appropriate Z-standard deviation values with respect to density of SDE at time t1 and time t2 for each grid cell) . A total forward transported value that would keep the forward transported expectation on forward probability P(t2) to retrieve the right value of f(X(t1)) would require a variance scaling of ([[dX(t2)/dZ]/[dX(t1)/dZ]-sigma^2*dt). This should work very well even for path integrals of SDEs where variance is changing locally in a different ratio. Multiplying by [[dX(t2)/dZ]/[dX(t1)/dZ] is like multiplying by ratio of  local variances related to same standard deviations(or same CDF levels) at time step one and time step 2. Please note that appropriate [dX(t2)/dZ] and [dX(t1)/dZ]  in the ratio are not related by same grid cells, but they are related by the same level of standard normal Z.
