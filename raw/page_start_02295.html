Friends, I have been working with daily stock data to trade on a daily basis. I wrote a program with enough success and want to share its basics with friends. 
First of all, we must notice that cross-sectional correlations might be quite stable but correlations across time (in days) to predict the future are quite cyclical (and continue to change from trending to mean-reverting and back and forth) with quite small and obviously variable periods. When we take a small rolling window of two weeks to find auto-correlations and other parameters, and try to predict the future direction of returns, mostly the period of validity of correlations is already about complete and correlations are already transitioning from one phase to other so we usually end up with poor forecasts.
So to some new ideas. First, I deal solely with log returns in prediction part of my program and raw stock prices are used only to calculate profit/loss on trades. 
Intuitively, In a small data set of 14 working days or so, if the market is trending, it is likely that average return will be reasonably large. On the other hand, if the market is mean-reverting, average return would be smaller.  we compare absolute returns without signs. I thought of a measure of mean-reversion should be calculated as ratio between absolute average return and standard deviation. This should work well for small samples only. We call this mean-reversion statistic.
Again we do not want to use correlations from past 14 days or so since market might already have switched in 14 working days. We want 2-4 days of recent returns to tell us what state of market is by comparing these returns with previous market average. Therefore, a better technique could be to divide the 14 day data in two groups. A front group of roughly three elements and a back group of roughly eleven data points. We compare mean reversion statistic of these two small groups on front and back. If abs(mu)/sigma is smaller in front group, we learn that market is mean reverting phase. And same logic for trending market i.e. if  abs(mu)/sigma is larger in front group, we learn that market is in trending phase. We take sign of mu (mean of returns) in front group and if market is trending, we trade in direction of this mean, mu and if market is mean-reverting, we trade against the sign of this mu in front group.
