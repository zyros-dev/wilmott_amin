Friends, I want to describe the simple numerical procedure that can help match the coefficients of orthogonal polynomials with respect to special densities so that moments of the series in orthogonal polynomials match with the moments of the data. I am sure this can be used with any orthogonal polynomials but I am using the notation for hermite polynomials in my exposition.
Suppose we want to expand a random variable in terms of hermite polynomials, its series expansion could be given as (I am expanding to five polynomials. You can expand to arbitrary number of polynomials)

[$] Y \, = \, \mu \, + \, ch_1 \, H_1(Z) \, + \, ch_2 \, H_2(Z) \, + \, ch_3 \, H_3(Z) \, + \, ch_4 \, H_4(Z) \, + \, ch_5 \, H_5(Z) \, [$]

here [$]ch_n[$] represents the coefficient of nth hermite polynomial and [$]H_n(Z)[$] represents the nth hermite polynomial itself. 
Our problem would be solved when we would have successfully chosen coefficients [$]ch_n[$] so that powers of expression on rhs would equate to raw moments of random variable Y (as calculated from data.)

Friends who are familiar with my earlier work would recall that before matching the moments, I standardized them and it was standardized moments that were matched with Z/hermite series. We could convert the standardized moments calibrated Z/hermite series to actual calibration by multiplying it with square root of variance and then could convert from central moments Z/hermite series to raw moments Z/hermite series by adding the mean to zeroth  coefficients in the series.

With standardized  central moments, our input second moment is unity. which implies that in hermite series, squared coefficients have to lie on a multidimensional ellipsoid. I try to show this as 

[$]E[(Y-mu)^2]  = E[ ch_1 \, H_1(Z) \, + \, ch_2 \, H_2(Z) \, + \, ch_3 \, H_3(Z) \, + \, ch_4 \, H_4(Z) \, + \, ch_5 \, H_5(Z) \,]^2 =\, 1\,[$]
[$] {ch_1}^2 \, + \, 2\, {ch_2}^2 \, + \,6 \, {ch_3}^2  \, + \, 24\, {ch_4}^2 \, +\, 120 \, {ch_5}^2 =\, 1\,[$]

The above equation shows that hermite coefficients to be optimized are under constraint to remain on surface of an ellipsoid given by above equation(of second standardized central moment/variance).

We try to leverage the above constraint to find a optimization-search method that perturbs the coefficients in a way that the ellipsoid constraint is satisfied and we continue to move towards higher value of criteria of fit objective function. We do not need to take derivatives for this optimization. All we have to do is to implement the ellipsoid constraint in an intelligent way.

We have not yet specified the objective function yet. We do not want to apply sum of  squared deviations between fitted moments and input moments since higher moments can be too big to eclipse and overwhelm the quality of fit in smaller moments that are even more important. I thought of criteria of fit as squared deviations between applied cumulants and fitted cumulants as choice of cumulants in squared deviation will somewhat alleviate the huge distortion caused by large moments in the degree of fit when moments are chosen as variables in squared deviations. We tried cumulants in earlier work with limited success but that problem was different and required calculation of derivatives where cumulants abruptly would change sign not in proportion to coefficients being optimized. Our problem now is different and much simpler and choice of cumulants in least squared deviations  criteria of fit should work well.

Now coming to crux of our strategy,  we know whatever coefficients we choose, the hermite coefficients have to be constarined to lie on an ellipsoid. We start with some trial coefficients which can be found possibly by one or two passes of simple smoothing method I used earlier in my previous work. We perturb one hermite coefficient by a small amount by increasing it and we know that weighted sum of rest of the squared coefficients have to be decreased by the same amount for all coefficients to remain on ellipsoid. We decide to keep the ratio of all other coefficients the same as we decrease them and find new coefficients. Again we have increased one coefficients by a small amount and decreased the cumulative expression of the rest of the coefficients by the same amount while decreasing them in the same ratio they earlier were. We evaluate the objective function and if the objective function decreases, we accept the new coefficients. Otherwise we move the first coefficient in negative direction and increase the expression for rest of the coefficients by the same amount keeping the squared coefficients in proportion they already were in. After accepting iteration for the first variable, we iterate the same procedure by increasing or decreasing all of the rest of the coefficients and continue to perturb until we get extremely close to desired solution, where we can get to perfect solution with one or two iterations of newton method.

Here I describe the above procedure with simple equations.
Our constraint it given as
[$] {ch_1}^2 \, + \, 2\, {ch_2}^2 \, + \,6 \, {ch_3}^2  \, + \, 24\, {ch_4}^2 \, +\, 120 \, {ch_5}^2 =\, 1\,[$]

Suppose, for trial evaluation of objective function, we want to increase [$]ch_1[$]. Our equation is given in terms of squared coefficients. We denote a change in squared coefficients by large delta and denote it s magnitude by C as 
[$] \Delta [{ch_1}^2] \, =\, C[$]
change in the rest of the expression should be an equivalent negative so we write that as
[$] \Delta \big[\, 2\, {ch_2}^2 \, + \,6 \, {ch_3}^2  \, + \, 24\, {ch_4}^2 \, +\, 120 \, {ch_5}^2 \big]=\, -C \,[$]
we introduce a proportionality constant in the above equation and equate it with the required change as

[$] \, 2\, \eta \,  {ch_2}^2 \, + \,6 \, \eta\, {ch_3}^2  \, + \, 24\,\eta \, {ch_4}^2 \, +\, 120 \,\eta \, {ch_5}^2 \, =\, -C \,[$]

from where we can calculate [$]\eta [$] as 

[$] \, \eta  = \,\frac{ -C }{\, 2\,  {ch_2}^2 \, + \,6 \, {ch_3}^2  \, + \, 24\, {ch_4}^2 \, +\, 120  \, {ch_5}^2 \, }\,[$]
from where we can infer that 
[$] \Delta [{ch_2}^2] \, =\, \eta {ch_2}^2[$]
[$] \Delta [{ch_3}^2] \, =\, \eta {ch_3}^2[$]
[$] \Delta [{ch_4}^2] \, =\, \eta {ch_4}^2[$]
[$] \Delta [{ch_5}^2] \, =\, \eta {ch_5}^2[$]

new values of coefficients would be (beware of signs. they can be both positive or negative.)

[$] \,ch_1 = \sqrt{{ch_1}^2 + \Delta [{ch_1}^2] }\, [$]
[$] \,ch_2 = \sqrt{{ch_2}^2 + \Delta [{ch_2}^2] }\, [$]
[$] \,ch_3 = \sqrt{{ch_3}^2 + \Delta [{ch_3}^2] }\, [$]
[$] \,ch_4 = \sqrt{{ch_4}^2 + \Delta [{ch_4}^2] }\, [$]
[$] \,ch_5 = \sqrt{{ch_5}^2 + \Delta [{ch_5}^2] }\, [$]

These new values of coefficients would again lie on the ellipsoid. So we would be able to evaluate the objective function for these new values of coefficients of hermite polynomials and then see if the objective criteria is minimized, if it is minimized, we accept it otherwise move the coefficient in other direction. We do not need to take derivatives of any equations  or coefficients however there could be some variant that uses derivatives and could be faster.

So we could slowly perturb the coefficients one by one and move towards better fit to objective function.

I do not have my computer on me and I have not run any of the analytics so I cannot know if taking large steps in simulation could be possible. 

Same technique could be used for polynomials other than hermite polynomials.

Friends, I still could not get my laptop and continued to just think about other applications of numerical optimization technique I described a few posts ago. 
One possible application is in calculations of Mean variance analysis and modern portfolio theory.
But when I look at wikipedia how variance is calculated in Mean variance analysis or Merkowitz criteria, I realized that calculating  variance this way is very suboptimal and is perfectly valid only when all assets involved are perfectly normal and have only two cumulants and their higher cumulants vanish. 
When higher cumulants exist, we have to divide the variance of each asset between hermite polynomials of various degree in a way that moments/cumulants of hermite polynomial series expression match the moments/cumulants of the asset.
The currently accepted expression of variance only includes total variances and covariances but when higher cumulants exist, it has to be modified and replaced by sum of variances and covariances associated with each hermite orthogonal polynomial. 
So total variance of each asset is decomposed into variances/coefficients of orthognal hermite polynomials. And each orthogonal polynomial of certain degree has correlations and hence covariances with hermite polynomials of the same degree associated with other assets. 
So instead of writing a single expression of sum of variances and covariances, when higher cumulants exist, we have to repeat this expression for sum of variances and covariances associated with each degree of hermite polynomials. So a total expression of variance should sum over variances and covariances of first hermite polynomial and then variances and covariances of second hermite polynomial and so on until we add the variances and covariances associated with the maximum degree of hermite polynomial we are using in our analysis.

Friends, in classic statistics literature, it is possible to find covariances if the random variables involved belong to one of the well-known densities. When density of a random variable deviates from the well-known major densities, the covariance calculations between such random variables become more and more approximate as the deviation of their densities from the base density increases.
This is where my research comes in and my research helps to faithfully find covariances between very large classes of densities where it was earlier difficult to make good covariance calculations.
This is very helpful since most of the statistical prediction, regression theory, pattern-recognition and filtering theory are based on reliable calculations of covariances between the random variables involved.
What I did in my research was that I represented the distribution of data in terms of a series in orthogonal polynomials with respect to a particular base density so that moments of the data match with moments of the orthogonal polynomial series representation of the data. 
The base density could be standard normal density with hermite polynomials, uniform density with legendre polynomials, or Laguerre polynomials with respect to gamma density. You could also possibly expand in Jacobi, Chebyshev or other orthogonal polynomials with respect to their respective densities. Though details are going to vary, the basic idea to represent the random variables in orthogonal polynomials of related density is exactly the same.
When large data sets are given so that CDF of the density of random variables associated with the data can be well-calculated, I have proposed another method that extracts coefficients of orthogonal polynomials using inner product of the data with orthogonal polynomials of various order.
Once a series representation of the data random variables have been found in terms of common orthogonal polynomials with respect to a base density, we have to find correlations and covariances between two random variables differently for orthogonal polynomial of every order. 
Pure Gaussian densities can be described by just first orthogonal polynomial and classic formulas for correlations and covariances simply assume that we are describing the density of random variables in data in terms of first orthogonal polynomial only. This representation might not always be appropriate especially for densities that have non-negligible higher cumulants. More the density of data deviates from normal density or other base density, the more inappropriate the formulas of correlations and covariances that are based on first orthogonal polynomial representation of the density. of random variables in data.
Similarly, the portfolio variance calculations in Merkoitz criteria are valid  if assets are perfectly normal. The more the deviations from Gaussianity, the more inappropriate the total variance of the portfolio  calculated in classic way since it is based on covariances that assume just the first orthogonal hermite polynomial. Also it neglects the fact that each hermite polynomial of certain order takes its own correlations across different assets.

We do not need to take derivatives for this optimization. All we have to do is to implement the ellipsoid constraint in an intelligent way.

We have not yet specified the objective function yet. We do not want to apply sum of  squared deviations between fitted moments and input moments since higher moments can be too big to eclipse and overwhelm the quality of fit in smaller moments that are even more important. I thought of criteria of fit as squared deviations between applied cumulants and fitted cumulants as choice of cumulants in squared deviation will somewhat alleviate the huge distortion caused by large moments in the degree of fit when moments are chosen as variables in squared deviations. We tried cumulants in earlier work with limited success but that problem was different and required calculation of derivatives where cumulants abruptly would change sign not in proportion to coefficients being optimized. Our problem now is different and much simpler and choice of cumulants in least squared deviations  criteria of fit should work well.

Now coming to crux of our strategy,  we know whatever coefficients we choose, the hermite coefficients have to be constarined to lie on an ellipsoid. We start with some trial coefficients which can be found possibly by one or two passes of simple smoothing method I used earlier in my previous work. We perturb one hermite coefficient by a small amount by increasing it and we know that weighted sum of rest of the squared coefficients have to be decreased by the same amount for all coefficients to remain on ellipsoid. We decide to keep the ratio of all other coefficients the same as we decrease them and find new coefficients. Again we have increased one coefficients by a small amount and decreased the cumulative expression of the rest of the coefficients by the same amount while decreasing them in the same ratio they earlier were. We evaluate the objective function and if the objective function decreases, we accept the new coefficients. Otherwise we move the first coefficient in negative direction and increase the expression for rest of the coefficients by the same amount keeping the squared coefficients in proportion they already were in. After accepting iteration for the first variable, we iterate the same procedure by increasing or decreasing all of the rest of the coefficients and continue to perturb until we get extremely close to desired solution, where we can get to perfect solution with one or two iterations of newton method.

Here I describe the above procedure with simple equations.
Our constraint it given as
[$] {ch_1}^2 \, + \, 2\, {ch_2}^2 \, + \,6 \, {ch_3}^2  \, + \, 24\, {ch_4}^2 \, +\, 120 \, {ch_5}^2 =\, 1\,[$]

Suppose, for trial evaluation of objective function, we want to increase [$]ch_1[$]. Our equation is given in terms of squared coefficients. We denote a change in squared coefficients by large delta and denote it s magnitude by C as 
[$] \Delta [{ch_1}^2] \, =\, C[$]
change in the rest of the expression should be an equivalent negative so we write that as
[$] \Delta \big[\, 2\, {ch_2}^2 \, + \,6 \, {ch_3}^2  \, + \, 24\, {ch_4}^2 \, +\, 120 \, {ch_5}^2 \big]=\, -C \,[$]
we introduce a proportionality constant in the above equation and equate it with the required change as

[$] \, 2\, \eta \,  {ch_2}^2 \, + \,6 \, \eta\, {ch_3}^2  \, + \, 24\,\eta \, {ch_4}^2 \, +\, 120 \,\eta \, {ch_5}^2 \, =\, -C \,[$]

from where we can calculate [$]\eta [$] as 

[$] \, \eta  = \,\frac{ -C }{\, 2\,  {ch_2}^2 \, + \,6 \, {ch_3}^2  \, + \, 24\, {ch_4}^2 \, +\, 120  \, {ch_5}^2 \, }\,[$]
from where we can infer that 
[$] \Delta [{ch_2}^2] \, =\, \eta {ch_2}^2[$]
[$] \Delta [{ch_3}^2] \, =\, \eta {ch_3}^2[$]
[$] \Delta [{ch_4}^2] \, =\, \eta {ch_4}^2[$]
[$] \Delta [{ch_5}^2] \, =\, \eta {ch_5}^2[$]

new values of coefficients would be (beware of signs. they can be both positive or negative.)

[$] \,ch_1 = \sqrt{{ch_1}^2 + \Delta [{ch_1}^2] }\, [$]
[$] \,ch_2 = \sqrt{{ch_2}^2 + \Delta [{ch_2}^2] }\, [$]
[$] \,ch_3 = \sqrt{{ch_3}^2 + \Delta [{ch_3}^2] }\, [$]
[$] \,ch_4 = \sqrt{{ch_4}^2 + \Delta [{ch_4}^2] }\, [$]
[$] \,ch_5 = \sqrt{{ch_5}^2 + \Delta [{ch_5}^2] }\, [$]

These new values of coefficients would again lie on the ellipsoid. So we would be able to evaluate the objective function for these new values of coefficients of hermite polynomials and then see if the objective criteria is minimized, if it is minimized, we accept it otherwise move the coefficient in other direction. We do not need to take derivatives of any equations  or coefficients however there could be some variant that uses derivatives and could be faster.

So we could slowly perturb the coefficients one by one and move towards better fit to objective function.

I do not have my computer on me and I have not run any of the analytics so I cannot know if taking large steps in simulation could be possible. 

Same technique could be used for polynomials other than hermite polynomials.

Friends, in classic statistics literature, it is possible to find covariances if the random variables involved belong to one of the well-known densities. When density of a random variable deviates from the well-known major densities, the covariance calculations between such random variables become more and more approximate as the deviation of their densities from the base density increases.
This is where my research comes in and my research helps to faithfully find covariances between very large classes of densities where it was earlier difficult to make good covariance calculations.
This is very helpful since most of the statistical prediction, regression theory, pattern-recognition and filtering theory are based on reliable calculations of covariances between the random variables involved.
What I did in my research was that I represented the distribution of data in terms of a series in orthogonal polynomials with respect to a particular base density so that moments of the data match with moments of the orthogonal polynomial series representation of the data. 
The base density could be standard normal density with hermite polynomials, uniform density with legendre polynomials, or Laguerre polynomials with respect to gamma density. You could also possibly expand in Jacobi, Chebyshev or other orthogonal polynomials with respect to their respective densities. Though details are going to vary, the basic idea to represent the random variables in orthogonal polynomials of related density is exactly the same.
When large data sets are given so that CDF of the density of random variables associated with the data can be well-calculated, I have proposed another method that extracts coefficients of orthogonal polynomials using inner product of the data with orthogonal polynomials of various order.
Once a series representation of the data random variables have been found in terms of common orthogonal polynomials with respect to a base density, we have to find correlations and covariances between two random variables differently for orthogonal polynomial of every order. 
Pure Gaussian densities can be described by just first orthogonal polynomial and classic formulas for correlations and covariances simply assume that we are describing the density of random variables in data in terms of first orthogonal polynomial only. This representation might not always be appropriate especially for densities that have non-negligible higher cumulants. More the density of data deviates from normal density or other base density, the more inappropriate the formulas of correlations and covariances that are based on first orthogonal polynomial representation of the density. of random variables in data.
Similarly, the portfolio variance calculations in Merkoitz criteria are valid  if assets are perfectly normal. The more the deviations from Gaussianity, the more inappropriate the total variance of the portfolio  calculated in classic way since it is based on covariances that assume just the first orthogonal hermite polynomial. Also it neglects the fact that each hermite polynomial of certain order takes its own correlations across different assets.
.
.
Many types of calculations of variances that were considered very difficult, can be done with this new method. You can find formulas for calculations of distribution of sums of lognormals or sum of mixed densities like some CEV and some lognormal etc. You can also find sum of heavy-tailed lognormal based or mixed densities. 
All you have to do is to find the hermite representation of each density and then find correlations/covariances between hermite polynomials of various order and then add over variances and covariances of all hermite polynomials in squared fashion while taking account of portfolio weights.

Friends, sorry, I was too tired last night to get myself to write latex equations for variance of sum of  (gaussian-like but non-gaussian) random variables despite my efforts but here they are. Even lognormal density is gaussian-like since it can be represented by a series expansion in hermite polynomials of standard Gaussian density. 

In this post I want to make calculations of a sum of two random variables with different weights when their hermite representation has been calculated by matching matching data moments with moments of hermite representation.

Suppose our first asset/random variable is [$]Y_1[$] with weight [$]w_1[$] and hermite representation (upto fourth hermite polynomial) of the first asset/random variable is given as

[$]\, Y_1\, = \, a_0 \, + \, a_1 \, Z_1 \, + \, a_2 \, \big[ {Z_1}^2 -1 \big]\, + \, a_3 \, \big[{Z_1}^3 -3 \, Z_1\big]\, + \, a_4 \, \big[{Z_1}^4 \, -3 \, {Z_1}^2 \, +3 \big]\,[$]

our second asset/random variable is [$]Y_2[$] with weight [$]w_2[$] and hermite representation (upto fourth hermite polynomial) of the second asset/random variable is given as

[$]\, Y_2 \, = \, b_0 \, + \, b_1 \, Z_2 \, + \, b_2 \, \big[ {Z_2}^2 -1 \big]\, + \, b_3 \, \big[{Z_2}^3 -3 \, Z_2\big]\, + \, b_4 \, \big[{Z_2}^4 \, -3 \, {Z_2}^2 \, +3 \big]\,[$]

Now 

[$]Var[Y_1] \, = \, {a_1}^2 \, + \, 2 \, {a_2}^2 + \, 6 \, {a_3}^2+ \, 24 \, {a_4}^2 [$]

[$]Var[Y_2] \, = \, {b_1}^2 \, + \, 2 \, {b_2}^2 + \, 6 \, {b_3}^2+ \, 24 \, {b_4}^2 [$]

We also have correlations between orthogonal polynomials of the same order as given below. 

[$] \rho_1 \, =\frac{ E\big[Z_1 \, Z_2 \big]\,}{\sqrt{E\big[{Z_1}^2 \big] \, E\big[{Z_2}^2 \big] }} [$]

[$] \rho_2 \, =\frac{ E\big[({Z_1}^2-1) \, ({Z_2}^2-1) \big] \,}{\sqrt{E\big[({Z_1}^2-1)^2 \big] \, E\big[({Z_2}^2-1)^2 \big] }} [$]

[$] \rho_3 \, =\frac{ E\big[({Z_1}^3- 3 \, Z_1) \, ({Z_2}^3-3 \, Z_2) \big] \,}{\sqrt{E\big[({Z_1}^3-3 \, Z_1)^2 \big] \, E\big[({Z_2}^3-3 \, Z_2)^2 \big] }} [$]

[$] \rho_4 \, =\frac{ E\big[({Z_1}^4- 6 \, {Z_1}^2 \, + 3) \, ({Z_2}^4-6\, {Z_2}^2 \,+3 ) \big] \,}{\sqrt{E\big[({Z_1}^4-6 \, {Z_1}^2 +3 )^2 \big] \, E\big[({Z_2}^4-6 \, {Z_2}^2 \, +3)^2 \big] }} [$]

please look at his post to see how to actually make these calculations on data: https://forum.wilmott.com/viewtopic.php?f=4&t=99702&start=1665#p874028

Friends, in my previous post, I was thinking about variance of the sum of two random variables. But many friends would find it interesting to find a hermite polynomial series expression for the density of the sum of to random variables that can be used for further analytics. Here I try to write the equations for that.

Suppose our first asset/random variable is [$]Y_1[$] with weight [$]w_1[$] and hermite representation (upto fourth hermite polynomial) of the first asset/random variable is given as

[$]\, Y_1\, = \, a_0 \, + \, a_1 \, Z_1 \, + \, a_2 \, \big[ {Z_1}^2 -1 \big]\, + \, a_3 \, \big[{Z_1}^3 -3 \, Z_1\big]\, + \, a_4 \, \big[{Z_1}^4 \, -3 \, {Z_1}^2 \, +3 \big]\,[$]

our second asset/random variable is [$]Y_2[$] with weight [$]w_2[$] and hermite representation (upto fourth hermite polynomial) of the second asset/random variable is given as

[$]\, Y_2 \, = \, b_0 \, + \, b_1 \, Z_2 \, + \, b_2 \, \big[ {Z_2}^2 -1 \big]\, + \, b_3 \, \big[{Z_2}^3 -3 \, Z_2\big]\, + \, b_4 \, \big[{Z_2}^4 \, -3 \, {Z_2}^2 \, +3 \big]\,[$]

and we want to find hermite series expression for the sum random variable where

[$] \, Y_3 \, = w_1 \, Y_1 \, + \, w_2 \, Y_2 [$]

Let us first describe the hermite form of this summed random variable [$] Y_3[$] and then we would relate its hermite coefficients to hermite coefficients of [$]Y_1[$] and [$]Y_2[$]. This hermite form of [$]Y_3[$] with yet unknown coefficients is given as

[$]\, Y_3 \, = \, c_0 \, + \, c_1 \, Z_3 \, + \, c_2 \, \big[ {Z_3}^2 -1 \big]\, + \, c_3 \, \big[{Z_3}^3 -3 \, Z_3\big]\, + \, c_4 \, \big[{Z_3}^4 \, -3 \, {Z_3}^2 \, +3 \big]\,[$]

Now we write the coefficients of [$]Y_3[$] in terms of coefficients of [$]Y_1[$] and [$]Y_2[$]
Zeroth coefficient is mean in this expansion and is added linearly. Coefficients of higher order hermite polynomials are added in sum squared fashion but accounting for sign.

[$]c_0 \, =\,  w_1\, a_0 \, + \,w_2 \, b_0 \,[$]

Expressions below are very simple but since higher hermite coefficients can have both negative and positive signs, you have to be careful in squared sum that when a large negative coefficient is squared and added to a small positive coefficient squared, the result is a smaller negative squared. We take its absolute value, take its squared root and assign it a negative sign. 

Please note that coefficient of first hermite polynomial coefficients would almost always be positive. I have never encountered a situation in which coefficient of first hermite polynomial was negative otherwise the derivative of random variable with respect to standard normal will possibly take a negative value which is not allowed for densities. All hermite polynomials after first hermite polynomial can have negative signs for their coefficients.
This is really a simple squared sum but accounting for sign makes the resulting expression a bit complicated.

[$]c_1 \, = \sqrt{ \,{w_1}^2 \, {a_1}^2 \, + \, {w_2}^2 \,{b_1}^2 + \, 2 \, \rho_1 \, w_1 \, w_2 \,a_1 \, b_1} \,[$]

[$]c_2 \, = sgn({sgn(a_2) \,{w_1}^2 \, {a_2}^2 \, + \, sgn(b_2) {w_2}^2 \,{b_2}^2 + \, 2 \, \rho_2 \, w_1 \, w_2 \,a_2 \, b_2}) \\ *\, \sqrt{ |{sgn(a_2) \,{w_1}^2 \, {a_2}^2 \, + \, sgn(b_2) {w_2}^2 \,{b_2}^2 + \, 2 \, \rho_2 \, w_1 \, w_2 \,a_2 \, b_2}|} \,[$]

[$]c_3 \, = sgn({sgn(a_3) \,{w_1}^2 \, {a_3}^2 \, + \, sgn(b_3) {w_2}^2 \,{b_3}^2 + \, 2 \, \rho_3 \, w_1 \, w_2 \,a_3 \, b_3}) \\ *\, \sqrt{ |{sgn(a_3) \,{w_1}^2 \, {a_3}^2 \, + \, sgn(b_3) {w_2}^2 \,{b_3}^2 + \, 2 \, \rho_3 \, w_1 \, w_2 \,a_3 \, b_3}|} \,[$]

[$]c_4 \, = sgn({sgn(a_4) \,{w_1}^2 \, {a_4}^2 \, + \, sgn(b_4) {w_2}^2 \,{b_4}^2 + \, 2 \, \rho_4 \, w_1 \, w_2 \,a_4 \, b_4}) \\ *\, \sqrt{ |{sgn(a_4) \,{w_1}^2 \, {a_4}^2 \, + \, sgn(b_4) {w_2}^2 \,{b_4}^2 + \, 2 \, \rho_4 \, w_1 \, w_2 \,a_4 \, b_4}|} \,[$]
Since a formal expression taking into account signs is unnecessarily complicated, I am writing simpler expression without correlations for friends to gain intuition.
If there are zero correlations between hermite polynomials, we get simpler expressions that are given in case of zero correlations as

[$]c_1 \, =\sqrt{ \,{w_1}^2 \, {a_1}^2 \, +  {w_2}^2 \,{b_1}^2 }\,[$]
