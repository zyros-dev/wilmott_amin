Here are some quick notes about the previous program.
The diffusion I am analytically simulating is dX=epsilon X^gamma dz(t).
The results are very encouraging. However the accuracy sharply deteriorates when significant mass of the diffusion gets absorbed in zero. If diffusion reaches zero but significant mass does not get absorbed in zero, the results are still very good. The reason is that we have divided the total density into standard deviation fractions. The most negative standard deviations expand the fastest in negative direction and the most positive standard deviation expand fastest in the positive direction at a rate proportional to standard deviation count and so on for intermediate values. When significant mass is absorbed at zero, a large fraction  of the negative standard deviations is absorbed in zero but remaining more positive standard deviation fractions continue to advance. By contrast, when we simulate by monte carlo, the mass that is away from zero contains all standard deviations  and hence a method that mimicks Gaussian must contain all standard deviations. Our analytic method works by advancing all standard deviations independently. When significant standard deviation fractions are absorbed at zero, we must re-assign standard deviation fractions to all the probability mass that has not reached zero so that it contains all standard deviation fractions. This is however not very simple or straightforward to do and would require an algorithmic effort to do it. Another way to approach the problem would be to reflect the negative standard deviation fractions that reach zero back into positive domain with negative amplitude that cancels the increased amplitude due to lack of negative standard deviation. This will also require some effort and I have not worked on it. Close vicinity of zero is not a problem for the algorithm. The only problem is that negative standard deviation fractions that get absorbed in zero are lacked in the positive body/domain of the noise diffusion where all standard deviation  fractions must be present for a perfectly Gaussian algorithm. Some way around it can be worked out with a bit of effort.
After a few years (>1-4)the accuracy starts to decrease. Here I will like to mention that my estimate of mean is only based on a rough summation so you will have to write your own integration routine on subdivisions each with different width if you want perfect precision. 
Here I would like to remind readers of my density correction technique that changes the density so as to make its mean equal to a given number while keeping the mass equal to unity. I presented it in an old program in which I calculated transition densities with Girsanov. I will find that out again and explain it here in a day or two. That routine can be very handy for removing small biases in the density in a way that density moves in the desired direction and its mass remains unity and average over the density becomes equal to the desired average. With the help of that small density correction routine, you can freely use the above method even where large accuracy is required for the CEV noises where significant mass is not absorbed at zero for short to medium time horizons. I will explain that routine here probably in my next post.

I have been thinking if we can solve a general Pde by using techniques similar to stochastic calculus of standard deviations. Yesterday, I had some ideas so I thought I would share them with friends here. I am extremely confident that something can be developed based on these ideas. I have enough clarity here. 

The idea here is to divide the PDE domain into small subdivisions. We set the change in PDE variable equal to zero as we march into time. We take Laplace transform locally for each subdivision. From this local Laplace transform, we can find the moments of movement in the PDE variable that can be used with some appropriate orthogonal polynomial to move and/or expand the small subdivision so that some quantities related to PDE variable remains constant in time.


We try to find the local solution of a simple PDE of the form
[$]\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=\mu (x)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}+.5 \sigma ^2(x)\frac{\partial ^2f}{\partial x^2}[$]

At the start I would like to recall the integration technique I mentioned earlier in this thread. 
[$]\int_a^b f(x) \, dx=f\left(x_0\right)\left(\int _a^{x_0}dx+\int _{x_0}^bdx\right)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\left(x_0\right)\left(\int _a^{x_0}\int_a^{x_0} dx \, dx+\int _{x_0}^b\int_{x_0}^b dx \, dx\right)[$]
[$]+\frac{\partial ^2f}{\partial x^2}\left(x_0\right)\left(\int _a^{x_0}\int _a^{x_0}\int _a^{x_0}dxdxdx+\int _{x_0}^b\int _{x_0}^b\int _{x_0}^bdxdxdx\right)[$]

where [$]x_0=\frac{a+b}{2}[$] is the middle of the subdivision

We would now show how to apply it to taking the Laplace transform of the second order term in the PDE
[$]\int _a^be^{-s x}\sigma ^2(x)\frac{\partial ^2f}{\partial x^2}dx=\left(e^{-s x}\sigma ^2(x)\frac{\partial ^2f}{\partial x^2}\right){}_{x=x_0} \left( \int_a^{x_0} dx +\int_{x_0}^b dx \right)[$]
[$]+\frac{\partial }{\partial x}\left(e^{-s x}\sigma ^2(x)\frac{\partial ^2f}{\partial x^2}\right) {}_{x=x_0\text{  }}\left(\int _a^{x_0}\int_a^{x_0} dx \, dx+\int _{x_0}^b\int_{x_0}^b dx \, dx\right)[$]
[$]+\frac{\partial ^2}{\partial x^2}\left(e^{-s x}\sigma ^2(x)\frac{\partial ^2f}{\partial x^2}\right) {}_{x=x_0\text{  }}\left(\int _a^{x_0}\int _a^{x_0}\int _a^{x_0}dxdxdx+\int _{x_0}^b\int _{x_0}^b\int _{x_0}^bdxdxdx\right)[$]   Equation(A)

coming back to the PDE

[$]\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=\mu (x)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}+.5 \sigma ^2(x)\frac{\partial ^2f}{\partial x^2}[$]

We equate the time derivative to zero so as to find the curve where PDE variable does not change as time passes.
[$]0=\mu (x)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}+.5 \sigma ^2(x)\frac{\partial ^2f}{\partial x^2}[$]
Taking the Laplace transform of the above PDE
[$]F(s)=\int _a^b\left(\mu (x)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}+.5 \sigma ^2(x)\frac{\partial ^2f}{\partial x^2}\right)e^{-s x}dx[$]

If the initial data is given in analytic form or we can find its appropriate numerical derivatives, the terms above can be calculated by the integration technique shown at the start in Equation(A) and we will get an analytic expression in Laplace transform variable 's'. This expression can be repeatedly differentiated and setting s=0, we can find the values of various moments.
F(0) = mass
[$]\frac{\partial F}{\partial s}(0)=[$] 1st moment.
[$]\frac{\partial ^2F}{\partial s^2}(0)=[$] 2nd moment

These local moments can be turned into cumulants and used with some orthogonal polynomials to march the PDE into time like I did in stochastic calculus of standard deviations. When PDE has growth/killing terms or forcing terms, we can integrate them along the trajectory of PDE variable and add them to the result.

Those friends who downloaded and played with the program for simulation of CEV noise in post 105 would have noticed that the program has good accuracy. I am now working on basis point accuracy in simulation of CEV noise.
Those researchers who extrapolated the method directly to SDEs with varaible drift would have realized that the method lacks accuracy especially when there is strong mean reversion and other similar extreme variations in drift across the density. The reason is that when drift is variable it affects every point in density differently and hence contributes to local variance everywhere. To get accuracy in simulations of SDEs with variable (possibly mean reversion) drift, I have introduced the concept of variance time that is local to every subdivision(different for each part of the density). For example when mean reversion drift contracts a density, there is a negative contribution to variance different everywhere locally for each subdivision and hence local variance time is smaller than actual time depending upon the degree of local contraction in the density. With this new work, we will have great insights into how variable drift and volatility shape and alter the variance of the distribution of an SDE together and their interactions.
Though I have been away from the work for some time, I have decided to write a formal paper that would be ready in a few weeks. However, I will upload a program on this thread for the simulation of densities of SDEs with variable drift and mean reversion in a week. The logic of variance time adjustment is very simple. I have given a heuristic derivation. Without a drift, we have to principal order the following equation for volatility term.

[$]\sigma (x)\text{dz}(t)=\sigma (x) n \text{$\Delta $X0} \left(\sqrt{t_1}- \sqrt{t_0}\right)[$]

We have to map the above integral (starting with principal order) to change in time. Obviously we see

[$]\sigma (x) n \text{$\Delta $X0} \left(\sqrt{t_1}- \sqrt{t_0}\right) \propto  \left(\sqrt{t_1}- \sqrt{t_0}\right)[$]

When we want to find the time adjustment for any other integral (integral of u(x) dt for example) for a very short time period, we can directly apply the proportionality as

[$]=\frac{1}{n \text{$\Delta $X0}}\frac{\mu (x) \text{dt} }{\sigma (x) }[$]

which is the calculation for principal order. In my program  [$]n \text{$\Delta $X0}[$]  is equal to const/prob2. Rest of the calculations follow.

Here is a rough analysis of why we have extra problems in the simulation of SDEs with strongly state dependent drift. I also give suggestions for the solution to the problem. Consider a general SDE with state dependent drift

[$]\text{dX}=\mu (X) \text{dt} + \sigma (X) \text{dz}(t)[$]

Representing the density in the form of a normal density, we can write the exponential part without normalization as 

[$]\text{EXP}\left[\frac{-.5\left(X-X_0-\int_{t_0}^{t_1} \mu (X) \, dt\right){}^2}{\int_{t_0}^{t_1} \sigma (X)^2 \, dt}\right]=\text{EXP}\left[-.5\frac{\left(X-X_0\right){}^2+\left(\int_{t_0}^{t_1} \mu (X) \, dt\right){}^2-2\left(X-X_0\right)\left(\int_{t_0}^{t_1} \mu (X) \, dt\right)}{\int_{t_0}^{t_1} \sigma (X)^2 \, dt}\right][$]

The density appears as a square of sum of stochastic variables on the exponential in the normal. We know when we square a sum of stochastic variables, We have to account for covariances in addition to individual variances. When there is a drift, the analysis of these covariances is very important since these introduce extra terms in simulation and these extra terms are not directly known from the SDE. To analyze covariances, we look at the volatility of various parts of the last term on exponential 

[$]\left(X-X_0\right)\left(\int_{t_0}^{t_1} \mu (X) \, dt\right)[$]

Looking at vols of each part, the highest order volatility integral of  first part

[$]\text{Vol}\left(X-X_0\right)=\int_{t_0}^{t_1} \sigma (X) \, dz(t)[$]

and volatility integral of the second part is given as

[$]\text{Vol}\left(\int_{t_0}^{t_1} \mu (X) \, dt\right)=\int dt\int _{t_0}^{t_1}\frac{d \mu (X)}{\text{dX}}\sigma (X)dz(t)[$]

We see right away that dz(t) and dz(t) terms in the two Vol terms should correlate to dt. We can see that there is covariance between dz(t) and dz(t) dt terms and their covariance is of the order of dt*dt. We can see that a dt^2 term in the square on normal translates roughly into a dt term in SDE dynamics which is almost never negligible to be neglected.  heuristically, we can say that the covariance is roughly of the order of 

[$]\left.\text{Cov}\left(\left(X-X_0\right),\left(\int_{t_0}^{t_1} \mu (X) \, dt\right)\right)=\int dt\int _{t_0}^{t_1}\frac{d \mu (X)}{\text{dX}}\sigma ^2(X)dt\right)[$]

This term cannot be safely neglected in either monte carlo or other types of simulations of the SDE with state dependent drift. Since we never knew and always neglected this term in our simulation of density of mean reversion SDEs, we were getting rough results. I will be posting a new program on Wilmott with careful analysis of this covaraince term and much better simulation of mean reversion and other SDEs in a few days. I have not done any analysis but I do believe that this term has to be added to monte carlo of nonlinear highly state dependent SDEs to get accurate results. So when we are simulating an SDE with strongly state dependent drift, we have to know that extra terms are introduced in simulation because of the covariance between normal state dependent drift and other terms and these covariance terms are not known from the SDE directly.

The improved strategy goes like this. I am using a normal y with constant variance but variable drift  where z is standard brownian motion as        dy(t)=dz(t)+mu(X(t))/sigma(X(t))dt.
