Limiting the Number of Variables in Math AI Hermite/Legendre Polynomial Regression

We have found, when we are doing hermite or legendre regression with all the products of various orders included, the number of regression variables increase at a rate of    \( \,C_1^n \,+\,C_2^n \,+C_3^n\,+\, \ldots\, C_n^n\,=\,2^n-1 \)  which effectively limits how to include large number of regressors in the orthogonal polynomial regression. 

It turns out that there could be ways to limit the number of variables included in regression if covariance matrix across each hermite polynomial of all regression variables could be described by a small number of eigenvectors with significant eigenvalues using PCA(Principal component analysis) . This can hugely reduce the number of total regressors in the regression but at the cost of possibly very small loss of R-squared. For example if there are M=8 input variables and there are N=10 hermite polynomials associated with each variable, we would have 1 + 255 * 10 total basis functions in the regression. However if (255 * 255) covariance matrix of each of the ten hermite polynomials could be described by 20 significant principle components, the number of basis functions could reduce to 1 + 20 * 10 basis functions in total. Since there is one covariance matrix for each polynomial order in Hermite series, we would have to do one PCA for each covariance matrix. If there are ten hermite polynomials in regression, there would be ten covariance matrices and ten PCAs in total with one PCA for each of these matrices

We first revisit Hermite polynomial Regression.  Let us consider the case of hermite polynomial regression with M variables and N Hermite polynomials in regression. This means that when we include all products of M variables, the total number of basis functions would be \(2^{(M-1)} \, \, N\, \).
Suppose we want to regress a random variable  Y on \(X_1,\,, X_2,\,X_3,\, \ldots, X_M \),  and their products of all order. Let us suppose \(K\, =\,2^{(M-1)}\),  We would have to make hermite/Legendre series of all of the above variables as

\[\, X_k(Z_{X_k})\,=\, ah_0 \, + \, ah_1 \, H_1(Z_{X_k}) \,+ \, ah_2 \, H_2(Z_{X_k}) \,+ \ldots \,+ \, ah_N \, H_N(Z_{X_k}) \,\text{where} \, k\,=\,1,\,2,\, \dots, \,K \] 

\[\, Y(Z_Y)\,=\, bh_0 \, + \, bh_1 \, H_1(Z_Y) \,+ \, bh_2 \, H_2(Z_Y) \,+ \ldots \,+ \, bh_N \, H_N(Z_Y) \, \] 



Let us suppose that we have a total of P joint data observations of above K regressors  and we include N hermite polynomials for each of the K explanatory variable in the regression. And we also have P joint observations of the target regressand and all of the three explanatory variables. In order to perform a regression of Y on hermite polynomials of \(X_k\) , We would have to map data of  \(X_k\) and \(Y\) on Standard normal density to find corresponding \(Z_{X_{k,p}}\) and \(Z_{Y_{p}}\). In Order to decrease the number of regressors in Hermite/Legendre regression and limit the least square regression to only relevant regressors, we follow an analytics based numerical procedure.

Let us suppose we calculate Covariance matrix of \(X_1, \,X_2,\,\ldots\,,X_K\) with Y. This is a (K+1)*(K+1) matrix. If there are N hermite polynomials of every regressor in regression, there will be N such matrices described as  \(\boldsymbol{\rho_{X_k\, Y,n}}\)


\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\rho_{X_k\, Y,n}}\,=\,\begin{bmatrix}  \rho_{X_{1}X_{1}, n} & \rho_{X_{1}X_{2},n} & ....& ..... &  \rho_{X_{1}X_{K},n} &  \rho_{X_{1}Y,n} \\

\rho_{X_{K}X_1, n} & \rho_{X_{k}X_{2},n} & ....& ..... &  \rho_{X_{K}X_{K},n} &  \rho_{X_{K}Y,n} \\
\rho_{Y\,X_1, n} & \rho_{Y\,X_{2},n} & ....& ..... &  \rho_{Y \,X_{K},n} &  \rho_{Y\,Y,n} \\
\end{bmatrix}   
\end{equation}

for the new readers, I will write the equation for correlation between hermite polynomials as


\begin{equation}

\rho_{XY,n} \, = \,  \frac{\sum\limits_{p=1}^{P}\, H_n(Z_{X,p}) \, \, H_n(Z_{Y,p}) \,}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{p=1}^{P} \, {H_n(Z_{X,p})}^2 \,\sum\limits_{p=1}^{P} \, \, {H_n(Z_{Y,p})}^2 \,}}
\end{equation}
