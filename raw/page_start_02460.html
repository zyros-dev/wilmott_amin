function [Wh] = EvalHermiteArrayH0(Z,Order)

function [Hz] = HermitePoly(Order)
Hz(1:Order+1,1:Order+1)=0.0;
Hz(1,1)=1.0;
for k=1:Order
    for m=1:k
       Hz(k+1,m+1)=Hz(k,m);
       if (m>1)
            Hz(k+1,m-1)=Hz(k+1,m-1)-(m-1)*Hz(k,m);
       end
    end
end

\end{bmatrix}   \begin{bmatrix} \beta_0 \\ \beta_{X,1} \\ \ldots \\ \beta_{X,N} \\ \beta_{Y,1}\\\dots \\ \beta_{Y,N} \\ \beta_{XY,1}\\\dots \\ \beta_{XY,N} \end{bmatrix} \\ \,=\, \begin{bmatrix} V_1 \\ V_2 \\ ...  \\  V_p \\ ... \\  V_P \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{5.1.11}
\boldsymbol{H \, \beta}\,=\,\boldsymbol{v}
\end{equation}

and we solve this equation with least squares to find the regression coefficients of equation as

\begin{equation}\label{5.1.12}
\boldsymbol{\beta}\,=\,\boldsymbol{{\left(H^T\, H \right)}^{-1}\,H^T\, v }
\end{equation}

since there are three random variable regressors and Hermite-Series of each random variable includes Hermite polynomials terms up to Nth order and there are a total of P joint data observations, then matrix \(\boldsymbol{H} \) has matrix size as  \( \text{P  X (1 + 3 N)} \) , \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\) has array size of \(\text{(1 +  3 N ) X 1} \) and \(\boldsymbol{v}\) has array size of \(\,\text{P X 1} \).

Friends, there are several kinds of densities of interest that do not have orthogonal polynomials. And as it turns out, it is quite easy to find orthogonal polynomials for a large number of densities (with respect to density weights). You do not necessarily need to solve an appropriate differential equation to find orthogonal polynomials and you could easily do that with a bit of calculus and algebra. I have earlier discussed it previously in post # 2269 here: https://forum.wilmott.com/viewtopic.php?t=99702&start=2265#p880664 

Here I explain the ideas again with latexed equations.

In construction of orhtogonal polynomials with respect to different density weights, we choose coefficients of various orthogonal polynomials recursively by solving equations that ensure orthogonality of each polynomial with respect to all previous polynomials and also solve one condition that ensures that each polynomial is also ortho-normalized. Here I give the details. Suppose the arbitrary density with respect to which we want to find orthogonal polynomials is given as p(X). We suppose that \(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty} \, p(X)dX \,=\,1 \) . I have integrated from zero to infinity but this integration would be over complete support of the density. If this density weight integral is not unity, we will have to change our equations below appropriately.

The zeroth order polynomial is chosen as a constant.
\[P_0(X)= 1\]
The first polynomial can be  
\[P_1(X)=a_1 X + a_0 \] 
where a0 and a1 have to be found from equations
For P0 and P1 to be orthogonal the integral of product of P0(X) and P1(X) when evaluated with respect to specified density should be zero. Limits could be \(-\infty\) to \(+\infty\) or zero to \(+\infty\) or as dictated by support of the density.

\[\int\limits_{0}^{\infty} P_0(X)\,P_1(X) \, p(X) dX \,=\,0 \]
and we can have one non-linear equation for normality. This condition is not necessary and can also be represented by various alternate forms like constraints on leading coefficients etc but this is a useful condition. 
\[\int\limits_{0}^{\infty} P_1(X)\,P_1(X) \, p(X) dX \,=\,1 \]
We can write the above integral equations as linear equations including expectations of powers of X with respect to our weight density

\[\, a_0 \,+ \, a_1 \, E[X]\,=\,0\]
\[\,{a_0^2} \,+\, 2 \,a_0\, a_1 \,E[X]\, + \,{a_1}^2 \,E[X^2] \,= \,1\]

Above polynomial has two unknown coefficients and we have specified two independent conditions to find those coefficients.

Once first polynomial is fixed, the second polynomial is 
\[P_2(X)\,=\,b_0\,+\, b_1\, X \, +\,  b_2\, X^2 \]

But we know that second polynomial has to be orthogonal to zeroth and first polynomial and we want it to be normalized so we get the following two equations for orthogonality and one last equation for normality as

\[\int\limits_{0}^{\infty} P_2(X)\,P_0(X) \, p(X) dX \,=\,0 \]
\[\int\limits_{0}^{\infty} P_2(X)\,P_1(X) \, p(X) dX \,=\,0 \]
\[\int\limits_{0}^{\infty} P_2(X)\,P_2(X) \, p(X) dX \,=\,1 \]

Converting above integral equations to equations with expectations where expectations are with respect to density weight, we get

\[\, b_0\, + \,b_1\, E[X]\, + \,b_2\, E[X^2] =0\]
\[\, a_0\, b_0\, +\, a_0\, b_1 \,E[X]\, +\, a_0\, b_2 \,E[X^2]\, +\, a_1\, b_0\, E[X]\, + \,a_1\, b_1 \,E[X^2] \,+\, a_1\, b_2\, E[X^3]\,=\,0 \]
\[\, {b_0}^2 \,+\, 2\, b_0\, b_1\, E[X]\, +\, 2\, b_0\, b_2\, E[X^2]\,   +\, {b_1}^2 \, E[X^2] \,+\,2\, b_1\, b_2\, E[X^3] \,+\, {b2}^2 \,E[X^4]\, =\,1\]

This will give us three equations for three unknown coefficients that can be solved to find the coefficients of 2nd polynomial.

We repeat the procedure for third polynomial and write it with undetermined coefficients as

\[P_3(X)= \,c_0\,+\, c_1\, X\, +\, c_2\, X^2 \,+ \,c_3 \,X^3 \,\]

We write three orthogonality equations with respect to first three polynomials and one normality equation as 

\[\int\limits_{0}^{\infty} P_3(X)\,P_0(X) \, p(X) dX \,=\,0 \]
\[\int\limits_{0}^{\infty} P_3(X)\,P_1(X) \, p(X) dX \,=\,0 \]
\[\int\limits_{0}^{\infty} P_3(X)\,P_2(X) \, p(X) dX \,=\,0 \]
\[\int\limits_{0}^{\infty} P_3(X)\,P_3(X) \, p(X) dX \,=\,1 \]

Converting above integral equations to equations with expectations with respect to density weight, we get

\[ \,c_0\, +\, c_1 \,E[X]\, +\, c_2\, E[X^2] \,+\, c_3\, E[X]^3 \,=\,0 \]
\[\, a_0\, c_0\, +\, a_0\, c_1\, E[X] \,+ \,a_0\, c_2\, E[X^2] \,+\, a_0\, c_3\, E[X^3] \,+ a_1\, c_0\, E[X]\, +\, a_1\, c_1\, E[X^2] +   a_1\, c_2 \,E[X^3]\, \\+\, a_1\, c_3\, E[X^4]\,=\,0\]
\[\, b_0\, c_0\, +\, b_0\, c_1 \, E[X]\, + \,b_0\, c_2\, E[X^2]\, +\, b_0\, c_3\, E[X^3]\, +\, b_1\, c_0\, E[X] \,+\, b_1\, c_1\, E[X^2]\, \\+\, b_1\, c_2\, \, [E[X^3]\,+\, b_1\, c_3\, E[X^4]\,+ \,b_2\, c_0\, E[X^2]\, +\, b_2\, c_1\, E[X^3]\, +\, b_2\, c_2\, E[X^4]\,+\, b_2\, c_3\, E[X^5]\,=\,0\, \]
\[\, {c_0}^2 \,+\, 2\, c_0\, c_1\, E[X] \,+ \,2 \,c_0\, c2 \,E[X^2]\, +\,2\, c_0\, c_3\, E[X^3]\,  +\, {c_1}^2\, E[X^2]\, +\,2\, c_1\, c_2\, E[X^3]\, \\+\,2\, c_1\, c_3\, E[X^4]\, +\, {c_2}^2 \,E[X^4]\, +\, c_2\, c_3\, E[X^5]\,+\, {c_3}^2 \,E[X^6]\,=1 \]

similarly, We can recursively continue this process to specify orthogonality and normality equations and solve them to find coefficients of higher order orthonormalized polynomials
