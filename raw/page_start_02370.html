Friends, I believe there could be a way to predict the market within a regression-like framework of hermite polynomials that we used for conditional expectations. For this we would have to convert every indicator to hermite-polynomial/Z series of its own. Many of these indicators are designed from Z-series of log returns already. But we would have to re-convert them into Z-series of its own again (this can be done from historical data and inversion to find Z could be done in real time on live trading day and during backtesting).
For example, Our indicator for state of a single stock would be 

[$]IS_{state}(t)\, =\, \sum\limits_{k=0}^{3} \, \, w_k\, Z_m(t-k)\,[$] 

We would have to decide and use the precise forms of indicators(the form that has maximum explanatory power) to be used in regression. Below I am using two indicators that we successfully used in a recent program post#2372. For example if we want to use [$]IS_{state}(t)\, - \, IS_{state}(t-1)\,[$] as an effective explanatory variable for regression, we would have to convert it to its Z-series. As 

[$]\big[IS_{state}(t)\, - \, IS_{state}(t-1)\big]\,=\, c_{I_s,0} \, + \, c_{I_s,1} \, H_1(Z_{I_s})\, + \, c_{I_s,2} \, H_2(Z_{I_s})\, + \, \ldots[$]

Similarly we would have to make a Z-series for change of auto-correlation of stock (autocorrelation was earlier represented by Z_{corr}(t)) variable  as

[$]\big[Z_{corr}(t)\, - \, Z_{corr}(t-1)\big]\,=\, c_{I_{corr},0} \, + \, c_{I_{corr},1} \, H_1(Z_{I_{corr}})\, + \, c_{I_{corr},2} \, H_2(Z_{I_{corr}})\, + \, \ldots[$]

So we would have to make Z-series of effective relevant forms of 7-10 or more indicators that have large explanatory power related to both single stock being traded and related to broader market. These Z-series could be made on previous day from live trading day using past few weeks of data and we would only need their inversion in live trading. 

For brevity of exposition, let us suppose we only have two indicators, we could write our hermite series regression formula as

[$]dR(t+1)\, = \, \alpha_1\, \, H_1(Z_{I_s})\,+ \, \alpha_2\, \, H_2(Z_{I_s})\,+ \, \alpha_3\, \, H_3(Z_{I_s})\,+ \, \alpha_4\, \, H_1(Z_{I_{corr}})\,+ \, \alpha_5\, \, H_2(Z_{I_{corr}})\,+ \, \alpha_6\, \, H_3(Z_{I_{corr}})\, [$]

where alphas in above equation are regression coefficients that would have been calculated in advance from historical data. 
In the above equation, we have taken only two explanatory variables but there could be anywhere between  7-15 explanatory variables in total.

In the above we have tried to predict the next log-return but ideally we want to optimize for profits in a simulation. And in that case (of simulation) we would have to deviate from a simple regression as (given here for only two explanatory variables). Now we would have to embed the below equation on a backtest simulation and optimize for the coefficients so that profit would be maximized.

We use the variable to define the trade opening and closing indicators in simulation as

[$]    I_1(t)= 
\begin{cases}

I think there are many ways we can combine probabilistic methods like Z-series with other deterministic methods like ODEs and PDES. 
There are a large number of phenomenon we can easily solve with deterministic methods like ODEs and PDEs and we can easily get their analytic result in terms of equations based on a few coefficients. Sometimes it turns out that there is some parameter in those equations that is random and that makes it very difficult to solve those problems analytically. 
In order to easily solve such problems, we need to first make a Z-series associated with the distribution of random parameter. And then solve the PDE or ODE analytically multiple times (may be 50 times) with random parameter taking values associated with its Z-series on its ideal normal grid. Then we can regress the coefficients of analytic solution of ODE/PDE on Z-series of the random parameter and find Z-series of all of the coefficients of analytical solution in terms of a common Z that describes the randomness of the original random parameter. This way we can augment a simple analytical solution into a sophisticated solution that perfectly describes the behavior of analytical solution as value of the random parameter in the problem changes.
We can use the above method to find solution of stochastic volatility problems very easily. For example we might have a normal/lognormal/CEV/displaced diffusion model with stochastic volatility. If the volatility were constant, we could mostly solve the resulting distribution of the model in closed form in terms of equations described by a few coefficients. We would have to first find the Z-series of univariate volatility/integral of volatility(we did in past years how to find Z-series of integral of a univariate mean reverting SDE like that of volatility. This can be done from simulations as well). And then we could solve the analytical underlying formula with constant volatility multiple times with values of stochastic volatility taken from the Z-series of integral of volatility on its ideal normal grid. Then we could simply regress the coefficients of analytical solution of closed form formula on Z-series of volatility and find a Z-series representation of coefficients of stochastic volatility model with respect to a common Z associated with the integral of stochastic volatility.
There are so many models that we can solve linearly very easily but their solution becomes very difficult when some random parameter is introduced in the problem. Many of such problems could be solved by combining Z-series regression with analytic solution of the associated ODE/PDE.
