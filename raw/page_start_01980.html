Friends, I want to very briefly explain the algorithm for inversion of 2D Hermite series to get the bivariate probability density on the specified grid of asset [$]X_n[$] and volatility [$]V_m[$] on a uniform or non-uniform rectangular grid. My notation might be different from matlab program.
We have an independent univariate Z-series/Hermite Series of volatility. 

[$]V(Z_v)\, = \, c_0 \, + c_1 \, Z_v \,+ c_2 \, {Z_v}^2 \,+ c_3 \, {Z_v}^3 \,+ c_4 \, {Z_v}^4 \,+ c_5 \, {Z_v}^5 \,+ c_6 \, {Z_v}^6 \,+ c_7 \, {Z_v}^7 \,[$]
Equation(1)

Each point on stochastic volatility grid is represented as [$]V_m[$]  and this variable has a numerical value [$]v_m[$] at that point.

We first invert the above Z-series of volatility in Eq(1) to find the value of [$]Z_v(m)[$] on each volatility grid point [$]V_m[$]. I have used the more stable bisection root finding procedure for that. So that [$]V_m(Z_v(m))\, =\, v_m[$]

Then we have equation for bivariate Hermite Series that is equal to bivariate product of stochastic volatility and asset on each cell of the rectangular grid.
This equation is given as

[$]U(Z_x,Z_y) \, =\, \sum\limits_{k_1=0}^7 \, \sum\limits_{k_2=0}^7 \, C(k_1,k_2) \, H_{k_1}(Z_x) \, H_{k_2}(Z_y) [$]  Equation(2)
We can also write the above bivariate product U on the grid points  (X_n,V_m) as
[$]U(Z_x(X_n,V_m),Z_y(V_m)) \,=X_n\, V_m [$]

We already know the value of [$]Z_v(V_m)[$] on all of the volatility grid points as we earlier did that. We substitute the numerical value of [$]Z_v(V_m)[$] in the two dimensional hermite series (sequentially for each grid point [$]V_m[$]) to get a Z_series/Hermite Series only in [$]Z_x[$]. This new series in only in [$]Z_x[$] since numerical value of [$]Z_v(V_m)[$] has been substituted in the bivariate Hermite Series. This resulting Hermite Series in [$]Z_x[$] is the product of univariate hermite Series of [$]X_n[$] , representing the X_axis associated with the particular value of stochastic volatility associated with [$]Z_v[$] that we had substituted in the 2D Hermite Series.
To get a pure Z-series of X-axis assiociated with the particular stochastic volatility point we divide the above resulting Z-series with the value of [$]V_m=v_m[$] on that volatility grid point. And after the division with volatility at that grid point, it gives us a true Z-series of X-axis associated with the particular grid point [$]V_m[$]

Writing the above explanation in the form of Equations, we have if the numerical value of stochastic volatility at mth grid point [$]V_m=v_m[$], and associated numerical value of underlying [$]Z_v=z_m[$], we substitute it in the 2D Hermite Equation U and divide by [$]v_m[$]. This division is done since U here is product of [$]X_n[$] and [$]V_m[$] and we want to divide by [$]V_m=v_m[$] to get the equation of only [$]X_n[$] out of the equation of the product.

[$]X(Z_x(n,m)) \, =\Large \, \frac{\, U(Z_x(X_n,V_m),Z_v(V_m)) \, \LARGE {|}^{Z_v(V_m)=z_m}}{v_m}[$]  Equation(3)

The above formula gives us a univariate Hermite Series for that line of asset grid that is associated with mth volatility grid point [$]V_m[$]. This univariate Z-series can easily be inverted with our bisection algorithm to find [$]Z_x[$] associated with [$]X_n[$] line on mth volatility grid point.

The above formula is repeated for each of the points [$]V_m[$] on the stochastic volatility grid and it gives us a Z-series that is valid on the X points associated only for mth volatility grid cell. 
if total number of volatility grid points is M, We can then invert each of the M dependent Z-series to get the value of [$]Z_x(n,m)[$]. Each of the Z-series is valid for X points associated with that particular volatility grid point that was substituted in the Equation(3) to get the value of Z-series. All of these M Z-series are inverted with the bisection root-finding algorithm that I have previously posted by using a loop that has M iterations.

So with above analytics, we calculate [$]Z_x(n,m)[$] and [$]Z_v(m)[$] associated with each grid point [$]X_n[$] and [$]V_m[$] on the rectangular grid.

After this we calculate the derivatives [$]\frac{dZ_v(m)}{dV_m}[$] and [$]\frac{dZ_x(n,m)}{dX_n}[$] on all of the grid points using simple finit differences. These derivatives are then used to calculate the Jacobian of the density of bivariate variable [$]X_nV_m[$] with respect to two standard normals. 
Since stochastic volatility did not have any dependence on [$]Z_x[$], our jacobian is simply product of above two derivatives.

We get the Equation of Bivariate Density as

[$]p(X_n,V_m)\, = \, p_N(Z_x(n,m)) \, \frac{dZ_x(n,m)}{dX_n} \,  \, p_N(Z_y(m)) \, \frac{dZ_y(m)}{dV_m} \,[$] 

Here in the RHS of Equation [$]p_N[$] denotes standard normal density.
