Friends, I have been thinking that in 2D Z-series of Y, after we have removed the correlation with X as follows (please refer to post 2815)
\[\tilde{Y}_p\,=\,Y_p-\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,\rho_{XY_n} \, c_n \, H_n(Z_{X_p}) \,\]
We are left with zero mean noise uncorrelated with X. I have called it Ydecorr in my matlab program. I suspect that trying to find the coefficients of variance of noise by regression on noise is a futile effort. Rather this regression has to be done on Variance of noise (which in this case matches the second moment since mean is zero) since cancellation effect in regression directly on noise is not there when we do regression on variance.
So I suggest that we should do a non-linear regression on variance of noise to find the 2D Z-series of  \(\tilde{Y}\)

Here I try to write the whole process of finding 2D coefficients of  Z-series of \(\tilde{Y}\) as step by step points. First we construct Z-series of X and Y by one dimensional optimization as

\[Y(Z_Y)\,=\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, c_n\, H_n(Z_Y) \] 

\[X(Z_X)\,=\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, b_n\, H_n(Z_X) \] 

We remove the correlation effect of X from corresponding pairs of Y. This is done as

\[\tilde{Y}_p\,=\,Y_p-\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,\rho_{XY_n} \, c_n \, H_n(Z_{X_p}) \,\]

We fit a univariate Z-series to moments of \(\tilde{Y}\). This Z-series is denoted as

\[\tilde{Y}(\tilde{Z}_Y)\,=\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, d_n\, {\tilde{Z}_Y}^n \] 

Here I want to mention that \(\tilde{Z}_Y\) is different from \(Z_Y\). \(Z_Y\) is possibly correlated with \(Z_X\) but \(\tilde{Z}_Y\) is orthogonal to \(Z_X\).

We back out \(Z_{X_p}\) and \(\tilde{Z}_{Y_p}\) for each pth joint observation pair of X and \(\tilde{Y}\). We suppose that total observations of data are denoted as P.

Now we have to do non-linear iterative least squares on the equation of the form to minimize the expression below

\[\, \sum_{p=1}^{P}\, \left[ {  \,{\tilde{Y_p}}^2 \,- \,\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, b_{n,m}\,{\tilde{Z}_{Y_p}}^n\,{Z_{X_p}}^m\,\right)}^2\right]^2 \] 

I suggest that we use the values of \(\tilde{Z}_{Y_p}\) and \(Z_{X_p}\) found from the data of \(\tilde{Y}_p\) and \(X_p\) pairs in the above equation.

In order for optimization, the derivative of the above objective function with respect to arbitrary \(b_{n_1,m_1}\) is given as

\[\,4\, \sum_{p=1}^{P}\, \left[ {  \,{\tilde{Y_p}}^2 \,- \,\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, b_{n,m}\,{\tilde{Z}_{Y_p}}^n\,{Z_{X_p}}^m\,\right)}^2\right]\,\,\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, b_{n,m}\,{\tilde{Z}_{Y_p}}^n\,{Z_{X_p}}^m\,\right) \,{Z_{X_p}}^{m_1}\,{\tilde{Z}_{Y_p}}^{n_1} \] 

I am very confident that iterative solution of the above equation would give far better results than we got when we regressed the hermite polynomials of \(\tilde{Z}_Y\) and \(Z_X\) on the first power of \(\tilde{Y}\).

This could be an efficient way to solve the problem of finding coefficients of 2D Z-series by matching of variances and I am very sure that it would give equally good and stable results as we had from full moment matching.

Friends, I am trying to think of some possible alternatives but you might have to bear with my mistakes. As I see, in our models, least squares is minimizing variances by minimizing over Gaussian noise (in case of 1D regression) and 2D Gaussian noise(in our 2D regression) and in many cases one dimensional model has better R-squared than 2D Z-series models. It can simply minimize squared deviations by 1D form of noise rather than 2D form of noise and give even better R-squared when optimizing with 1D form of noise. 

I really think that minimizing squared deviations over squared values(squared observations and squared values of model values) would give better results for double stochasticity in parameters as we have in the form of 2D Z-series.

However variances or second moments have no respect for signs of coefficients of 2D Z-series (as all squared values are positive) and we could get right signs of coefficients only from simple least squares regression that we have been doing before. 

It might seem very odd but I suggest that we simultaneously minimize a simple squared differences (of observations and model values) form and squared differences of squared values(of observations and model values). This would have to be done with a highly over-constrained Newton method. This minimization is given as

Now we have to do non-linear iterative Newton method on the equation of the form to minimize the expression below. Basically we want to optimize for coefficients \(b_{n,m}\) of 2D Z-series so that value of dual objective function below is minimized.

\[\, \sum_{p=1}^{P}\, \left[ {  \,{\tilde{Y_p}} \,- \,\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, b_{n,m}\,{\tilde{Z}_{Y_p}}^n\,{Z_{X_p}}^m\,\right)}\right]^2 \\ + \sum_{p=1}^{P}\, \left[ {  \,{\tilde{Y_p}}^2 \,- \,\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, b_{n,m}\,{\tilde{Z}_{Y_p}}^n\,{Z_{X_p}}^m\,\right)}^2\right]^2 \]  

This is minimization over observations and not minimization over moments.


It is well known that regression usually fails to pick Gaussian noise. We could regress 1D Z-series over underlying Gaussian since the observations and underlying noise was in a simultaneously increasing ordered sequence.

In 2D Z-series of decorrelated \(\tilde{Y}\), \(Z_y\) is in the same ordered sequence as the decorrelated observations, so it will be picked by our regression. But \(Z_x\) that appears in coefficients of \(Z_y\) is in no particular order to decorrelated data and might appear as pure Gaussian noise that cannot be picked by regressions and we might have to add a regression over squares to pick its proper values.
