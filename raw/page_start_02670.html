Friends, I am going to post the matlab programs for Algorithmic trading with Hermite regressions. I had earlier mentioned this technique but never posted the complete program. Even though, the program I currently give uses stock data, I have used this technique very successfully for crypto currencies as I have earlier pointed out in my paper. Here is the relevant portion of the paper (Matlab Programs with stocks trading in next following posts).



Applications of Z-series Towards Algorithmic Trading of Stocks

In order to find the predictive power of Hermite-Series regression, we tried to predict the next ten seconds prices of bitcoin using ten other largest currencies as explanatory variables. We updated our Hermite-series of the log-returns of each of the ten largest Crypto-currencies with four coefficients (one zeroth Hermite and three higher Hermite polynomials) by discrete normal grid density method (first sorting the data and then regressing on associated values of hermite polynomials) after every hundred points on our ten seconds grid. So calculation of Hermite series was re-done again and again after every 16 minutes and 40 seconds. Crypto trades for 24 hours on Binance so there are 8640 points on time grid.  In each of the successive recalculations of Hermite-Series, all the data from start of the day was used so first regression had hundred data count while second regression had 200 data count and so on.  

The regression for estimation of coefficients is done in cross-correlation sense. I am not using hermite polynomials because purpose is to emphasize the time index only.

Following section(\ref{sec5.1}), we write the regression equation to explain Y by M explanatory variables \(X_{m,t},\, m\,=\,1,\dots,M\)  where we include N first hermite polynomials of each \(X_{m,t}\) as


\begin{equation}\label{6.4.1}
\,Y_t\,=\, \beta_0\,+\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,\,\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\,\beta_{m,n}\, H_n(Z_{m,t})\,
\end{equation}


and prediction is done by using the equation ---- emphasizing time index only 

\begin{equation}\label{6.4.2}
\,Y_{t+1}\,=\, \beta_0\,+\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,\,\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\,\beta_{m,n}\, H_n(Z_{m,t})\,
\end{equation}

The regression is done in cross-correlation sense on the explanatory assets at the same  time level as dependent asset on 10 seconds grid. Here in regression index of regressand and regressors both is t.

We use the following criteria to open and close the trades.

\begin{equation}\label{6.4.3}
    I(t)= 
\begin{cases}
    1 ,& \text{if } Y_{t+1}  \geq Y_h\\
    -1 ,& \text{if } Y_{t+1}\leq Y_l\\
    0,              & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

When \(I(t)\,=\,1\), we end any open sell trades and open a buy trade similarly when  \(I(t)\,=\, -1\), we end any open buy trades and open a sell trade. When \(I(t)\,=\,0\), we take no action where \(Y_h\) and \(Y_l\) are calculated by experimentation on data.

The choice of ten seconds grid, and choice of 100 intervals to redo all the hermite series and regression calculations was a bit arbitrary and friends might like to use their own judgement about increasing and decreasing these intervals. Since I had daily data in separate files and first regression was done after hundred time grid points, friends can see that in all the graphs red line for predicted returns starts after 100 time intervals since this was the time when first regression results became available in my set up.

[attachment=0]Hermite-Predicted-Returns03.png[/attachment]
 


The complete Crypto data was for 42 days and despite that I was recalculating regression after 16.66 minutes of data, it still took only between 15 to 20 minutes to do all the calculation of 42 days of data on my simple laptop. 

My program made a hypothetical profit of 296,034 dollars for trading one bitcoin (which was roughly around 65,000 dollars) for 42 days(with 24 hours continuous trading) and made 127,969 trades making 2.31 dollars per trade. However unfortunately due to high commissions proportional to currency being traded on crypto currencies, this was not able to pay even the two side commissions on the Bitcoin trades for an ordinary user although similar trading could have been hugely profitable for a lot of stocks trading in most stock markets due to a different fixed (as opposed to proportional) commission structure in many stock markets.   

The seemingly high profits without commissions were possible since our hermite regression was able to capture quite a large share of total variance/variation in bitcoin price on ten seconds scale as could be seen through graphs in previous post. But in reality quite a few caveats would exist like impact of our trades on the market, order slippage, competition between several traders and many other factors that we did not consider in our calculations and making such profits would be impossible. But still I believe there is very good opportunity for people who could have very high trading volumes and they can make very handsome profits though actual profits would be substantially (possibly an order of magnitude smaller) smaller than in the simulation.

Friends, when we are dealing with statistics of skew and kurtosis in financial trading, we would also want to know how variable these statistics are. Does the skew or kurtosis remain in a small range of they vary a lot and are all over the place?

This is where sixth and eighth moment comes in. Sixth moment is intuitively associated with variance of skew. If standardized sixth moment is very large with respect to third moment, it means that skew is changing all over the place and is highly variable. If sixth moment is smaller with respect to third moment, it would mean that skew is changing in a small range. 

We can have a useful standardized statistic to know this variability. This statistic could be square root of standardized sixth moment divided by standardized third moment. The larger the absolute value of this statistic, the larger the relative variability in skew. Here the statistic \(\rho_3\)  can be given as

\[\,\rho_3=\, \Large{\frac{\,\sqrt{\mu_6}}{\,\mu_3}} \, \]

where \(\mu_3\) and  \(\mu_6\) are standardized third and sixth moments.

A similar statistic \(\rho_4\) for variability of kurtosis would be given as

\[\,\rho_4=\, \Large{\frac{\,\sqrt{\mu_8}}{\,\mu_4}} \, \]

where \(\mu_4\) and  \(\mu_8\) are standardized fourth and eighth moments. Higher the value of \(\rho_4\), the larger the variability in kurtosis. And if \(\rho_4\) is small, it means that kurtosis remains in a relatively small range and does not change all over.

These indicators \(\rho_3\)  and \(\rho_4\) would be very helpful in algorithmic trading of stocks and options.
