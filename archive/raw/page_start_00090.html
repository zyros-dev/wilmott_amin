You could easily apply the method of iterated integrals for the evaluation of integrals. I will give a general example and later we will see in an application that many integrals like for example, fourier, laplace, mellin and other transforms can be very easily calculated by the method sometimes even when the closed form formulas for those transforms do not exist.Let us consider an integral of the form[$]g(k)=\int _a^bf(k,x)\text{dx}[$] Eq(1)Just like we did in the expansion of ODE coefficients, we can write the function f(k,x) as[$]f(k,x)=f(k,x(a))+\int _a^b\frac{\partial f(k,x)}{\partial x}\text{dx}[$] Eq(2)Expanding further two terms one by one, we get the equation[$]f(k,x)=f(k,x(a))+\int _a^b\frac{\partial f(k,x(a))}{\partial x}\text{dx}+\int _a^b\int _a^b\frac{\partial ^2f(k,x(a))}{\partial x^2}\text{dxdx}\text{++}\int _a^b\int _a^b\int _a^b\frac{\partial ^3f(k,x)}{\partial x^3}\text{dxdxdx}[$] Eq(3)Now we put Eq(3) in the integral in Eq(1) to get[$]g(k)=\int _a^bf(k,x(a))\text{dx}+\int _a^b\int _a^b\frac{\partial f(k,x(a))}{\partial x}\text{dxdx}+\int _a^b\int _a^b\int _a^b\frac{\partial ^2f(k,x(a))}{\partial x^2}\text{dxdxdx}+\text{higher order terms}[$][$]=f(k,x(a))\int _a^b\text{dx}+\frac{\partial f(k,x(a))}{\partial x}\int _a^b\int _a^b\text{dxdx}+\frac{\partial ^2f(k,x(a))}{\partial x^2}\int _a^b\int _a^b\int _a^b\text{dxdxdx}+\text{higher order terms}[$] Eq(4)One could observe the similarity with expansion of ODE and SDE coefficients when one would look at similar iterated integrals and constant coefficients.our function f(k,x) can have many many simpler forms. For example Eq(1) could be [$]g(k)=\int _a^be^{i \text{kx}}h(x)\text{dx}[$][$]g(k)=\int _a^bv(k,x)h(x)\text{dx}[$]Just like exponential of very large numbers, we might have to take very large number of terms in expansion to get complete convergence for difficult transforms though the problem should not be severe for most of the transform integrals. But when the solution of a difficult transform has to be found, one could easily divide the integration domain into 4, 8, or 16 subdivisions and locally apply the method of iterated integrals. This could also work very well for numerical calculation of transforms where one could find transform locally in each of the subdivisions by the formula by expanding upto 5-6 terms and then patch the solutions together across different subdivisions.We can see that after calculation of fourier/laplace transform, our transform could be in the form of sum of terms of the kind [$]a e^{b k}k^n[$] Eq(5) where a and b are arbitrary constants and n is arbitrary power. The way we generally find the solution of a PDE by transforms could now be modified by taking a general solution of for the term given by equation (5) above (by for example substituting in the PDE) and finding a complete solution by the principle of superposition of different terms. As complexity increases, there could be many modifications on the theme I mentioned limited only by ingenuity of the mathematician.

I wrote this attempt at solution of general fokker planck PDE the day before yesterday on Latex. I have not numerically verified it so I cannot be sure whether it is right or not. Since my research reaches some American institutions of higher learning, I have decided to put it on internet otherwise I would have waited to verify it numerically.Let us consider a general fokker planck equation stated as [$]\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}=-\frac{\partial [\mu (x)p(x,t)]}{\partial x}+\frac{.5\partial ^2\left[\sigma (x)^2p(x,t)\right]}{\partial x^2}[$] Eq(1)Expanding the first order and second order derivatives on the right side of PDE in Eq(1), we get[$]\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}=-\mu (x)\frac{\partial p(x,t)]}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial \mu (x)}{\partial x}p(x,t)+\frac{\partial \sigma (x)}{\partial x}p(x,t)+2\sigma (x)\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial x}+.5\sigma (x)^2\frac{\partial ^2p(x,t)}{\partial x^2}[$] Eq(2)We divide the x-dimension in PDE into small subdivisions like we did in stochastic calculus of Standard deviations, and we know that each standard deviation moves in a way that mass is preserved in each subdivision. We want to find a local solution to this PDE for each subdivision. Let us fix some notations first. Each subdivision at time t starts at coordinates given as [$]x_a(\text{t})[$] and ends at coordinates [$]x_b(\text{t})[$]. These are the limits of integration when we integrate for the probability mass over each subdivision. We also set [$]x_0(\text{t})[$] as the centre of the subdivision at time t. [$]C_n[$] is the probability mass in subdivision related to nth standard deviation of the normal. When we have to deal with normal distribution after setting the equations, we replace normal distribution by appropriate arbitrary standard deviation n and then solve the equations for each n separately. We want to try a time averaged form of normal distribution as a solution in this pde. We try a solution where [$]\bar{\mu }[$] and [$]\bar{\sigma }[$] are time averaged values of drift and volatility for the specific step size from [$]t_0[$] to [$]t_1[$] and subdivision limits [$]x_a(\text{t})[$] to [$]x_b(\text{t})[$] and we use them as the local solution for that specific x and t discretisation. We try to present the averaged local solution for each subdivision as a normal given by the equation[$]g(x,t)=\frac{1}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{2\pi t}}\exp \left(-.5\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right){}^2\right)[$] Eq(3)We will like to emphasize that once the two ends of subdivisions [$]x_a(\text{t})[$] and [$]x_b(\text{t})[$], or alternatively mid of the subdivision [$]x_0(\text{t})[$], and drift and volatility parameters [$]\bar{\mu }[$] and [$]\bar{\sigma }[$] are given, we can find the ends of subdivisions at the next time step as[$]x_a\left(t_1\right)=x_a\left(t_0\right)+\bar{\mu }\left(t_1-t_0\right)-.5\bar{\sigma }\left(\sqrt{t_1}-\sqrt{t_0}\right)[$] Eq(4)[$]x_b\left(t_1\right)=x_b\left(t_0\right)+\bar{\mu }\left(t_1-t_0\right)+.5\bar{\sigma }\left(\sqrt{t_1}-\sqrt{t_0}\right)[$] Eq(5)We start by taking the appropriate derivatives of the equation before we put it in the Pde Eq(2). The derivatives are given as[$]\frac{\partial g(x,t)}{\partial x}=-g(x,t)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)[$] Eq(6)[$]\frac{\partial ^2g(x,t)}{\partial x^2}=g(x,t)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right){}^2-\frac{g(x,t)}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}[$] Eq(7)[$]\frac{\partial g(x,t)}{\partial t}=g(x,t)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{2\bar{\sigma }t^{1,5}}+\frac{\bar{\mu }}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)[$] Eq(8)We integrate both sides of the PDE in Eq(2) over time from [$]t_0[$] to [$]t_1[$] to find the change in probability mass and then we integrate over each subdivision [$]x_a(\text{t_1})[$] and [$]x_b(\text{t_1})[$] to find the total probability mass in each subdivision that is kept constant by supposition and is the same as the mass at the start in subdivision [$]x_a(\text{t_0})[$] and [$]x_b(\text{t_0})[$] given by [$]C_n[$]. We get two equations from both sides of the PDE and we solve them to find the unknowns that are [$]\bar{\mu }[$] and [$]\bar{\sigma }[$], the time averaged values of drift and volatility for the specific step size from [$]t_0[$] to [$]t_1[$] and subdivision limits [$]x_a(\text{t_1})[$] to [$]x_b(\text{t_1})[$]We put Eq(6) in LHS of PDE in Eq(2) and do this as[$]\int _{x_a(\text{t1})}^{x_{b(\text{t1})}}\int _{t_0}^{t_1}\frac{\partial p(x, t)}{\partial t} \text{dt} \text{dx}=C_n[$] Eq(8)[$]\int _{x_a(\text{t1})}^{x_b(\text{t1})}\int _{t_0}^{t_1}g(x,t)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{2\bar{\sigma }t^{1,5}}+\frac{\bar{\mu }}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)\text{dt} \text{dx}=C_n[$] Eq(9)We replace normal function g(x,t) by specific standard deviation 'n' for which we are solving the PDE. We know standard deviations are units of normal. We will have to solve the equation for each different n using the same algorithm.[$]\int _{x_a(\text{t1})}^{x_b(\text{t1})}\int _{t_0}^{t_1}n\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{2\bar{\sigma }t^{1,5}}+\frac{\bar{\mu }}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)\text{dt} \text{dx}=C_n[$] Eq(10)using Eq(4) and Eq(5) given again as[$]x_a\left(t_1\right)=x_a\left(t_0\right)+\bar{\mu }\left(t_1-t_0\right)-.5\bar{\sigma }\left(\sqrt{t_1}-\sqrt{t_0}\right)[$] Eq(4)[$]x_b\left(t_1\right)=x_b\left(t_0\right)+\bar{\mu }\left(t_1-t_0\right)+.5\bar{\sigma }\left(\sqrt{t_1}-\sqrt{t_0}\right)[$] Eq(5)and putting them as limits of integration for each subdivision, we get[$]\int _{x_a\left(t_0\right)+\bar{\mu }\left(t_1-t_0\right)-.5\bar{\sigma }\left(\sqrt{t_1}-\sqrt{t_0}\right)}^{x_b\left(t_0\right)+\bar{\mu }\left(t_1-t_0\right)+.5\bar{\sigma }\left(\sqrt{t_1}-\sqrt{t_0}\right)}\int _{t_0}^{t_1}n\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{2\bar{\sigma }t^{1,5}}+\frac{\bar{\mu }}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)\text{dt} \text{dx}=C_n[$] Eq(11)We put Eq(7) and Eq(8) in RHS of PDE (2) and just Similar to what we did for the left hand side of the PDE as[$]\int _{x_a\left(t_1\right)}^{x_b\left(t_1\right)}\int _{t_0}^{t_1}\left(-\frac{\partial [\mu (x)p(x,t)]}{\partial x}+\frac{.5\partial ^2\left[\sigma (x)^2p(x,t)\right]}{\partial x^2}\right) \text{dt} \text{dx}=C_n[$] Eq(12)[$]\int _{x_a\left(t_1\right)}^{x_b\left(t_1\right)}\int _{t_0}^{t_1}\left(-\mu (x)\frac{\partial p(x,t)]}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial \mu (x)}{\partial x}p(x,t)+\frac{\partial \sigma (x)}{\partial x}p(x,t)+2\sigma (x)\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial x}+.5\sigma (x)^2\frac{\partial ^2p(x,t)}{\partial x^2}\right) \text{dt} \text{dx}=C_n[$] Eq(13)[$]\int _{x_a\left(t_1\right)}^{x_b\left(t_1\right)}\int _{t_0}^{t_1}\left(\mu (x)g(x,t)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)-\frac{\partial \mu (x)}{\partial x}g(x,t)+\frac{\partial \sigma (x)}{\partial x}g(x,t)-2\sigma (x)g(x,t)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)+.5\sigma (x)^2\left(g(x,t)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right){}^2-\frac{g(x,t)}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)\right) \text{dt} \text{dx}=C_n[$] Eq(14)We replace normal function g(x,t) by specific standard deviation 'n' for which we are solving the PDE. We know standard deviations are units of normal. We will have to solve the equation for each different n using the same algorithm.[$]\int _{x_a\left(t_1\right)}^{x_b\left(t_1\right)}\int _{t_0}^{t_1}n\left(+\mu (x)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)-\frac{\partial \mu (x)}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial \sigma (x)}{\partial x}-2\sigma (x)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)+.5\sigma (x)^2\left(\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right){}^2-\frac{1}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)\right) \text{dt} \text{dx}=C_n[$] Eq(15)using Eq(4) and Eq(5) and replacing the ends of the subdivisions in the integration limits as[$]\int _{x_a\left(t_0\right)+\bar{\mu }\left(t_1-t_0\right)-.5\bar{\sigma }\left(\sqrt{t_1}-\sqrt{t_0}\right)}^{x_b\left(t_0\right)+\bar{\mu }\left(t_1-t_0\right)+.5\bar{\sigma }\left(\sqrt{t_1}-\sqrt{t_0}\right)}\int _{t_0}^{t_1}n\left(\mu (x)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)-\frac{\partial \mu (x)}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial \sigma (x)}{\partial x}-2\sigma (x)\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)+.5\sigma (x)^2\left(\left(\frac{x-x_0-\bar{\mu }t}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right){}^2-\frac{1}{\bar{\sigma }\sqrt{t}}\right)\right) \text{dt} \text{dx}=C_n[$] Eq(16)Now we have two equations in two unknowns that are [$]\bar{\mu }[$] and [$]\bar{\sigma }[$], the local time averaged values of drift and volatility for the specific step size from [$]t_0[$] to [$]t_1[$] and subdivision limits [$]x_a(\text{t_1})[$] to [$]x_b(\text{t_1})[$] and we have two equations Eq(11) and Eq(16) that we can solve together for each value of standard deviation n to find the two unknowns. Ok, we were very close in stochastic calculus of standard deviations but some improvement was required in the method. We know how to tackle parts involving time and gaussian dependent terms g(z,t). Let us consider one step advance of the densityX(t+1)=X(t)+f_1(X(t))g_1(z , t)+f_2(X(t))g_2(z,t)+..f_k(X(t))g_k(z,t);where f_n(X) is evaluated at X=X_0 i.e centre of each of the subdivisions at time t.The terms of type f(X) are sometimes very non-linear and especially close to zero they may become extremely non-linear. On top of that probability density may be very non-linear close to zero and mid point evaluated value f(X_0) may not capture steep change in probability density. We have to take a value of each f(X) term which is representative of the value(effective value) in the particular subdivision interval and we cannot freely choose it to be f(X_0) which can however work well if the subdivisions are very small and representative/effective value and value at centre become very close.Representative/effective value can easily be calculated using the conditional Bayes law by taking an integral of f(X)p(X)dX evaluated over the particular subdivision divided by the integral of p(X)dX evaluated over the same subdivision interval. If the interval is very small or the shape of pdf is so that we can suppose p(X) is a level surface in that interval (meaning non-linearity comes only from f(X) term), we can take integral of f(X)dX in numerator and integral of dX in denominator and this could work decently well. Here p(X) is used to denote probability density.
