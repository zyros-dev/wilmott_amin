First the basics of exotics hedging with single underlying. We will cover interest rate derivatives and multiple underlyings later. I will post a working program in a few days.

Friends let us consider a permissible exotic derivative on a single underlying represented by X(t, T) . T corresponds to maturity of the derivative contract and t is the present observation date. 

We calculate probability density function or moments associated with the probability density function of X(T) as seen at time t. This probability density function can be calculated from simulation of some appropriate model or in a model independent way directly from the recent data of the underlying. Here I want to stress that distributions and associated moments of various vanilla and exotic derivatives can also be calculated directly from the data.(For example in order to calculate density/moments of derivative V(t,T) where t=0 and T=six months from historical data, we can collect daily data for t=0 to 3 months and T= t + six months for three months and then construct derivative density( of V(t,T)=V(0,six months)) and moments from that data. Of course many times you would have to be careful and judicious about range over which a data has to be collected for above calculations. We will cover all these issues as our research progresses.

Once we calculate the pdf/moments of the exotic derivative and underlying, we would still have to be careful since this distribution is changing over the life of the derivative as for example we advance V(t,T) from V(0,6 months) to next day V(1 , 6 months) but we will cover these issues later. This post wants to deal with very simple basics. We will take care of all details on top of our basics one by one later.

Suppose after calculation of moments of the exotic derivative, we represent it as a hermite series random variable as

[$] V_x(t, T) \, = \,a_0 \, + a_1 \, H_1(Z)\, +\,  a_2 \,H_2(Z) \, + \, a_3 \, H_3(Z)[$]

After delta-hedging for the mean payoff associated with zeroth polynomial, we want to find hedges in terms of vanilla derivatives so that volatility of the exotic derivative goes to zero (or very very small in practice)  

We take vanilla options as hedging derivatives but we would have to be careful to choose them so as to minimize collinearity. So their strikes have to be as apart as practically possible. For example one vanilla option can be reasonably deep in the money, second could be ATM while the third could be reasonably OTM. 

Let us suppose we are hedging [$]V_x(t,T)[$] with three vanilla options  [$]V_1(t,T)[$], [$]V_2(t,T)[$], and [$]V_3(t,T)[$], whose hermite representations are given as

[$] V_1(t, T) \, = \,b_0 \, + b_1 \, H_1(Z)\, +\,  b_2 \,H_2(Z) \, + \, b_3 \, H_3(Z)[$] 
[$] V_2(t, T) \, = \,c_0 \, + c_1 \, H_1(Z)\, +\,  c_2 \,H_2(Z) \, + \, c_3 \, H_3(Z)[$] 
[$] V_3(t, T) \, = \,d_0 \, + d_1 \, H_1(Z)\, +\,  d_2 \,H_2(Z) \, + \, d_3 \, H_3(Z)[$] 

Please know that all the three vanilla derivatives are on the same common underlying as the exotic derivative so they share the same underlying Z among themselves and with the exotic derivative as well.

We want to take k1 of first derivative [$]V_1(t,T)[$] and k2 of second derivative [$]V_2(t,T)[$] and k3 of first derivative [$]V_3(t,T)[$] so that variance of the complete portfolio of [$]V_x(t,T)[$] and  [$]v_1(t,T)[$], [$]v_2(t,T)[$] and [$]v_3(t,T)[$] goes to zero.

So we write the linear equations of variance of various hermite polynomial components going to zero as

condition for first hermite polynomial of the portfolio going to zero would be 
[$]\, a_1\, + \, k1 \,b_1\,+ \, k2 \,c_1\,+ \, k3 \,d_1\,=\,0[$]
For second hermite polynomial of portfolio to become zero, we have
[$]\, a_2\, + \, k1 \,b_2\,+ \, k2 \,c_2\,+ \, k3 \,d_2\,=\,0[$]
For third hermite polynomial of portfolio to become zero, we have
[$]\, a_3\, + \, k1 \,b_3\,+ \, k2 \,c_3\,+ \, k3 \,d_3\,=\,0[$]

When above three linear  equations are solved for three unknowns k1, k2, and k3, we would get the quantity of [$]V_1(t,T)[$], [$]V_2(t,T)[$] and [$]V_3 (t,T)[$] in our portfolio with [$]V_x(t,T)[$] so that total local variance of the portfolio goes to zero. 

So cost of the options that make the portfolio riskless till maturity would be price of the option. But we have made it riskless only locally for a small time and we would have to re-hedge possibly everyday or as judicious to rebalance our hedge according to changing probability density of the exotic derivative. We can use both real data or model simulations with daily re-adjustment (as we will learn about it in later posts) of the hedges to calculate the cumulative cost of the exotic derivative.  In reality there would probably always be a small variance due to model mis-specification, parameter mis-calculation and parameter variance and other related factors.

It is [$],\mu_{V_x} [$] which is the expected value (fair value) of the exotic derivative that we usually calculate out of simulations. Rest of the coefficients represent variances of the expected value of the exotic derivative. Please note that zeroth hermite is a point value with zero first order variance if uncertainty of the problem has been properly specified.

Now we write the linear equations so that payoff of complete portfolio goes to zero and variance of various hermite polynomial components of the portfolio goes to zero as

condition for expected value of the portfolio going to zero would be 
[$]\, \mu_{V_x}\, + \, k0 \, \big[\mu_X \,- \, K \big]\,+ \, k1 \, \mu_{V_1}\,+ \, k2 \, \mu_{V_2}\,+ \, k3 \, \mu_{V_3} \,=\,0[$]
condition for first hermite polynomial of the portfolio going to zero would be 
[$]\, a_1\, + \, k0 \, \bar{a_1}\, + \, k1 \,b_1\,+ \, k2 \,c_1\,+ \, k3 \,d_1\,=\,0[$]

So cost of the options that make the portfolio riskless till maturity would be price of the option/exotic derivative. So our true exotic option/derivative price would be the cumulative cost of all the transactions that are required to make the portfolio riskless till maturity.

Friends, when I investigated the above method further, I realized that for several options it was not working well at all since series expansion of analytic Heaviside function was getting worse at points away from the strike. So I decided to change my strategy.
I directly calculated the moments of the option prices with different strikes when the asset is given by a Z-series. These are basically truncated moments but modified so that density integrates to one. We apply the same normalization factor to truncated moments that we apply to the density. This follows from conditionality or Bayes formula. After calculation of first eight moments of the option prices, I went ahead and converted the moments into density of option prices. We model options as assets in themselves that have a density starting at zero on option price axis (as opposed to asset axis where it starts at strike). I noticed that for a large number of CEV like densities of assets, the match to moments of call option prices was perfect and all eight moments perfectly matched all the time. 
I used a lot of programs from previous work. Friends let us consider a  derivative on a single underlying represented by X(t, T) . T corresponds to maturity of the derivative contract and t is the present observation date. We calculate probability density function or moments associated with the probability density function of X(T) as seen at time t. 

Suppose  we represent the underlying asset as a hermite series random variable as

[$] X_{t,T}(Z) \, = \,a_0 \, + a_1 \, Z\, +\,  a_2 \, (Z^2-1) \, + \, a_3 \, (Z^3\,-\,3\,Z)[$]

We can then represent a call option as a truncated Z-series. I want to say that it is perfectly valid Z-series and can be used in analytics. We can find correlations of derivative with respect to underlying, other exotic derivatives on the same underlying, and other assets and exotic derivatives on other underlyings using this representation. Only to find expectations, we cannot simply take expectations of hermite polynomials and we would have to use proper integrations.

[$]V_x(Z)\,=\, \begin{cases}
(\,a_0\,- k\,) + \, a_1\, Z\,+ \, a_2\, (Z^2-1)\, + \, a_3 \, (Z^3\,-\,3\,Z)\, , & \text{ if } Z \geq Z_k \\
0,   & \text{ otherwise }
\end{cases}  [$]
In the above equation [$]Z_k[$] represents the value of Z at which asset equals the value of strike X(Z)=k. or in other words [$]X(Z_k)=k[$]

The expectation of this option can be found by the formula (with zero interest rates)
[$]\,=\,(\,a_0\,- k\,) (1-\Phi(Z_k))\, +\, \phi(Z_k) \, [ a1 \,+\, a_2\, Z_k\, + \, a_3\, ({Z_k}^2-1)\,] [$]


Correlations are between hermite polynomials as ever. In order to find correlations between hermite polynomials of asset and the option (we can also use very similar formula for correlations between two options). We know hermite polynomials of the asset and underlying are the same in positive payoff zone but they are zero in No pay off zone.

We use the formula for correlations to find the correlation between 1st hermite polynomials as

[$]corr_1(X,V_x)\, =\Large{ \, \frac{  \int\limits_{Z_k}^{\infty} \, Z \,*\, Z  \,p(Z) \,dZ }{\sqrt{\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty} \, Z ^2 \,p(Z) \,dZ}\, \sqrt{\int\limits_{Z_k}^{\infty} \, Z^2 \,p(Z) \,dZ}} } [$] 
[$]=\Large{ \, \frac{  (1\,-\, \Phi(Z_k)) + \phi(Z_k) \, Z_k}{\sqrt{(1\,-\, \Phi(Z_k)) + \phi(Z_k)}}}[$]

[$]=\sqrt{  (1\,-\, \Phi(Z_k)) + \phi(Z_k) \, Z_k}[$]

To find these integrals, please convert the square of hermite polynomials into a series in hermite polynomials and then integrate using the simple formula

[$] \int\limits_{Z_k}^{\infty} \, H_n(Z)  \,p(Z) \,dZ \, =\, \phi(Z_k) \, H_{n-1}(Z_k) [$]

similarly we find correlations between second and third hermite polynomials of underlying asset and the option as

[$]corr_2(X,V_x)\, =\Large{ \, \frac{  \int\limits_{Z_k}^{\infty} \, (Z^2\, -\, 1)\, *\, (Z^2\,-\,1)  \,p(Z) \,dZ }{\sqrt{\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty} \, (Z ^2-1)^2 \,p(Z) \,dZ}\, \sqrt{\int\limits_{Z_k}^{\infty} \, (Z^2\,-\,1)^2 \,p(Z) \,dZ}} } [$] 

[$]=\Large{ \, \frac{ 2 \, (1\,-\, \Phi(Z_k)) + \phi(Z_k) \, \Large[  4\, H_1(Z_k) \, +\, H_3(Z_k)         \Large]}{2\,\sqrt{ 2 \, (1\,-\, \Phi(Z_k)) + \phi(Z_k) \, \Large[  4\, H_1(Z_k) \, +\, H_3(Z_k)         \Large]}}}[$]

[$]=\Large{ \, \frac{ \sqrt{ 2 \, (1\,-\, \Phi(Z_k)) + \phi(Z_k) \, \Large[  4\, H_1(Z_k) \, +\, H_3(Z_k)         \Large]}}{2\,}}[$]

for correlations between third hermite polynomials of call price and the asset we have

[$]corr_3(X,V_x)\, =\Large{ \, \frac{  \int\limits_{Z_k}^{\infty} \, (Z^3\, -\, 3\,Z)\, *\, (Z^3\,-\,3\,Z)  \,p(Z) \,dZ }{\sqrt{\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty} \, (Z ^3-3\,Z)^2 \,p(Z) \,dZ}\, \sqrt{\int\limits_{Z_k}^{\infty} \, (Z^3\,-\,3\, Z)^2 \,p(Z) \,dZ}} } [$] 

[$]=\Large{ \, \frac{ 6 \, (1\,-\, \Phi(Z_k)) + \phi(Z_k) \, \Large[  18\, H_1(Z_k) \, +\, 9\, H_3(Z_k) +\,  H_5(Z_k)  \Large]}{6\,\sqrt{  (6 \, (1\,-\, \Phi(Z_k)) + \phi(Z_k) \, \Large[  18\, H_1(Z_k) \, +\, 9\, H_3(Z_k) +\,  H_5(Z_k)  \Large]}}}[$]

[$]=\Large{ \, \frac{ \sqrt{ (6 \, (1\,-\, \Phi(Z_k)) + \phi(Z_k) \, \Large[  18\, H_1(Z_k) \, +\, 9\, H_3(Z_k) +\,  H_5(Z_k)  \Large]}}{6\,}}[$]

We can find these correlations between hermite polynomials of any underlying asset/option with other options on the same underlying and also (using correlations between two assets) between two options with different underlying assets.

Friends, on a risk neutral measure, the hermite series of terminal distribution of asset random variable at terminal time T is given as

[$] X(t, T) \, = \,x_0 \, \exp(r\,(T-t)) + a_1 \, H_1(Z)\, +\,  a_2 \,H_2(Z) \, + \, a_3 \, H_3(Z)[$]

while terminal distribution of a call price is given as

[$]V_x(Z)\,=\, \begin{cases}
(\, \,x_0 \, \exp(r\,(T-t))\,- k\,) + \, a_1\, Z\,+ \, a_2\, (Z^2-1)\, + \, a_3 \, (Z^3\,-\,3\,Z)\, , & \text{ if } Z \geq Z_k \\
0,   & \text{ otherwise }
\end{cases}  [$]
In the above equation [$]Z_k[$] represents the value of Z at which asset equals the value of strike X(Z)=k. or in other words [$]X(Z_k)=k[$]

I need to clarify this first since I had not been correct in some of the recent earlier posts. We can use risk neutral pricing argument only due to delta hedging which mitigates the option value risk due to movements of the spot asset. Our underlying asset can be assumed to grow at the riskless rate only due to delta hedging argument 
There are other different types of hedging. For example in spirit of vega hedging, we can hedge against the changes in hermite series coefficients, [$]a_1[$], [$]a_2[$] and a [$]a_3[$] of the terminal distribution. 
We can also decrease the variance of any of(or some combination, or all of) the hermite polynomials thus altering the effective variance of the risk neutral portfolio of the derivative and its hedge instruments. 
So there is delta hedging that ensures that our portfolio is immune to small changes in spot. Then there is vega hedging that makes the portfolio immune to changes in the hermite series coefficients or alternatively immune to changes in different modes of cumulative variance till maturity. Then there could be other types of hedging that could altogether alter the variance of the portfolio. We could also mitigate a multi-option portfolio against changes in variance of some select stocks or we could go one step further and make our portfolio totally insensitive to variance of some stocks/components. 

We will continue to explore all of this along with correlations but first I want to discuss delta hedging in our framework. I had earlier given a wrong formula for delta hedging a few posts earlier in which I hedged for the first hermite polynomial and erroneously called it delta hedging.
Here is the right formula.
First expected value of call option is given without interest rates(we will cover all complete formulas very soon) as

[$]\,E[V_x\,]=\, \int\limits_{Z_k}^{\infty}\, \Big[ \,(\,x_0 \, \,- k\,) + \, a_1\, Z\,+ \, a_2\, (Z^2-1)\, + \, a_3 \, (Z^3\,-\,3\,Z) \Big] \, p(Z) \, dZ\,[$] 
or simplifying
[$]\,E[V_x\,]=\, \int\limits_{Z_k}^{\infty}\, g(x_0,Z) \, p(Z) \, dZ\,[$]
 
By Liebniz Rule, we can right the derivative of above integral with respect to [$]x_0[$] as

[$]\Large{\frac{d[E[V_x]]}{dx_0}\, = \, - g(x_0,Z_k)\, \frac{dZ_k}{dx_0} \, + \, \, \int\limits_{Z_k}^{\infty}\, \frac{dg(x_0,Z)}{dx_0} \, p(Z) \, dZ\,}[$]

To calculate  [$]\frac{dZ_k}{dx_0} \,[$], we first write the hermite representation of the asset as

[$] X(t, T) \, = \,x_0 \, + a_1 \, H_1(Z)\, +\,  a_2 \,H_2(Z) \, + \, a_3 \, H_3(Z)[$]

We know that [$]Z_k] is value of Z for which X(Z) is equal to strike, K. So

[$] k \, = \,x_0 \, + a_1 \, H_1(Z_k)\, +\,  a_2 \,H_2(Z_k) \, + \, a_3 \, H_3(Z_k)[$]

Differentiating above equation with respect to [$]x_0[$], we get

[$]\, 0\,=\, 1\,+ a_1\, \frac{dZ_k}{dx_0}\, + \,a_2\, 2\, Z_k\, \frac{dZ_k}{dx_0}\,+\, a_3\, (3\, {Z_k}^2\, -\, 3\,) \frac{dZ_k}{dx_0}\,[$] 

or 

[$]\Large{\, \frac{dZ_k}{dx_0}\,=\, \frac{-1}{a_1\, +\,2\,  a_2\, Z_k \, + \,   a_3\, (3\, {Z_k}^2\, -\, 3\,)}}[$]

So formula for our delta becomes

[$]\Large{\frac{d[E[V_x]]}{dx_0}\, = \, - g(x_0,Z_k)\, \frac{dZ_k}{dx_0} \, + \, \, \int\limits_{Z_k}^{\infty}\, \frac{dg(x_0,Z)}{dx_0} \, p(Z) \, dZ\,}[$]


[$]= \large{\, -  \Big[ \, \big[(\,x_0 \, \,- k\,) + \, a_1\, {Z_k}\,+ \, a_2\, ({Z_k}^2-1)\, + \, a_3 \, ({Z_k}^3\,-\,3\,Z_k) \big] \, \phi(Z_k)\Big]\,} \Large{ \Big[\, \frac{-1}{a_1\, +\,2\,  a_2\, Z_k \, + \,   a_3\, (3\, {Z_k}^2\, -\, 3\,)}  \Big] \,} \\
+\large{\, \big[1\, -\, \Phi(Z_k) \big]\,}[$]

Friends, in this post we construct a portfolio of an exotic derivative and its hedge instruments and calculate the amount of hedge assets required to make the total portfolio immune to small changes in spot underlying asset and changes in hermite series coefficients(volatility coefficients) of the underlying asset.
We take unit amount of exotic derivative and k0 of underlying asset and [$]k_1[$], [$]k_2[$], and [$]k_3[$] of options V1,V2 and V3.

Now our portfolio is given as

[$]\Pi\, =\, V_x\, + \, k_0 \, X \, + \, k_1\, V_1\,+ \, k_2\, V_2\,+ \, k_3\, V_3\,[$] 

The condition to make the portfolio immune to changes in spot asset is given as

[$]\, \Large{ \frac{\partial\Pi}{\partial x_0}\, =\, \frac{\partial V_x}{\partial x_0}\, + \, k_0 \, \frac{\partial X}{\partial x_0}\, + \, k_1\, \frac{\partial V1}{\partial x_0}\,+ \, k_2\, \, \frac{\partial V_2}{\partial x_0}\,+ \, k_3\,\, \frac{\partial V_3}{\partial x_0}\,=\,0}[$] 

The condition to make the portfolio immune to changes in coefficients of 1st hermite polynomial of distribution of  asset X(t,T) is given as

[$]\, \Large{\frac{\partial\Pi}{\partial c_1}\, =\, \frac{\partial V_x}{\partial c_1}\, + \, k_0 \, \frac{\partial X}{\partial c1}\, + \, k_1\, \frac{\partial V1}{\partial c_1}\,+ \, k_2\, \, \frac{\partial V_2}{\partial c_1}\,+ \, k_3\,\, \frac{\partial V_3}{\partial c_1}\,\,=\,0}[$] 

Similarly the next two conditoins to make the portfolio immune to small changes in [$]c_2[$] and [$]c_3[$] as 

[$]\, \Large{\frac{\partial\Pi}{\partial c_2}\, =\, \frac{\partial V_x}{\partial c_2}\, + \, k_0 \, \frac{\partial X}{\partial c_2}\, + \, k_1\, \frac{\partial V1}{\partial c_2}\,+ \, k_2\, \, \frac{\partial V_2}{\partial c_2}\,+ \, k_3\,\, \frac{\partial V_3}{\partial c_2}\,\,=\,0}[$] 

[$]\, \Large{\frac{\partial\Pi}{\partial c_3}\, =\, \frac{\partial V_x}{\partial c_3}\, + \, k_0 \, \frac{\partial X}{\partial c3}\, + \, k_1\, \frac{\partial V1}{\partial c_3}\,+ \, k_2\, \, \frac{\partial V_2}{\partial c_3}\,+ \, k_3\,\, \frac{\partial V_3}{\partial c_3}\,\,=\,0}[$]
