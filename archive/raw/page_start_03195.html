Friends, here I want to explain some of what I had suggested in a previous post. I had explained most of it post 2928 a few months earlier.

Basically, I am trying to explain how we can re-expand a Z-series around some other point \(Z_y=Z_{Y_0}\) as opposed to its natural expansion around its median \(Z_y=0\). In the context of our problem where density of Y has to be found conditional on several explaining variables, we would like to expand the Z-series of Y around the conditional median given the explaining variables. Please note that this conditional median is slightly different from  conditional mean given explaining variables.

Suppose we have a Z-series of Y expanded around its median \(Y_{\text{median}}\), where \(Z_y=0\) and we want to expand this Z-series around some other forward point \(Y_0\) so that \(Y(Z_{Y_0})=Y_0\). The coefficients of this new Z-series expanded around \(Z_y=Z_{Y_0}\) would be different from its coefficients when expanded around \(Z_y=0\). Let us go through the steps to make this conversion.

Suppose we have a Hermite-series of random variable Y given as

\[Y(Z_y)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,H_n(Z_y)\]

We suppose its equivalent Z-series of Y is given as

\[Y(Z_y)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,\tilde{a}_n\,{Z_y}^n\]


Suppose we want to find to expand the above Z-series around  \(Y\,=\,Y_0\). At \(Y_0\) the value of \(Z_y\) is \(Z_{Y_0}\). We take the derivatives of Z-series at \(Z_{Y_0}\) and re-expand the Z-series as a Taylor series around  \(Z_{Y_0}\) using derivatives calculated there. 

Taking above Example of the Z-series

\[Y(Z_y)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,\tilde{a}_n\,{Z_y}^n\]

Taking conditional value at \(Z_{Y_0}\), we have

\[Y_0\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,\tilde{a}_n\,{Z_{Y_0}}^n\]

The value of first derivative at \(Z_{Y_0}\) is given as

\[\frac{dY}{dZ}\,|^{\,\left( Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\,\right)}\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N-1}\,(n+1)\,\tilde{a}_{n+1} \,\,{Z_{Y_0}}^n\]

Similarly

\[\frac{d^2Y}{dZ^2}\,|^{\,\left(\, Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\,\right)}\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N-2}\,(n+1)\,(n+2)\,\tilde{a}_{n+2} \,\,{Z_{Y_0}}^n\]

and mth derivative is given as

\[\frac{d^mY}{dZ^m}\,|^{\,\left(\, Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\,\right)}\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N-m}\,(n+1)\,(n+2)\,\ldots\,(n+m)\,\tilde{a}_{n+m} \,\,{Z_{Y_0}}^n\]

We use above derivatives to form a local Taylor series around \(Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\) as
\begin{align}
Y(Z_y\,-\,Z_{Y_0})\,&=\,Y_0\,+\,\left[\frac{dY}{dZ}\,|^{\,\left( Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\,\right)}\,\right]\,\left(Z_y\,-Z_{Y_0}\right) \\
&+\,\frac{1}{2}\,\left[\frac{d^2Y}{dZ^2}\,|^{\,\left( Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\,\right)}\,\right]\,{\left(Z_y\,-Z_{Y_0}\right)}^2 \\
&+\,\frac{1}{6}\,\left[\frac{d^3Y}{dZ^3}\,|^{\,\left( Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\,\right)}\,\right]\,{\left(Z_y\,-Z_{Y_0}\right)}^3 \\
&+\,\ldots \\
&+\,\frac{1}{N!}\,\left[\frac{d^NY}{dZ^N}\,|^{\,\left( Z_Y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\,\right)}\,\right]\,{\left(Z_y\,-Z_{Y_0}\right)}^N \\
\end{align}

We convert the above Taylor to our familiar  Z-series by using

\begin{align}
\tilde{c}_0\,&=\,Y_0\,\\
\tilde{c}_1\,&=\left[\frac{dY}{dZ}\,|^{\,\left( Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\,\right)}\,\right]\, \\
\tilde{c}_2\,&=\frac{1}{2}\,\left[\frac{d^2Y}{dZ^2}\,|^{\,\left( Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\,\right)}\,\right]\, \\
\tilde{c}_3\,&=\,\frac{1}{6}\,\left[\frac{d^3Y}{dZ^3}\,|^{\,\left( Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\,\right)}\,\right]\, \\
\,\ldots \\
\tilde{c}_N\,&=\frac{1}{N!}\,\left[\frac{d^NY}{dZ^N}\,|^{\,\left( Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\,\right)}\,\right]\, \\
\end{align}

and get our new Z-series as

\[Y(Z_y-Z_{Y_0})\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,\tilde{c}_n\,{(Z_y\,-Z_{Y_0})}^n\]

We do a change of variable and define \(Z\,=(Z_y-Z_{Y_0})\) to get

\[Y(Z)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,\tilde{c}_n\,{Z}^n\]


The conditional or local moments of the value \( Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\,\) can be found as the expectation of the Z-Series below

\[\,E\left[ \,Y^m\,|\,Y\,=\,Y_{0}\right]\,=\,E\left[ \,Y^m\,|\,Z_y\,=\,Z_{Y_0}\right]\,=E\left[\left(\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,\tilde{c}_n\,{Z}^n\right)^m\right]\] where Z in above Z-series is a standard normal random variable.

For example if the associated equivalent hermite-series is given as

\[Y(Z)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,c_n\,H_n(Z)\]

then we can automatically calculate the conditional second moment as

\[E\left[Y^2|(Y\,=Y_0)\right]\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,c_n^2\,n!\]

and variance would be given as (just omitting the zeroth index term)

\[E\left[Var(Y)|(Y\,=Y_0)\right]\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,c_n^2\,n!\]

Friends, our current working example under research is about finding the conditional density of sum of a number (in our case three) of SDE random variables. When I started out on work, I was thinking more of correlated random variables like they appear in finance. But SDEs were a simple way to find conditional density through transition densities. Then myself and a lot of friends also realized that summation of random variables and finding the conditional density of sum was a very interesting example and many new ideas in stochastics would possibly open later if we could do this example right.

However, we continued with the earlier infrastructure of correlated variables but now I think that if mathematical structure of correlated variables is known, it has to be used and information has to be extracted out of it.

Again coming to our sum of SDE random variables set up, in our example, we suppose that there are three correlated SDE variables, \(X_1\), \(X_2\) and \(X_3\). We suppose that we have joint data of values of  \(X_1(t_1)\), \(X_2(t_1)\) and \(X_3(t_1)\) is known at a certain forward time \(t_1>0\). We also suppose that we also have have joint data of values of  \(X_1(t_2)\), \(X_2(t_2)\) and \(X_3(t_2)\) at another forward time \(t_2>t_1>0\). this data is in form of path by path realizations as it appears usually in monte carlo simulations of SDEs of  \(X_1\), \(X_2\) and \(X_3\). We suppose that \[Y(t_2)\,=\,X_1(t_2)\,+\,X_2(t_2)\,+\,X_3(t_2)\]

 Our task is to find given above data, the conditional density of \(Y(t_2)\) given arbitrary starting values of correlated random variables  \(X_1(t_1)\), \(X_2(t_1)\) and \(X_3(t_1)\). We write the conditional density by the follwing notation

\[P(Y(t_2)| (X_1(t_1)=x_1, X_2(t_1)=x_2, X_3(t_1)=x_3)\]

This was the context. I want to say that we have taken forward our work from correlation problem but in the current example we possibly should exploit the structure of the problem.

We have that 

\[Y(t_2)\,=\,X_1(t_2)\,+\,X_2(t_2)\,+\,X_3(t_2)\]

I want to write 

\[V(t_2)\,=\,Y(t_2)\,-\left[X_1(t_1)\,+\,X_2(t_1)\,+\,X_3(t_1)\right]\]

since the value of \(X_1(t_1)\,+\,X_2(t_1)\,+\,X_3(t_1)\) is known directly from the conditioning variables, I suppose that working with the analytics of \(V(t_2)\) should be enough to reconstruct the density of \(Y(t_2)\).

Instead of expanding the density of Y as a function of \(Z_{x_1},\,\,Z_{x_2},\,\,Z_{x_3},\,\text{and}\, Z_{\tilde{y}}\), I suppose we should try to expand V as a function of \(Z_y,\,\,Z_{x_1},\,\,Z_{x_2},\,\,Z_{x_3},\,\text{and}\, Z_{\tilde{v}}\).

We were expanding

\[{Z_y}^m\,=\,{(\rho_{m,1}\,Z_{x_1}\,+\,\rho_{m,2}\,Z_{x_2}\,+\,\rho_{m,3}\,Z_{x_3}\,+\,\zeta_{m}\,Z_{\tilde{y}})}^m\]

where values of \(Z_{x_1}\). \(Z_{x_2}\) and \(Z_{x_3}\) are all known and density expansion is done in \(Z_{\tilde{y}}\). But I suppose we should expand as

\[{Z_v}^m\,=\,{(\rho_{m,1}\,Z_{x_1}\,+\,\rho_{m,2}\,Z_{x_2}\,+\,\rho_{m,3}\,Z_{x_3}\,+\,\rho_{m,4}\,Z_{y}\,+\,\,\zeta_{m}\,Z_{\tilde{v}}\, )}^m\]

where \(Z_{y}\) in above equation is calculated/backed out after finding the conditional expected value Y from a linear regression. So  values of \(Z_{x_1}\). \(Z_{x_2}\), \(Z_{x_3}\) and \(Z_y\) are all known and density expansion is done in \(Z_{\tilde{v}}\)

function [Z]=CalculateZgivenXAndHermiteSeriesBisection2(Y,coeff,median,Hz,Order)

%This program takes a value of Y as input and returns the corresponding 
%value of Z given the parameters of the Z-series.
%It first see if the value of Y is greater than median or smaller than
%median. If the value of Y is greater than median Z has to be greater than
%zero and if the value of Y is smaller than median Z has to be negative.
% Once we know whether Z is greater than Z or smaller than Zero, we divide
% the program into two repeated if loops, one for Z greater than zero and 
%one for Z smaller than zero. 
%If Z is positive, we have to assign it a valid band of upper and
%lower values between which it can be found. 
%This is done sequentially by calculating Y(.5) at Z=.5, if Y is smaller 
%than Y(.5), we know that true value of Y will lie between 0 and .5
%so we assign a lower band value Za=0 and upper band value Zb=.5
%If however Y is greater than Y(.5), we know that true Z should be larger 
%than .5 and we again evaluate Y(1) at Z=1, If Y is smaller than Y(1), 
%we know that true value of Z should be between .5 and 1, and we assign a 
%lower band value Za=.5 and upper band value of Zb=1. Through repeated 
%elseif we continue until we find an appropriate band value of Z.

%Once we have determined the upper band value of Zb and lower band value of
%Za, We find Zmid=(Za+Zb)/2. We then evaluate Y(Zmid) and if Y < Y(Zmid) we
%move the upper band to Zb=Zmid while keeping Za at older value. However if
% Y> Y(Zmid), we assign the lower band value equal to Zmid as Za=Zmid. In a
% few iterations, this bisection procedure gets extremely close to true
% value of Z for which Y(Z)=Y
%EvalHermiteSeriesH0(Coeff,Z,Order,Hz)
YdefectBest=Y-coeff(1);
%Z=(Y-coeff(1))/coeff(2);
ZtryBest=0.0;
BracketSize=.5;
if(Y>=median)
    Ztry=.5;
    ZtryBefore=0;
    NotBracketed=1;
    while((NotBracketed)&&(Ztry<=5))
        [Ytry,dYtrydZ]= EvalHermiteSeriesH0(coeff,Ztry,Order,Hz); 
        if((Y<= Ytry))
            Za=ZtryBefore;
            Zb=Ztry;
            NotBracketed=0;
            Ydefect=Y-Ytry;
            if(abs(Ydefect)<abs(YdefectBest))
            ZtryBest=Ztry;
            YdefectBest=Ydefect;
            end
        end
        ZtryBefore=Ztry;
        Ztry=Ztry+BracketSize;
    end
    if(NotBracketed==1)
        Za=5;
        Zb=6.5;
    end
    for mm=1:20
        Zmid=.5*Za+.5*Zb;
        [Ymid,dYmiddZ]= EvalHermiteSeriesH0(coeff,Zmid,Order,Hz);
        Ydefect=Y-Ymid;
            if(abs(Ydefect)<abs(YdefectBest))
            ZtryBest=Zmid;
            YdefectBest=Ydefect;
            end
        if((Y< Ymid)|| (dYmiddZ<0))
            Zb=Zmid;
        elseif(Y> Ymid)
            Za=Zmid;
        end
    Z=.5*Za+.5*Zb; %This can be taken out of the loop and does not 
    %necessarily have to be calculated again and again. Has to be
    %calculated at least once after the loop to determine the true value of
    %Z.
    
    end
    Z=ZtryBest;
    
end


if(Y<median)
    Ztry=-.5;
    ZtryBefore=0;
    NotBracketed=1;
    while((NotBracketed)&&(Ztry>=-5))
        [Ytry,dYtrydZ]= EvalHermiteSeriesH0(coeff,Ztry,Order,Hz); 
        if((Y>= Ytry))
            Za=Ztry;
            Zb=ZtryBefore;
            NotBracketed=0;
             Ydefect=Y-Ytry;
            if(abs(Ydefect)<abs(YdefectBest))
            ZtryBest=Ztry;
            YdefectBest=Ydefect;
            end
        end
        ZtryBefore=Ztry;
        Ztry=Ztry-BracketSize;
    end
    if(NotBracketed==1)
        Za=-6.5;
        Zb=-5;
    end
    for mm=1:20
        Zmid=.5*Za+.5*Zb;
        [Ymid,dYmiddZ]= EvalHermiteSeriesH0(coeff,Zmid,Order,Hz);
        Ydefect=Y-Ymid;
        if(abs(Ydefect)<abs(YdefectBest))
            ZtryBest=Zmid;
            YdefectBest=Ydefect;
        end
        if((Y> Ymid)|| (dYmiddZ<0))
            Za=Zmid;
        elseif(Y<  Ymid)
            Zb=Zmid;
        end
    Z=.5*Za+.5*Zb; %This can be taken out of the loop and does not 
    %necessarily have to be calculated again and again. Has to be
    %calculated at least once after the loop to determine the true value of

    end
    Z=ZtryBest;
end
