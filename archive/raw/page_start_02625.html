Further brainstorming about market dynamics, I could come back to the idea behind mean-reversion towards the market again. The whole idea is that information is constantly arriving in the market in the form of news and other trading activity. This arrival of information is most reflected in the stocks whose trading volume sharply increases the average mean trading volume (increased volume in the whole market is similarly also synonymous with increased information in the market). So arrival of information flows from the stocks whose volume abnormally increases and bellwether stocks and rest of the stocks respond to this information. Again rest of the stocks have to follow the information stocks with increased volume and the bellwether stocks and broader market absorbs the arrival of information into it and reacts appropriately. Arrival of information is a forcing term in the dynamics of all other stocks in the market that continues to alter the direction of market from time to time.

Combining these new (for me) ideas with the ideas in the copied post, I think the problem is to find out given all the information in the market if we can project the existing market environment variables related to information leading stocks and the bellwether stocks on to a single (but suitable) time step ahead expected distribution of the market or one step expected transition probabilities of the market based on the present value of market environment variables and the information in the market. If we can indeed make this one suitable (small or large depending upon the problem) time step ahead projection of the expected distribution of the leading information stocks and bellwether stocks in the market (or the broader market in other words), we can then more easily solve the related simple problem about when to allow a trade to live into the future and when to end it given the trade has already been opened in the past.

A simple non-quant way of putting it would be that new trades are opened with the arrival of new information in the market and are expected to last as long as the market continues to absorb the effect of this arrival of new information. However to put all of this in a quantitative and technical framework is what we need to do in next few days or weeks. Friends, I want to tell something interesting from the past. Those friends who had been following me for several years would easily recall that I was claiming of another interesting method to trade stocks and crypto currencies that worked very well and I had not shared it with anyone at all. I was able to explore that method for trading financial securities even before I had worked on hermite regression. That was just an intuitive and non-rigorous method that exploited correlations. Even though, we can calculate correlations of a security with a large number of securities, it is not easily clear how to turn these correlations into actionable signals and insights. Doing a least square regression in the standard way works relatively poorly. What I was able to explore was to turn the log-returns of various securities and find partial correlation between them. Partial correlation controls for the effect of all other securities on both assets between which partial correlation is being found. As it turns out, especially as we are working with returns that are already normalized in a way, we could multiply partial correlation coefficients (between one asset at next step and all other assets at previous set --- as in autocorrelation sense)  of previous returns (with the associated past returns themselves) and linearly add them to find a trading signal of size and direction of next return to be predicted. This gave a signal an order of magnitude better than a plain least square regression. We all know that correlations cannot be added linearly at all but in some special cases partial correlations can be added to each other since the effect of all other securities have been removed/controlled from both assets whose partial correlation is being calculated.

Friends, I have some difficulty in making a hermite polynomial series version of partial correlations so I am putting it for a longer term project. However, I have something else interesting for friends. We have been trying to know if the market or a stock is locally mean-reverting or trending i.e. whether its increments have negative correlation or positive correlation between them. 

A very simple statistic that can tell us whether a stock is locally negative correlation or positive correlation can be given as follows. A stock has locally negative correlation if 

\[{\left[\sum\limits_{n=1}^{n=Lag}\, \Delta{X_n} \,\right]}^2\, \lt \sum\limits_{n=1}^{n=Lag}\, \left[\Delta{X_n} \right]^2 \]

Similalry A stock has locally positive correlation if 

\[{\left[\sum\limits_{n=1}^{n=Lag}\, \Delta{X_n} \,\right]}^2\, \gt \sum\limits_{n=1}^{n=Lag}\, \left[\Delta{X_n} \right]^2 \]

We can develop a statistic that gives us local strength of correlation for a single stock given by index m as

\[IS_{\rho}^{m}\,=\,\frac{{\left[\sum\limits_{n=1}^{n=Lag}\, \Delta{X_n} \,\right]}^2\,}{  \sum\limits_{n=1}^{n=Lag}\, \left[\Delta{X_n} \right]^2} \]

We can sum the value of above indicator over M market stocks with suitable weights \(w_m\) to find out the value of indicator for the market as

\[IM_{\rho}\,=\, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{m=M}\,w_m\, IS_{\rho}^m\,  \]

This can easily tell us if the market is locally mean-reverting with negative correlation between successive time intervals or trending with positive correlation between successive time intervals.

Two Dimensional Z-Series/Hermite-Series: Introduction to Two Dimensional Joint And Conditional Gaussian Series of Non-Gaussian Continuous Random variables


Suppose we have two non-Gaussian continuous random variables, X and Y and we can make observations of their joint realizations. We can calculate the univariate Z-series/Hermite-series of X and Y respectively as

\[\,X(Z_x)\,=\, ah_0 \, +\, ah_1 \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, ah_2 \,H_2(Z_x) \,+\, ah_3 \,H_3(Z_x) \,+\,\ldots\] 
\[\,Y(Z_y)\,=\, bh_0 \, +\, bh_1 \,H_1(Z_y) \,+\, bh_2 \,H_2(Z_y) \,+\, bh_3 \,H_3(Z_y) \,+\,\ldots \] 

Bivariate Joint Hermite-Series of X and Y is denoted by a random variable W in the form of a product (of joint realizations of X and Y) and is given as (please note that \(aH_{m,n}\) are merely numerical coefficients while \(H_n(.)\) are hermite polynomials)

\begin{align}

W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, = \, X\, Y\,&=\, \Big[\, aH_{0,0} \, + \, aH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, aH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, aH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, aH_{1,0} \, + \, aH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, aH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, aH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, aH_{2,0} \, + \, aH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, aH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, aH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

The bivariate conditional Hermite-Series of X conditional on Y is given as (please note that \(bH_{m,n}\) are numerical coefficients)

\begin{align}
\left[X|Y\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \,&=\, \Big[\, bH_{0,0} \, + \, bH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, bH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, bH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, bH_{1,0} \, + \, bH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, bH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, bH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, bH_{2,0} \, + \, bH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, bH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, bH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

Similarly, the bivariate conditional Hermite-Series of Y conditional on X is given as (please note that \(cH_{m,n}\) are numerical coefficients)

\begin{align}
\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \,&=\, \Big[\, cH_{0,0} \, + \, cH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, cH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{1,0} \, + \, cH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, cH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{2,0} \, + \, cH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, cH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

Please note that  \(W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y),\,\left[X|Y\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \text{and}\, \left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\) are neither probability distributions or expectations, rather they are Bivariate Hermite-series with associated probability distributions and expectations.

However, Bayes law should perfectly apply and we should have 
\[ \,W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, =\,\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\,X(Z_x)\,\]
\[ \,W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, =\,\left[X|Y\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\,Y(Z_y)\,\]

Similarly, we should be able to retrieve one dimensional marginal Hermite-Series from Bivariate joint Hermite-Series after integrating over the second variable.

\[\,Y(Z_y)\,=\,\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\, \,W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, dZ_x\]
\[\,X(Z_x)\,=\,\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\, \,W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, dZ_y\]
In the next post, we discuss numerical procedure for estimation of  Bivariate joint Hermite-Series of X and Y given by  \(W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y),\,\), Bivariate conditional hermite-series of X conditional on Y given by \(\left[X|Y\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \) and bivariate conditional Hermite-Series of Y conditional on X given by  \(\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\)  given the joint observations of X and Y.

Estimation procedure for Two Dimensional Joint or Conditional Hermite-Series/Z-Series From Joint Observation Data of Two Random Variables

Suppose we have P joint observations of random variables X and Y. We represent \(\text{pth}\) observations of X as \(X_p\) and  \(\text{pth}\) observations of Y as \(Y_p\). We would sort the values of X and Y independently and map each of the observations of   \(X_p\) and \(Y_p\)independently on two independent discretized Gaussian distributions separately(analogously to what we did for one estimation of one dimensional Z-series in section 3.2 of my paper: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm ... id=4933283 ).
Using the sort and mapping each variable's data independently on respective univariate discretized Gaussian density, we associate a unique value of discretized Gaussian \(Z_{X_p}\) to each observed value of \(X_p\) and similarly but independently, we associate a value of univariate discretized Gaussian \(Z_{Y_p}\) to each observed value of \(Y_p\)

For estimation of joint bivariate Hermite-Series of X and Y given by \(W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, = \, X\, Y\), we set up the regression for each observation

\[\,X_p\,Y_p\,=\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, aH_{n,m}\,H_m(Z_{X_p})\,H_n(Z_{Y_p})\] 

The values \(X_p\) and \(Y_p\) on LHS are known for each observation while values of  \(,H_m(Z_{X_p}) \) and \(\,H_n(Z_{Y_p})\) on RHS are known for each observation and we are only left with finding \(aH_{n,m}\) which have to be found from least squares linear regression.

Similarly for estimation of conditional bivariate Hermite-Series of X conditional on Y, given as \(\left[X|Y\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\),  we would do the regression (in which RHS known hermite variables would be exactly the same but coefficients to be determioned would be diffferent) as

\[\,X_p\,=\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, bH_{n,m}\,H_m(Z_{X_p})\,H_n(Z_{Y_p})\] 

We have to find \(bH_{n,m}\) from above regression.

Again for estimation of conditional bivariate Hermite-Series of Y conditional on X, given as \(\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\),  we would do the regression with equation of pth observation as


\[\,Y_p\,=\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, cH_{n,m}\,H_m(Z_{X_p})\,H_n(Z_{Y_p})\] 

We have to find \(cH_{n,m}\) from this regression.
This way, we can easily calculate the bivariate joint and conditional Hermite-series of two random variables given the joint data for the two random variables.
reference: section 3.2 of my paper, https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm ... id=4933283 

This can easily be extended to more dimensions and we could easily find conditional Hermite-series of a random variable conditional on realizations of several other jointly distributed random variables.

Please note that preliminary conditions on first derivatives of both distributions of X and Y required for our sort and map on discretized Gaussian density must hold. For details, please see the referenced paper above.

Friends, regressions given in post 2637 are perfectly right. Only thing we need to determine is how to associate \(Z_{x_p}\) with \(X\) and \(Z_{y_p}\) with \(Y\). Once we have properly made this association, our regressions would be exactly the same as in post 2637.

Let us try again.

In order to associate data observations of first variable \(X_p\) with its underlying Gaussian, \(Z_{X_p}\), we simply sort the observations \(X_p\) and associate them with discretized Gaussian density \(Z_{X_p}\) just as we did for univariate densities.

Now we are left with associating the observations of second variable \(Y_p\) with \(Z_{Y_p}\). This is more difficult.

First we decorrelate the observations \(Y_p\) by removing the effect of \(X_p\). For this we will have to remove the correlated hermite polynomials of \(Z_{X_p}\). This can also be done by a univariate regression of the observations \(Y_p\) on hermite polynomials of joint observations of \(X_p\) given by \(H_n(Z_{X_p})\). Suppose decorrelated (and de-meaned) observations are denoted as \(\bar{Y}_p\). These observations would have zero mean. 

In order to find dependence structure of variance of observations \(\bar{Y}_p\) on \(Z_{X_p}\), we regress \({ \bar{Y}_p}^2\) on hermite polynomials of \(Z_{X_p}\) along with a constant. We call this variance regression as  \(Var\left[\bar{Y}\right](Z_{X_p})\) which would be an expression in hermite polynomials of  \(Z_{X_p}\).

Now  the decorrelated \(\bar{Y}\), standardized by square root of variance \(Var\left[\bar{Y}\right](Z_{X_p}) \), should be distributed in an increasing order i.e. the standardized values  \(\frac{\bar{Y}}{\sqrt{Var\left[\bar{Y}\right](Z_{X_p})}}\) could be sorted and assigned to discretized density of \(Z_{Y_p}\) in increasing order.
