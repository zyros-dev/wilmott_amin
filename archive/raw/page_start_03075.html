Friends, I was thinking of other possible improvements on the basic Hermite regression algorithm so it works better even away from the mean of the density. Suppose we use the following notation

\[Y(Z_y)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,ah_n\,H_n(Z_y)\,=\,\,\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,Y_n(Z_y)\,\]  and
\[X(Z_x)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,bh_n\,H_n(Z_x)\,=\,\,\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,X_n(Z_x)\,\]

basically in a univariate regression, we regress each of the \(Y_n\) on same polynomial part \(X_n\) and this regression can be written as (Equation 1)

\[Y_n\,=\,ah_n\,\rho_n\,H_n(Z_x)\]

but for problems away from mean, we can have an improved estimator as

\[Y_n\,=\,ah_n\, \left(\rho_{0,n}\,+\,\rho_{1,n}\,H_1(Z_x)+\,\rho_{2,n}\,H_2(Z_x)\right)\,H_n(Z_x)\]

effectively we replace the point estimate of correlation \(\rho_n\) by a series estimate of correlation given as \(\left(\rho_{0,n}\,+\,\rho_{1,n}\,H_1(Z_x)+\,\rho_{2,n}\,H_2(Z_x)\right)\,\). The importance of this series correlation estimator would arise more so in multivariate regressions. 

In order to estimate the above series estiamtor, we could minimize a simple least square calculation of the form given below. If there are P instances of data 

\[\sum\limits_{p=1}^{P}\,{\Big[Y_{n,p}\,-\,\,ah_n\, \left(\rho_{0,n}\,+\,\rho_{1,n}\,H_1(Z_{x,p})+\,\rho_{2,n}\,H_2(Z_{x,p})\right)\,H_n(Z_{x,p})\Big]}^2\]

where we minimize over the correlation parameters of the form \(\rho_{0,n}\,\), \(\,\rho_{1,n}\,\) and \(\,\rho_{2,n}\). Since we know the starting value of \(\rho_{0,n}\,=\,\rho_n\) and \(\,\rho_{1,n}\,=0\) and \(\,\rho_{2,n}=0\), we have to improve upon these starting values, it should not be an extremely difficult problem. I suppose with arrival of quantum computing, we should be solving many of such multivariate problems with similar optimizations.

Friends, very brief review of logic of the new  program. We have different correlation matrices for seven different hermite polynomials of explaining variable Xn's. When we orthogonalize each of them, we have seven eigenvector basis (C1-C7) and one original basis (C0). In our original algorithm to calculate correlation coefficients of fifth and lower order terms, we have to add terms from higher binomial expansions. but these higher order terms are in different basis. We first convert the higher order terms from nth basis  in zero basis(C0) and then re-convert them to mth basis Cm (where m<n) and after conversion of the higher order basis terms into mth basis, these terms are directly added/subtracted to other terms already in mth basis.

Second difference is in calculation of \(Z_{n_0}\) which is the delta realized values of nth \(Z_{x_n}\) on which to condition. We first calculate all \(Z_{n_0}\) as before from inversion of Z-series of nth explaining variable and then turn them into hermite polynomial of mth basis(this requires conversion from C0 to Cm basis) to get the delta realized value of mth hermite in Cm. We then invert these hermite polynomial values to find the corresponding  \(\tilde{Z}_{n_0}\) (which are slightly different from original  \(Z_{n_0}\) and then use weighted sum of these \(\tilde{Z}_{n_0}\) as final values in the binomial expansion. In my matlab program, this inversion is still ad-hoc and more work is needed to definitively settle this part but I decided to post the program as this works reasonably well.
