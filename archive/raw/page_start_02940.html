The purpose of this post is to describe research towards finding the conditional moments of Y given X. Once we find these conditional moments, we can use them to construct the 1D conditional Z-series of Y given X. First some preliminaries for orientation purpose.

1D Hermite series of Y and X is given as

\[Y(Z_y)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,H_n(Z_y)\]
\[X(Z_x)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,b_n\,H_n(Z_x)\]

 and correlation between respective hermite polynomials of nth order (when total data count is P) is given as


\begin{equation}\label{4.1.5}
\rho_{n} \, = \,  \frac{\sum\limits_{p=1}^{P}\, H_n(Z_{x,p}) \, \, H_n(Z_{y,p}) \,}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{p=1}^{P} \, {H_n(Z_{x,p})}^2 \,\sum\limits_{p=1}^{P} \, \, {H_n(Z_{y,p})}^2 \,}}
\end{equation}

The general conditional value of Y given X is given as

\[\left[Y|X\right]\,=\,\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,\rho_n\,  H_n(Z_x) \]

We can take conditional expected value of Y given a certain realization of X as
\[E\left[\left[Y|X\right]|X=X_0\right]\,=\,\,\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,\rho_n\,  H_n(Z_x) \right)| Z_x=Z_{x_0}\,\]

and expected value of mth moment of Y given X is calculated by method of local Taylor described in previous post. We re-expand the Z-series of \(\left[Y|X\right]\) as a function of \(Z_x\) as a Taylor series around \(Z_x\,=\,Z_{x_0}\) and then use coefficients of this new Taylor series to calculate the conditional moments of \(\left[Y\,|\,X\right]\) given by notation \(E\left[{\left[Y|X\right]}^m|X=X_0\right]\,\)

Please note that \(E\left[\left[Y|X\right]^m|X=X_0\right]\,\)  is different from \(E\left[Y^m|X=X_0\right]\,\) when m>1 (they are only equal for first moment when m=1) and conditional moments of Y, we want to find for the purpose of conditional density are given as  \(E\left[Y^m|X=X_0\right]\,\)

My strategy is to write 
\[\,Y^m\,=\,\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,+\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)^m\]
and then apply binomial theorem and finally take conditional expectations on all terms of binomial expansion.


Explicitly doing it for second moment, we take the following steps as

\begin{align}
\,Y^2\, &=\,\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,+\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)^2\, \\
&=\,\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)^2+ \,2\,\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right) \,\left[Y|X\right]\,+ \, \,{\left[Y|X\right]\,}^2\, \\
\end{align}

Now we take conditional expectation w.r.t \(X\,=\,X_0\) as

\begin{align}
\,E\left[Y^2\,|\,X=X_0\right]\, &=\,E\left[\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,+\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)^2\,|\,X=X_0 \right]\\
&=\,E\left[\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)^2\,|\,X\,=\,X_0\right]\,\\
&+ \,2\,E\left[\left(\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right) \,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)\,|X=X_0\right] \\
&+ \, E\left[\,{\left[Y|X\right]\,}^2\,|\,X\,=X_0\,\right] \\
\end{align}

but middle term \( \,2\,E\left[\left(\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right) \,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)\,|X=X_0\right] \) goes to zero and we are left with expression for conditional second moment as


\begin{align}
\,E\left[Y^2\,|\,X=X_0\right]\, &=\,E\left[\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)^2\,|\,X\,=\,X_0\right]\,\\
&+ \, E\left[\,{\left[Y|X\right]\,}^2\,|\,X\,=X_0\,\right] \\
\end{align}

Please note that  \(E\left[\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)\,|\,X\,=\,X_0\right]\,\) is zero mean uncorrelated part and  \(E\left[\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)^2\,|\,X\,=\,X_0\right]\,\) is expectation of conditional variance of uncorrelated part. Please also note that we  know how to calculate \(E\left[\,{\left[Y|X\right]\,}^2\,|\,X\,=X_0\,\right] \) which has to be calculated by method of local Taylor Series as described in previous post. We re-expand the Z-series of \(\left[Y|X\right]\) as a function of \(Z_x\) as a Taylor series around \(Z_x\,=\,Z_{x_0}\) and then find expectation of conditional moments using Z-series centered around the conditional expected value.


For the third moment, we take the following steps as

\begin{align}
\,Y^3\, &=\,\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,+\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)^3\, \\
&=\,\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)^3+ \,3\,\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)^2 \,\left[Y|X\right]\,+\,3\,\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)\,\, \,{\left[Y|X\right]\,}^2\,+ \, \,{\left[Y|X\right]\,}^3\, \\
\end{align}

Now we take conditional expectation w.r.t \(X\,=\,X_0\) as


\begin{align}
\,E\left[Y^3\,|\,X=X_0\right]\, &=\,E\left[\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,+\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)^3\,|\,X=X_0 \right]\\
&=\,E\left[\left(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\right)^3\,|\,X\,=\,X_0\right]\,\\

In order to model the nth conditional moment of Y, we have to evaluate 

\[\,E\left[\,Y^m\,\,|\,X\,=\,X_0\,\right]\,=\,E\left[\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,+\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)^m\,|\,X\,=\,X_0\,\right]\] 

When we expand the above equation using binomial formula, we have to calculate conditional cross moments of the type given below

\[\,E\left[\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)^{(m-k)}\,\biggl(\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)^k\, \bigg|\,X\,=\,X_0\,\right]\] 

We have already reasonably correctly found conditional Z-series of  \(\left[Y|X\right]\,\) in terms of \(Z_x\) and we have already used them in approximate calculations of conditional distributions of CEV noises. For calculation of above cross-moments, we would need to also find the conditional Z-series of moments of \({\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)}^k\) in terms of \(Z_x\). We cannot find a conditional Z-series of  \({\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)}\) in terms of \(Z_x\) since this correlation has already been calculated as \(\left[Y|X\right]\,\) and subtracted from Y and therefore \({\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)}\) is orthogonal to \(Z_x\) (and higher hermite polynomials) meaning there is no first order correlation between \({\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)}\) and hermite polynomials of \(Z_x\) even though correlations in higher order moments of  \({\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)}^k\) and hermite polynomials of \(Z_x\) might exist (they are orthogonal but not independent). For independence all the higher order moments  \({\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)}^k\) and hermite polynomials of \(Z_x\) should have zero correlations. (We can possibly generalize it to say that two variables are independent when all their cross-moments take no correlations between their respective moments).

In order to find Z-series of kth moment, we could try regressing \({\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)}^k\) on powers of \(Z_x\). But then, in order to turn the Z-series into conditional Z-series about a point \(Z_{x_0}\), we would have to find all the appropriate moments of kth moment at  \(\,\,Z_x\,=\,Z_{x_0}\,\) and then convert these moments into conditional Z-series at  \(\,\,Z_x\,=\,Z_{x_0}\,\) . But this would not go smoothly as our density construction from moments program does not work smoothly when applied to find Z-series of moments of random variables where we have to use moments of moments(of the random variables) as input.

(Before you read next paragraph, please look at this post: https://forum.wilmott.com/viewtopic.php?t=99702&start=2925#p889697)

An alternative way to  find the conditional Z-series of \({\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)}^k\) at  \(\,\,Z_x\,=\,Z_{x_0}\,\) is to apply regression directly on shifted value of Z by regressing \({\biggl(\,Y_p\,-\,{\left[Y|X\right]}_p\,\biggr)}^k\) on \(\bigr(Z_{x_p}\,-\,Z_{x_0}\bigl)\) instead of straight regression of \({\biggl(\,Y_p\,-\,{\left[Y|X\right]}_p\,\biggr)}^k\) on \(\,Z_{x_p}\,\). This way we can directly get the  conditional Z-series of \({\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)}^k\) at  \(\,\,Z_x\,=\,Z_{x_0}\,\) using one regression and we would not have to first calculate moments at  \(\,\,Z_x\,=\,Z_{x_0}\,\) and then convert those moments to conditional Z-series at \(\,\,Z_x\,=\,Z_{x_0}\,\). (If we do not follow this alternative strategy then,) Our Z-series calculation program would almost certainly fail when applied to find Z-series of higher moments(from their own conditional moments).

Another important thing is to not use a very high order Z-series for higher moments and limit them to only order=4 or order=5 where order means order of highest Hermite polynomial in hermite -series. For proper modeling, We would want to draw the Z-series of moments and then decide what order gives us most stability in Z-series of moments by trying various orders for Z-series and then graphing them. Again, for finding Z-series of higher moments, we sometimes need to avoid the temptation to increase the number of hermite polynomials and rather decrease the number of hermite polynomials used in order to find a fit that is robust and approximate. 

Once we have found both  conditional Z-series of  \(\left[Y|X\right]\,\) in terms of \(Z_x\) and the conditional Z-series of kth moment \({\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)}^{(m-k)}\) in terms of \(Z_x\), then we can easily find the conditional cross-moments given below 
\[\,E\left[\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)^{(m-k)}\,\biggl(\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)^k\, \bigg|\,X\,=\,X_0\,\right]\] 
by first finding the appropriate powers of both conditional Z-series and then simply multiplying them with each other as both Z-series share the same underlying \(Z_x\) in their calculations  and then take expectations over the powers of gaussians (powers of \(Z_x\)) with odd powers going to zero.

Again, please let me emphasize that we are not using one conditional Z-series of  \(\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)\) as \(\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)\) is orthogonal to \(Z_x\) but higher moments of \(\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)\) do take correlations with \(Z_x\) and its hermite polynomials and therefore we have to find conditional Z-series of each of the (m-k)th moment  \({\biggl(\,Y\,-\,\left[Y|X\right]\,\biggr)}^{(m-k)}\) as a function of \(Z_x\) and use these Z-series of different moments to find the cross-moments needed in the main formula.

Here I first describe how to find the local moments around some point on a density using local Taylor series as I had previously mentioned. And then post the corrected version of yesterday's post.

Suppose we have a Hermite-series of random variable Y given as

\[Y(Z_y)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,H_n(Z_y)\]

We suppose its equivalent Z-series of Y is given as

\[Y(Z_y)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,\tilde{a}_n\,{Z_y}^n\]

The average expected moments are obviously given as

\[E\left[{Y(Z_y)}^m\right]\,=\,E\left[\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,\tilde{a}_n\,{Z_y}^n\,\right)^m\,\right]\]

But these are average moments of the distribution. And we want to find conditional moments at some point on the distribution. We can also sometimes call them local moments.

Suppose we want to find conditional moments of Y given \(Y\,=\,Y_0\). At \(Y_0\) the value of \(Z_y\) is \(Z_{y_0}\). We take the derivatives of Z-series at \(Z_{y_0}\) and re-expand the Z-series as a Taylor series around  (Z_{y_0}\) using derivatives calculated there. We take the coefficients of this new Z-Series/Hermite-Series around the conditional  value \(Z_y\,=\,Z_{y_0}\) to find the conditional moments/local moments.

Taking above Example of the Z-series

\[Y(Z_y)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,\tilde{a}_n\,{Z_y}^n\]

Taking conditional value at \(Z_{y_0}\), we have

\[Y_0\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,\tilde{a}_n\,{Z_{y_0}}^n\]

The value of first derivative at \(Z_{y_0}\) is given as
