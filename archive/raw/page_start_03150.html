Friends, I tried to specify median as a constant in the least squares regression to find the coefficients of Z-series. Data Median is specified as a constant and regression is done on only first seven powers of the ZI of ideal normal density grid. A constant is not specified in the regression. And it works extremely well. All moments are matched very closely by the regression calculated coefficients of the Z-series which was earlier not possible. I am posting the program, please use this program for finding optimal coefficients of Z-series.
.
.
.
.
Code: Select all
function [Coeff,Zy] = CalculateZSeriesFromData02GivenMedian(Yin,Order,ZI)

Ndata=length(Yin);

MeanYin=sum(Yin(1:Ndata))/Ndata;
Ym(1:Ndata)=Yin(1:Ndata)-MeanYin;
YmVar=sum(Ym(1:Ndata).^2)/Ndata;
Y(1:Ndata)=Ym(1:Ndata)/sqrt(YmVar);

[Yss,I]=sort(Y(1:Ndata));

DataMedian=(Yss(Ndata/2)+Yss(Ndata/2+1))/2;

Ys=Yss-DataMedian;

for hh=1:Order
    W(1:Ndata,hh)=ZI(1:Ndata).^(hh);
end


Ymu(1:Ndata,1)=Ys(1:Ndata);

WW=(W'*W);

coeff=(WW)\(W'*Ymu);


%Store in coefficietns array of all random varaibles
Coeff(2:Order+1)= coeff(1:Order,1);
Coeff(1)=DataMedian;
%Store the associated Z in Z-array of all coefficients.   
Zy(I(1:Ndata))=W(1:Ndata,2);


Coeff(1:Order+1)=Coeff(1:Order+1).*sqrt(YmVar);
Coeff(1)=Coeff(1)+MeanYin;

clf;
PlotZSeriesDensity(Coeff(1),Coeff(2:Order+1),'r')
hold on

NoOfBins=150;
MaxCutOff=20;
[XDensity,IndexOut,IndexMax] = MakeDensityFromSimulation_Infiniti_NEW(Yin,Ndata,NoOfBins,MaxCutOff );
plot(IndexOut(1:IndexMax),XDensity(1:IndexMax),'b');

str=input("Look at comparison of Density");


end

I simulated three SDEs and saved their paths at time T1 (these were Xk with K=3) and then I continued the simulation to a later time T2 and from the paths at time T2, I found the value of sum of three SDE joint simulation paths and this was variable to be conditionally predicted and I called it Y. The problem to solve is to find the conditional density of sum \(Y(T_2)\,=\,X_1(T_2)\,+\,X_2(T_2)\,+X_3(T_2)\,\)  given the arbitrarily chosen values of \(X1(T_1), X2(T_1), X3(T_1) \,\). I then did an independent monte carlo simulation of three densities from chosen values of \(X1(T_1), X2(T_1), X3(T_1) \). and continued it for time \(T2-T1\)(This gave me the true monte carlo conditional distribution of \(Y(T_2)\,=\,X_1(T_2)\,+\,X_2(T_2)\,+X_3(T_2)\,\)). I overlaid the densities of analytic solution of density given as \(P(Y(T_2)|\,X_1(T_1)=x_1\,,\,X_2(T_1)=x_2\,,X_3(T_1)=x_3\,) \) and the monte carlo derived density of Y. Even though I used three explaining variables, the logic is for arbitrary N variables. Just change NDim=3 to appropriate N and supply SDE parameters.

Basically \(Y(T_2)\,=\,X_1(T_2)\,+\,X_2(T_2)\,+X_3(T_2)\)  and we are conditioning as

\[P(Y(T_2)|\,X_1(T_1)=x_1\,,\,X_2(T_1)=x_2\,,X_3(T_1)=x_3\,) \]

The algorithm is very fast with almost zero training time ( less than a second for this data) as compared to machine learning that may take days or even weeks to properly train the network and the output of algorithm is better than what is mostly found out of  neural networks after several hundred hours of training. In our algorithm most time is used in monte carlo simulations of data that is required for generation of variables and for finding the reference simulation density(taken as true density for comparison) and not in training of the parameters.

Our algorithm has just knowledge of joint (random) data of \(Y(T_2)\) and  \(X1(T_1), X2(T_1), X3(T_1) \). And conditional value of density \(P(Y(T_2)|\,X_1(T_1)=x_1\,,\,X_2(T_1)=x_2\,,X_3(T_1)=x_3\,) \) is calculated by our algorithm.

I also want to notice that our predicted variable Y is sum of N variables in the above example so its variance and higher moments continue to increase with increasing predictors, however, when we are trying to explain just one fixed variable Y with N explaining variables correlated with Y, the predicted conditional density would continue to become sharper with decreasing variance.

An obvious corollary of the above posts seems to be that in mathematical systems defined by multiple independent basis, each order of basis should have its own set of correlations. This should not only apply to hermite-polynomials basis and Gaussian powers basis, in other mathematical problems it should apply to every independent basis to have its own set of correlations. It obviously seems deficient to have one set of correlations for problems involving multiple independent basis. 

Although this conclusion arises from the study of Hermite polynomial expansions and Gaussian power bases, the underlying idea is considerably more general. In any framework that employs a set of orthogonal or otherwise independent basis functions to represent complex systems, a single correlation structure is often adequate only when the representation involves a single basis component or when higher-order effects are negligible. However, when multiple basis orders contribute meaningfully to the representation, collapsing all dependence information into a single set of correlations can obscure important structural relationships and lead to an incomplete or inaccurate characterization of the system. 

What still needs to be understood even more importantly is the dynamics of interdependence of correlations across multiple correlated non-orthogonal bases(like Gaussian power bases).
