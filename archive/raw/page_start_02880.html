I have suggested in a previous post that we can solve for all of the 6X6 coefficients of 2D Hermite-series from knowledge of \(B_1,\ldots\,,\,B_6\) and \(C_1,\ldots\,,\,C_6\). This is because to satisfy squared sum across both rows and columns of a 2D Hermite-series, the squared terms inside the matrix have to be in specific ratios. We can use this knowledge in other ways to make numerical methods. For example we can find coefficients \(C_1,\ldots\,,\,C_6\) from one dimensional Hermite-series of \(Ydecorr|Z_y\). We had been using this coefficients as upper bound on row variances when our iterative method improved dramatically after introducing those analytic bounds. And instead of optimizing over 36 coefficients, we simply optimize over six variables given by \(B_1,\ldots\,,\,B_6\) . We have defined these variables  \(B_1,\ldots\,,\,B_6\) in a previous post.

In this way, we need to do only six dimensional optimization in our iterative method for fitting of 6X6 2D Hermite-series instead of optimizing over 36 values. I expect that this new technique would be both faster and more accurate and we would simultaneously fit to all cross-moments as well instead of relying on poorly worked out regressions with high variance in regression coefficients.

I suggest the we remove the 1st order correlation effect of X on Y and write Zero mean Ydecorr as


\begin{align}
\left[Ydecorr|X\right](\tilde{Z}_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,&=\, \, \Big[ \, b_{1,0} \, + \, b_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, b_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, b_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \,H_1(\tilde{Z}_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, b_{2,0} \, + \, b_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, b_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, b_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(\tilde{Z}_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, b_{3,0} \, + \, b_{3,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, b_{3,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, b_{3,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_3(\tilde{Z}_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

Expectation of second moment of  \(Ydecorr\) as a function of squared hermite polynomials is given by formula


\begin{align}
E\left[{Ydecorr}^2|X\right](\tilde{Z}_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,&=\, \, E \Bigg[ \Big[ \, {b_{1,0}}^2 \, + \, {b_{1,1}}^2 \,{H_1(Z_x)}^2 \,+\, {b_{1,2}}^2 \, {H_2(Z_x)}^2 \, + \, {b_{1,3}}^2 \,{H_3(Z_x)}^2\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \,{H_1(\tilde{Z}_y)}^2 \, \\
&+\,  \Big[ \, {b_{2,0}}^2 \, + \, {b_{2,1}}^2 \,{H_1(Z_x)}^2 \,+\, {b_{2,2}}^2 \, {H_2(Z_x)}^2 \, + \, {b_{2,3}}^2 \,{H_3(Z_x)}^2\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \,{H_2(\tilde{Z}_y)}^2 \, \\
&+\,  \Big[ \, {b_{3,0}}^2 \, + \, {b_{3,1}}^2 \,{H_1(Z_x)}^2 \,+\, {b_{3,2}}^2 \, {H_2(Z_x)}^2 \, + \, {b_{3,3}}^2 \,{H_3(Z_x)}^2\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \,{H_3(\tilde{Z}_y)}^2 \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \Bigg] \\
\end{align} 


In one dimension, we know that 
\begin{align}
E\left[{Ydecorr}^2|X\right](\tilde{Z}_y \,)\, \,&=\, \, E \Big[ \, {c_{0}}^2 \, + \, {c_{1}}^2 \,{H_1(\tilde{Z_y})}^2 \,+\, {c_{2}}^2 \, {H_2(\tilde{Z_y})}^2 \, + \, {c_{3}}^2 \,{H_3(\tilde{Z}_y)}^2\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \\
&=\, \,  \Big[ \, {c_{0}}^2 \, + \, {c_{1}}^2 \, \,+\,2\, {c_{2}}^2 \,  \, + \,6\, {c_{3}}^2 \,\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big]
\end{align}

In above \(c_n\) are coefficients of 1 dimensional hermite series of Ydecorr.

Suppose in other dimension, we have

\begin{align}
E\left[{Ydecorr}^2|X\right]({Z}_x \,)\, \,&=\, \, E \Big[ \, {d_{0}}^2 \, + \, {d_{1}}^2 \,{H_1({Z_x})}^2 \,+\, {d_{2}}^2 \, {H_2({Z_x})}^2 \, + \, {d_{3}}^2 \,{H_3({Z}_x)}^2\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \\
&=\, \,  \Big[ \, {d_{0}}^2 \, + \, {d_{1}}^2 \, \,+\,2\, {d_{2}}^2 \,  \, + \,6\, {d_{3}}^2 \,\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big]
\end{align}

We normalize the above sum to one and write
\[{\bar{d}_n}^2\,=\,\frac{{d_n}^2}{ \Big[ \, {d_{0}}^2 \, + \, {d_{1}}^2 \, \,+\,2\, {d_{2}}^2 \,  \, + \,6\, {d_{3}}^2 \,\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big]}\]

Then I conjencture that two dimensional expectation could be fulfilled by a structure of the kind (The first term in large brackets is already equal to second moment from one dimension calibration and second term in other large brackets has expectation of its variance/2nd moment equal to unity and does not alter the second moment but is chosen so that cross-moments of Ydecorr with X are as perfectly satisfied as possible)

\[E\left[{Ydecorr}^2|X\right](\tilde{Z}_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,=\, \,  \Big[ \, {c_{0}}^2 \, + \, {c_{1}}^2 \, \,+\,2\, {c_{2}}^2 \,  \, + \,6\, {c_{3}}^2 \,\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \Big[ \, {\bar{d}_{0}}^2 \, + \, {\bar{d}_{1}}^2 \, \,+\,2\, {\bar{d}_{2}}^2 \,  \, + \,6\, {\bar{d}_{3}}^2 \,\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big]\]

This would imply that (up to sign that has to be decided separately) coefficients of 2D hermite matrix are given as

\[\,b_{n,m}=\,c_n\,\bar{d}_m \]

I really think that above formulas for 2D Coefficients should work. At least they make a solution to the variance of 2D Z-series. We can iterate over six \(\bar{d}_m\) in our iterative program(with the constraint that their weighted sum or 2nd moment is unity as earlier described) and settle at values that give us best fit to the cross-moments. Even if we could not perfectly calibrate the cross-moments with above six coefficients, they can give us a very good and cheap initial guess that could be used in full-blown optimization of 36 coefficients. 

Again our proposed solution for 2D Hermite series is akin to product of two single dimensional hermite-series, the first of which is one dimensional hermite-series of the random variable and the second one is a normalized variance hermite-series in other variable \(Z_x\) so that cross-moments with X are as well calibrated as possible. Even if it does not perfectly satisfy the cross-moments, we can use it as a good initial guess for the full-blown optimization over all 36 different coefficients.

Friends, if we think more deeply about our problem, we realize that there need to be some mathematical structure and relationships between different coefficients of various hermites when we try to infer parameters of 2D Hermite-Series.

I should have realized it very long ago but we can recall from the time when we were modelling SDEs to higher order that coefficients of higher hermites in SDEs were based on derivatives of the SDE function of first hermite. Granted that SDEs evolution have a structure in time but we are dealing with smooth and continuous functions and they should also have a structure in which coefficients of higher  hermites depend upon derivatives of some expression of smaller hermites.

I will give a very simple example of an SDE

\[dy\,=\,\sigma(y)\, dz_y\,=\, \sigma(y)\, H_1(Z_y) \,\sqrt{\Delta(t)}+\, {\sigma(y)}' \sigma(y)\, H_2(Z_y) \,\frac{\Delta(t)}{2}+\, {\sigma(y)}'' {\sigma(y)}^2 \, H_1(Z_y) \,\Delta(t) + \, \left[ {\sigma(y)}'' +{{\sigma(y)}'}^2 \right] \,H_3(Z_y)\,\,\frac{{\Delta(t)}^{1.5}}{6} \]

I think in ordinary densities when there is no time evolution, we should still have derivatives structures in higher hermites possibly as given below

\[y\,=\, \frac{d\sigma(y)}{dZ_y}\, Z_y \,+\,\,\frac{1}{2}\, \frac{d^2{\sigma(y)}}{{dZ_y}^2} \, {Z_y}^2  + \,\frac{1}{6}\,  \frac{d^3{\sigma(y)}}{{dZ_y}^3} \, {Z_y}^3\, \]

In the above \(\sigma(y)\) is not known but once it is known as a function, coefficients of higher hermites are no longer free parameters but are given by some expression of derivatives of the first hermite. 

This should also be true in two dimensional and higher dimensional Hermite-series. 

We can take a reasonably complex functional form of dependence between underlying variable  Y and \({Z_y}\) and then we would be free to choose parameters of the first order expansion of Y as a function of Z and all the higher order expansions coefficients would be derivatives of the first order expansion of Y as a function of Z. This expansion may not be helpful in one dimension since but in higher dimensions we would need to find only six or eight parameters and coefficients of higher hermites would be derivatives of first order expansions.

OK I can write the whole thing with more clarity. It is like saying that I find it very difficult to optimize for 8X8 coefficients of a 2D Taylor series but I can possibly try a more simpler analytic expression with lesser number of free parameters for the original function and take its cross-derivatives and optimize on the smaller number of free parameters of the analytic function rather than having to find all the huge number of coefficients of 2D Hermite-Series through optimization. 

Again, we do not start by assuming a perfect relationship but rather a functional form that has its own free parameters that are much smaller than 2D Taylor series free parameters and optimize over the free parameters of the functional form for a robust calibration.

Though the idea is very simple, I believe it can hugely simplify our optimizations and calibration of hermite-series. I will come up with several interesting examples in a day or two.

For example, we can try a simple functional form for our two dimensional Hermite series Ydecorr (that we were trying to find 36/64 coefficients of) as

\[\text{Ydecorr}(Z_y,Z_x)\,=\,a_0\, {(c_1+b_0\,Z_y)}^{\alpha_1} \,+ \,a_1\, {(c_2+b_1\,Z_y)}^{\alpha_2}\,+\, a_2\,{(c_3+b_3\,Z_y)}^{\beta_1} \,\,{(c_4+b_4\,Z_x)}^{\gamma_1} \, \\+\, a_3\,{(c_5+b_5\,Z_y)}^{\beta_2} \,\,{(c_6+b_6\,Z_x)}^{\gamma_2} \,\]

But the above form still has a lot of free parameters. As a start, we can try a simpler functional form of the kind given below as

\[\text{Ydecorr}(Z_y,Z_x)\,=\,a_0\, {(c_1+b_1\,Z_y)}^{\alpha_1} \,+\, a_2\,{(c_3+b_3\,Z_y)}^{\beta_1} \,\,{(c_4+b_4\,Z_x)}^{\gamma_1} \, \,\]

and try to see how things go. If it works and we can find ways to simple solutions, we could later increase complexity.

If I try to write a Newton Solver for above or other similar forms, good thing is that it will be quite reasonably over determined system when we try to fit it to thirty-six or sixty-four cross-moments. An over-determined system is usually good for stability. And its higher powers would usually be reasonably tame and would not be explosive either.

First important thing to notice is that relative size of coefficientsof 2D Hermite-series of Y in Zx direction(along powers of Zx) mainly determines the fit to part of cross moments (of XY) related jointly to both variables while size of coefficients in Z_y direction mainly determines the match to moments of random variable Y itself. When fitting if the cross-moment were normalized by product of both univariate moments, relative size of 2D Hermite-series of Y in Z_x direction would largely matter.

We write the 2D Z-series of Ydecorr as 


\begin{align}
\left[Ydecorr|X\right](\tilde{Z}_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,&=\, \, \Big[ \, b_{0,0} \, + \, b_{0,1} \,Z_x \,+\, b_{0,2} \, {Z_x}^2 \, + \, b_{0,3} \,{Z_x}^3\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, \\ &+\, \Big[ \, b_{1,0} \, + \, b_{1,1} \,Z_x \,+\, b_{1,2} \, {Z_x}^2 \, + \, b_{1,3} \,{Z_x}^3\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \,\tilde{Z}_y \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, b_{2,0} \, + \, b_{2,1} \,Z_x \,+\, b_{2,2} \, {Z_x}^2 \, \, + \, b_{2,3} \,{Z_x}^3\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, {\tilde{Z}_y}^2 \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, b_{3,0} \, + \, b_{3,1} \,Z_x \,+\, b_{3,2} \, {Z_x}^2 \, \, + \, b_{3,3} \,{Z_x}^3\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, {\tilde{Z}_y}^3 \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

We can also write the above equation as 

\begin{align}
\left[Ydecorr|X\right](\tilde{Z}_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,&=\, \, \Big[ \, b_{0,0} \, + \, b_{1,0} \,\tilde{Z}_y \,+\, b_{2,0} \, {\tilde{Z}_y}^2 \, + \, b_{3,0} \,{\tilde{Z}_y}^3\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, \\ &+\, \Big[ \, b_{0,1} \, + \, b_{1,1} \,\tilde{Z}_y \,+\, b_{2,1} \, {\tilde{Z}_y}^2 \, + \, b_{3,1} \,{\tilde{Z}_y}^3\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \,Z_x \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, b_{0,2} \, + \, b_{1,2} \,\tilde{Z}_y \,+\, b_{2,2} \, {\tilde{Z}_y}^2 \, \, + \, b_{3,2} \,{\tilde{Z}_y}^3\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, {Z_x}^2 \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, b_{0,3} \, + \, b_{1,3} \,\tilde{Z}_y \,+\, b_{2,3} \, {\tilde{Z}_y}^2 \, \, + \, b_{3,3} \,{\tilde{Z}_y}^3\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, {Z_x}^3 \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

We further write in a simplified notation as


\begin{equation}
\left[Ydecorr|X\right](\tilde{Z}_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,=\, \, B_{0}(\tilde{Z}_y) \, + \, \, B_{1}(\tilde{Z}_y) \,Z_x +\, \, B_{2}(\tilde{Z}_y) \,{Z_x}^2 +\, \ldots\,+\, \, B_{5}(\tilde{Z}_y) \,{Z_x}^5 
\end{equation}

We notice that coefficients \(B_{n}(\tilde{Z}_y)\) are themselves Z-series. We also notice that if \(B_{n}(\tilde{Z}_y)\) are chosen properly, the moments of Ydecorr would be consistent with their observed values and also consistent with cross-product ratios. We again specify that  \(B_{n}(\tilde{Z}_y)\) are given as
\[B_{n}(\tilde{Z}_y)\,=\,\Big[ \, b_{0,n} \, + \, b_{1,n} \,\tilde{Z}_y \,+\, b_{2,n} \, {\tilde{Z}_y}^2 \, + \, b_{3,n} \,{\tilde{Z}_y}^3\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big]\] 
We notice that we cannot replace \(B_{n}(\tilde{Z}_y)\) by constant values \(B_n\) since moments would be inconsistent.

But we notice that we can capture each different moment of Ydecorr by constant values \(B_n\)  but choice of these \(B_n\)  would have to be different for each moment.

Our game plan is to find out different constant values of \(B_n\) for each mth moment and name them \(B^m_n\) (here m is not in sense of power and rather is an index only) and these constant values are so chosen that all cross moments of mth moment of Ydecorr with different (1:6) powers of Z-series of X are matched. All these calibrations are one-dimensional and can be done with one-dimensional Newton method. Again for each mth moment of Ydecorr, we will multiply with Z-series of one to six powers of X and back out constant \(B^m_n\) particularly for mth moment of Ydecorr. Please note that \(B^m_n\) are coefficients of 1st power of Ydecorr calibrated so that cross-moments with mth moment of Ydecorr are satisified. These will be found by one-dimensional Newton. All these calculations are done in one dimensional \(Z_x\) direction. I want to mention again that in our universal specification all coefficients \(B_{n}(\tilde{Z}_y)\) are themselves Z-series but we for each of the mth (one to six ) moments, we can replace the Z-series with constants \(B^m_n\) and these constants are so chosen that all cross-products with mth moment are captured and so these constants will be different for each different moment of Ydecorr. So in second moment expansion of Ydecorr, we will use constant coefficients \(B^2_n\) in the Z-series of Ydecorr and for third moment expansion of Ydecorr, we would use constant coefficients \(B^3_n\) in the Z-series of Ydecorr and so on. As I earlier mentioned that please do not confuse index m with power symbol.

Now we expand various mth moments of Z-series of Ydecorr in their respective coefficients \(B^m_n\) of the Z-series associated with each mth moment.

Expansion of 2nd Moment is given after keeping non-Zero values. In the below, we will use constant coefficients \(B_n\) derived from second moment of Ydecorr. I am not using notation \(B^2_n\) for coefficients below but they are all \(B^2_n\) as derived from second moment.
\[{B_0}^2+({B_1}^2+2 B_0 B_2) {Z_x}^2+({B_2}^2+2 B_1 B_3+2 B_0 B_4) {Z_x}^4+({B_3}^2+2 B_2 B_4+2 B_1 B_5) {Z_x}^6 \\+({B_4}^2+2 B_3 B_5) {Z_x}^8+{B_5}^2 {Z_x}^{10}\]

Expansion of 3rd Moment is given after keeping non-Zero values. In the below, we will use constant coefficients \(B_n\) derived from third moment of Ydecorr and these \(B_n\) are different from the ones used to find 2nd moment of Y-decorr. I am not using notation \(B^3_n\) for coefficients below but they are all \(B^3_n\) as derived from third moment.

\[{B_0}^3+(3 B_0 {B_1}^2+3 {B_0}^2 B_2) {Z_x}^2+(3 {B_1}^2 B_2+3 B_0 {B_2}^2+6 B_0 B_1 B_3+3 {B_0}^2 B_4) {Z_x}^4 \\+({B_2}^3+6 B_1 B_2 B_3+3 B_0 {B_3}^2+3 {B_1}^2 B4+6 B_0 B_2 B_4+6 B_0 B_1 B_5) {Z_x}^6 \\+(3 B_2 {B_3}^2+3 {B_2}^2 B_4+6 B_1 B_3 B_4+3 B_0 {B_4}^2+6 B_1 B_2 B_5+6 B_0 B_3 B_5) Zx^8\\+(3 {B_3}^2 B_4+3 B_2 {B_4}^2+6 B_2 B_3 B_5+6 B_1 B_4 B_5+3 B_0 {B_5}^2) {Z_x}^{10}\\+({B_4}^3+6 B_3 B_4 B_5+3 B_2 {B_5}^2) {Z_x}^{12}+3 B_4 {B_5}^2 {Z_x}^{14}\]

We can find all up to six or eight expansions like the expansions above. The point is that true Z-series coefficients \(B_{n}(\tilde{Z}_y)\) have to satisfy all of the above moment expansions made with different constant coefficients simultaneously. 

So from above two moments we can write the following equations. First writing equations from 2nd moment as (all the constant coefficients \(B_n\) on RHS of equations below are known and were backed out from cross moments of 2nd order moment of Ydecorr with all first six or eight moments of X.

\[E[{B_0(\tilde{Z}_y)}^2]\,=\,{B_0}^2\]
\[E[({B_1(\tilde{Z}_y)}^2+2 B_0(\tilde{Z}_y) B_2(\tilde{Z}_y))]={B_1}^2+2 B_0 B_2 \]
\[E[({B_2(\tilde{Z}_y)}^2+2 B_1(\tilde{Z}_y) B_3(\tilde{Z}_y)+2 B_0(\tilde{Z}_y) B_4(\tilde{Z}_y))]=({B_2}^2+2 B_1 B_3+2 B_0 B_4)\]
\[E[({B_3(\tilde{Z}_y)}^2+2 B_2(\tilde{Z}_y) B_4(\tilde{Z}_y)+2 B_1(\tilde{Z}_y) B_5(\tilde{Z}_y))]\,=\,({B_3}^2+2 B_2 B_4+2 B_1 B_5)\]
\[E[({B_4(\tilde{Z}_y)}^2+2 B_3(\tilde{Z}_y) B_5(\tilde{Z}_y))]\,=\,({B_4}^2+2 B_3 B_5)\]
\[E[{B_5(\tilde{Z}_y)}^2]\,=\,{B_5}^2\] 

Similarly now writing equations from 3rd moment as (all the constant coefficients \(B_n\) on RHS of equations below are known and were backed out from cross moments of 3rd order moment of Ydecorr with all first six or eight moments of X. These constant coefficients backed out from third moment of Ydecorr are different from constant coefficients found from 2nd moments of Ydecorr used in equations right above earlier than this block. 

\[E[{B_0(\tilde{Z}_y)}^3]={B_0}^3\]

and for second derivative, we have

\[\frac{\partial^2 X}{\partial Z^2}\,=\,\frac{\partial^2 X}{\partial W^2}\,{\left[\frac{\partial Z}{\partial W}\,\right]}^{-2} -\,\frac{\partial^2 Z}{\partial W^2}\,{\left[\frac{\partial Z}{\partial W}\,\right]}^{-3} \,\frac{\partial X}{\partial W}\, \]

I tried the above formulas for simple functions and found that they work seamlessly and for many analytic functions, formulas found by above method (second order finite-differences on uniform grid) matched perfectly with direct calculations of second order accurate non-uniform grid derivatives.

I am attaching an example where   \(\frac{\partial X}{\partial Z}\), \(\frac{\partial^2 X}{\partial Z^2}\) where   X=Y^2  and Z=Y^.5 so both X and Z are non-uniform grid when calculated with respect to Y. Y does not come into play in constant step finite-differences and  rather an integer function is used for W.

I have written a matlab program to verify above calculations and I am attaching it. function [] = NonUniformGridDerivatives(X,ZI,paths)


paths=800;

Y=(1:.005:5);%Y is used to create a non-uniform grid in X and ZI. Y does not appear in 
%any grid and the actual grid is between X and ZI which are calculated
%below.


X=Y.^.5;
ZI=Y.^2;

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
%In this block, we calculate dXdZ and d2XdZ2 using a uniform integer
%underlying grid.
for nn=2:paths-1
    dXdW(nn)=(X(nn+1)-X(nn-1))/2.0;
    d2XdW2(nn)=(X(nn+1)-2*X(nn)+X(nn-1));
    dZIdW(nn)=(ZI(nn+1)-ZI(nn-1))/2.0;
    d2ZIdW2(nn)=(ZI(nn+1)-2*ZI(nn)+ZI(nn-1));
    
    dXdZI_unif(nn)=dXdW(nn).*(dZIdW(nn)).^-1;
    d2XdZI2_unif(nn)=d2XdW2(nn).*(dZIdW(nn)).^(-2)-d2ZIdW2(nn).*(dZIdW(nn)).^(-3).*(dXdW(nn));
end

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%55

%In this block, we use second order accurate formulas for calculation of
%dXdZ and d2XdZ2 and directly calculate them using non-uniform grid
%finite-differences.

for nn=2:paths-1
    hp(nn)=ZI(nn+1)-ZI(nn);
    hn(nn)=ZI(nn)-ZI(nn-1);
   
    Zeta_n(nn)=hn(nn).*(hn(nn)+hp(nn));
    Zeta_0(nn)=(hn(nn).*hp(nn));
    Zeta_p(nn)=hp(nn).*(hn(nn)+hp(nn));
    
    dXdZI(nn)=-hp(nn)./Zeta_n(nn).*X(nn-1)+(hp(nn)-hn(nn))./Zeta_0(nn).*X(nn)+hn(nn)./Zeta_p(nn).*X(nn+1);
    
    d2XdZI2(nn)=2.0./Zeta_n(nn).*X(nn-1)-2.0./Zeta_0(nn).*X(nn)+2.0./Zeta_p(nn).*X(nn+1);
end

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%5

%dXdZI
%dXdZI_unif
%dXdZI_unif-dXdZI


plot(ZI(2:paths-1),dXdZI_unif(2:paths-1),'r',ZI(2:paths-1),dXdZI(2:paths-1),'g')


str=input("The first derivatives using both uniform and non-uniform grid overlaid");



%d2XdZI2
%d2XdZI2_unif
%d2XdZI2_unif-d2XdZI2

plot(ZI(2:paths-1),d2XdZI2_unif(2:paths-1),'r',ZI(2:paths-1),d2XdZI2(2:paths-1),'g')

str=input("Second derivatives using both uniform and non-uniform grid overlaid");



end
