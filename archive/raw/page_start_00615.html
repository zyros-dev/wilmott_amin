Ok, giving a little bit more details. I have been mentioning that transition density is known exactly  between any two points in the SCSD grid at two different times. This is basically equivalent to knowing green's function related to the SDE. Now I have found new results that give transition probability or green's function of an arbitrary SDE driven by  Gaussian noise on two different arbitrary time grids only from the knowledge of the CDF at two different time Grids. This is like saying that we have solution of density/CDF of an SDE on two different time grids and we can easily calculate Green's function between all different points on two grids. These density solutions at two different time grids can be found from a traditional PDE, transform method or something else. There are many methods in stochastics that calculate marginal probability/density/CDF at a certain time and are used for calibration etc. and these solution methods do not give knowledge of green's function between two points in time but now we can now easily know the green's function and transition probabilities across two such arbitrary grids. It is a very powerful and extremely interesting result and I am sure most stochastic experts would love it. I will post the details tomorrow or day after tomorrow. I hope it will have far reaching consequences for many numerical methods.

There is a word of caution about programs I uploaded recently for calculation of density of diffusions by SCSD. When there is absorbtion at zero, the CEV density becomes defective and absorbed particles do not go back into the positive domain. In my programs, I have calculated such densities with a comparison with normal density and such comparison is no longer valid for diffusion with absorbtion at zero. People would have noticed that all these densities of CEV diffusions remain accurate when they do not get to zero but after reaching zero serious differences arise between the SCSD density and the true density. Again to get good results, we would have to calculate defective normal density comparable to CEV density with defect somehow and  make a comparison with that to get the true density. Again the reason is that we use normal density without defect to make a comparison with another defective density and such a comparison is no longer valid and this problem has to be dealt more carefully by comparing with appropriate defective normal density.

We have marginal densities of an SDE given at two differnt points [$]t_1[$] and [$]t_2[$] in time. I want to show how we can easily find the conditional and joint density between the points [$]t_1[$] and [$]t_2[$] given only the knowledge of the marginal densities at [$]t_1[$] and [$]t_2[$].
Suppose we have solution of SDE density/marginal distribution given on a grid [$]X_n[$] conisting of N subdivisions at time [$]t_1[$] . Suppose [$]X_n[$] is the center of nth subdivision and [$]Q_n[$] is the CDF associated with the end of nth subdivision on this grid. Then we have that (integrated)probability mass in nth subdivision calculated from the difference of CDF over the subdivision is given as
[$]P_n(t_1)=Q_n(t_1)-Q_{n-1}(t_1)[$].  
We further suppose we have solution of SDE density/marginal distribution given at a different larger time [$]t_2[$]on a different grid [$]X_m[$] conisting of M subdivisions. Suppose [$]X_m[$] is the centre of mth subdivision and [$]Q_m[$] is the CDF associated with the end of mth subdivision at time [$]t_2[$]. Then we have that probability mass in mth subdivision is given as
[$]P_m(t_2)=Q_m(t_2)-Q_{m-1}(t_2)[$]
We want to find out the transition probability/conditional distribution and Green's function between any arbitrary points [$]X_n(t_1)[$],  and  [$]X_m(t_2)[$] on two different time grids.
In order to find the conditional probability between [$]X_n(t_1)[$]  and [$]X_m(t_2)[$], Using the knowledge of [$]Q_n(t_1)[$], the CDF associated with [$]X_n(t_1)[$] grid, we first construct a corresponding standard brownian motion grid consisting of N points denoted as [$]B_n(t_1)[$] such that [$]Q_n(t_1)[$] is the CDF at the end of the nth subdivision [$]B_n(t_1)[$]. We have constructed this brownian motion grid from knowlwdge of [$]Q_n(t_1)[$] associted with [$]X_n(t_1)[$] using inverse CDF for normal brownian motion. We emphasize that this is the same CDF associated with [$]X_n(t_1)[$] grid. We call [$]\tilde{P}_n(t_1)[$] the probability mass in nth subdivision. We emphasize that probabilty mass [$]\tilde{P}_n(t_1)[$]  in each of N subdivisions [$]B_n(t_1)[$] in this standard brownian motion grid is the same as[$]P_n(t_1)[$] in the nth subdivision of [$]X_n(t_1)[$]. This can easily be done for example using norminv function in matlab or using Quantile function in boost in C++.  I would like to mention that this standard brownian motion [$]B_n(t_1)[$]  shares the CDF with [$]X_n(t_1)[$] grid but this standard brownian motion has its own grid and both  [$]B_n(t_1)[$] and [$]X_n(t_1)[$] do not share the grid itself and these grids are constructed to share the same CDF only. We call this CDF-Equivalent Brownian motion grid. The following holds for these grids 
[$]P \left( X_n(t_1)=x_n\right)=\tilde{P} (B_n(t_1)=b_n)[$]

Similalry using the knowledge of [$]Q_m(t_2)[$], the CDF associated with [$]X_m(t_2)[$] grid, we first construct a corresponding standard brownian motion grid consisting of M points denoted as [$]B_m(t_2)[$] such that [$]Q_m(t_2)[$] is the CDF at the end of the mth subdivision. The probabilty mass [$]\tilde{P}_m(t_2)[$]  in each of M subdivisions in this standard brownian motion grid is the same as [$]P_m(t_2)[$] in the mth subdivision of [$]X_m(t_2)[$].  I would like to mention that this standard brownian motion grid [$]B_m(t_2)[$]  shares the CDF with [$]X_m(t_2)[$] grid but this standard brownian motion has its own grid and they do not share the grid itself and this BM grid is constructed to share the CDF only with [$]X_m(t_2)[$] grid. Since this is CDF_Equivalent Brownian motion grid, the following holds for these grids 
[$]P \left( X_m(t_2)=x_m\right)=\tilde{P} (B_n(t_2)=b_m)[$]

Now we have two standard brownian motion grids, one [$]\tilde{B}_n(t_1)[$] grid that shares the same CDF associated with time [$]t_1[$] grid [$]X_n(t_1)[$] with the same number of points.  Second standard brownian motion grid  [$]B_m(t_2)[$] that shares the same CDF associated with time [$]t_2[$] grid  [$]X_m(t_2)[$] with the same number of points.  I would like to mention that these standard brownian motion grids that share CDF with corresponding X grids, do not share the grid itself and these grids are constructed to share the CDF only.
The following holds obviously from Bayes's law.
[$]P\left(X_m\left(t_2\right)=x_m\right)[$][$]=\sum _{n=1}^N P\left( X_m\left(t_2\right)=x_m,X_n\left(t_1\right)=x_n\right)[$]
[$]=\sum_{n=1}^N P( X_m(t_2)=x_m |  X_n(t_1)=x_n) P\left( X_n\left(t_1\right)=x_n\right)[$]
Similarly for Brownian motion grids, it holds that
[$]\tilde{P}\left(B_m\left(t_2\right)=b_m\right)[$][$]=\sum _{n=1}^N \tilde{P}\left( B_m\left(t_2\right)=b_m,B_n\left(t_1\right)=b_n\right)[$]
[$]=\sum_{n=1}^N \tilde{P}( B_m(t_2)=b_m |  B_n(t_1)=b_n) \tilde{P}\left( B_n\left(t_1\right)=b_n\right)[$]
if it holds that
[$]P \left( X_n(t_1)=x_n\right)=\tilde{P} (B_n(t_1)=b_n)[$]
and
[$]P \left( X_m(t_2)=x_m\right)=\tilde{P} (B_n(t_2)=b_m)[$]
Then it also holds that
[$]P( X_m(t_2)=x_m | X_n(t_1)=x_n)=\tilde{P}( B_m(t_2)=b_m| B_n(t_1)=b_n)[$]

it means that we can find the transition probabilities or equivalently Green's function between any two [$]x_n(t_1)[$] grid and [$]x_m(t_2)[$] grid only from the knowledge of their marginal probabilities by equating their transition probabilities to the ones found from CDF-Equivalent Brownian motion grids. 
We Suppose that the process X is lognormal with drift with its transition probabilities are  given as
[$]P( X_m(t_2)=x_m| X_n(t_1)=x_n)=\int _{x_m-.5 \Delta x_m}^{x_m+.5 \Delta x_m}\frac{1}{X_m\text{sigma}\sqrt{2 \text{Pi}(t_2-t_1)}}\exp (-.5\frac{(\log(X_m)-\log(x_n)-(\text{mu}-.5\text{sigma}^2)(t_2-t_1)){}^2}{\text{sigma}^2(t_2-t_1)}) d X_m[$] 
1st order approximation of this integral holds if subdivisions are small
[$]P( X_m(t_2)=x_m| X_n(t_1)=x_n)=\frac{1}{x_m\text{sigma}\sqrt{2 \text{Pi}(t_2-t_1)}}\exp(-.5\frac{(\log(x_m)-\log(x_n)-(\text{mu}-.5\text{sigma}^2)(t_2-t_1)){}^2}{\text{sigma}^2(t_2-t_1)})\Delta x_m[$]           
Transition probabilities of the corresponding Brownian motion are given as
[$]\tilde{P}( B_m(t_2)=B_m| B_n(t_1)=b_n)=\int _{b_m-.5 \Delta b_m}^{b_m+.5 \Delta b_m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \text{Pi}(t_2-t_1)}}\exp (-.5\frac{(B_m-b_n){}^2}{(t_2-t_1)}) d B_m[$] 
1st order approximation of this integral holds that if subdivisions are small
[$]\tilde{P}( B_m(t_2)=b_m| B_n(t_1)=b_n)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \text{Pi}(t_2-t_1)}}\exp(-.5\frac{(b_m-b_n){}^2}{(t_2-t_1)})\Delta b_m[$]

Now since [$]P( X_m(t_2)=x_m | X_n(t_1)=x_n)=\tilde{P}( B_m(t_2)=b_m| B_n(t_1)=b_n)[$]
Transition probabilities of lognormal diffusion in terms of Brownian motion probabilities will be given as
[$]P( X_m(t_2)=x_m | X_n(t_1)=x_n)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \text{Pi}(t_2-t_1)}}\exp(-.5\frac{(b_m-b_n){}^2}{(t_2-t_1)})\Delta b_m[$]
and Green's function of the lognormal diffusion in terms of Brownian motion transition probabilities will be given as
[$]G( X_m(t_2)=x_m | X_n(t_1)=x_n)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \text{Pi}(t_2-t_1)}}\exp(-.5\frac{(b_m-b_n){}^2}{(t_2-t_1)})\Delta b_m/\Delta x_m[$]

This result will hold for SDEs but will cease to hold in case of any anomaly like absorbtion of diffusions, barriers, or killing etc. I think it should hold for SDEs with multiplicative diffusions like Stochastic volatility models but I have not checked that so I cannot be sure.  
There are so many problems in mathematical finance and derivatives pricing when we know the marginal probability but we still have to use other methods  that require the knowledge of dynamics across different time horizons. I am sure this research will also be used in filtering theory and stochastic control problems etc.

Suppose the distribution of X(t1) is symmetrical, e.g. a Gaussian. Now look at two cases:
1) X(t2)=X(t1)
2) X(t2)= - X(t1)
Both version of X(t2) have the exact same marginal distribution, but the joint distributions P(X(t1),X(t2)) (and hence the conditional distributions) are very different. You can't infer the joint distribution from marginals.

function [] = TRProbsFromCDF02()
%UNTITLED Summary of this function goes here
%   Detailed explanation goes here
%lognormal SDE is  dlog(X)=mu0 dt -.5*sigma0^2*dt+sigma0*dZ(t)
% with X0 as the starting point.
%We find the transition probability between X(T1) grid and X(T1) Grid from
%CDF-Equivalent Brownian motion method. 
X0=1.0;%Start of lognormal desnity. If you change this a lot you might have to tweak with the X1 and X2 grids.
mu0=.2;%mean associated with
sigma0=.75;
T1=.5;%Time horizon for the first Grid.
N=200;%Grid points for the first X1 grid at time t1 or T1
dX1=.06*sigma0*sqrt(T1);
X1(1:N)=exp((1:N)*dX1-N/2*dX1)
%Above is the time 1 or T1 grid for the lognormal distribution
X1_Starts(1)=0;
X1_Starts(2:N-1)=.5*X1(1:N-2)+.5*X1(2:N-1);%Start of the nth subdivision for grid at time T1.

%Above, Calculate the CDF associated with each subdivision on T2 Grid.

    %time 2,T2

BR2(2:M-1)=.5*BR2Ends(1:M-2)+.5*BR2Ends(2:M-1);

I have posted the research paper on SSRN here: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm ... id=3119980
The title of the paper is: Calculation of Transition Probabilities of SDEs only From the Knowledge of Marginal Probabilities using the CDF-Equivalent Brownian Motion Method.
The abstract is given as: In this article, we show how to calculate the conditional and transition probabilities of any SDE between two different points across time only from the knowledge of their marginal probabilities on these two time grids. Briefly, we construct CDF-Equivalent standard Brownian motion grids from the knowledge of marginal probabilities of the SDE. The solution of conditional and transition probability of the SDE is the found from the solution of transition probabilities between the corresponding nodes of the CDF-Equivalent standard Brownian motion grids which can be easily calculated. What is the most interesting is that we do not need to know the SDE dynamics for calculation of conditional probabilities and the method requires only the knowledge of marginal probabilities. We can, in fact, use this method to determine the SDE that is best suited as a data generating process.
