Friends, I am posting two matlab functions. First is LegendrePoly which calculates coefficients of Legendre polynomials upto any arbitrary order of the polynomial and returns a matrix of coefficients. Second function converts a simple polynomial to a Legendre series of the same order just like we did for hermite polynomials in previous post.

Code: Select all
function [Lu] = LegendrePoly(Order)
Lu(1:Order+1,1:Order+1)=0.0;
Lu(1,1)=1.0;
Lu(2,2)=1.0;
for n=2:Order
    
    for m=1:n
       Lu(n+1,m+1)=Lu(n+1,m+1)+Lu(n,m)*(2*(n-1)+1)/((n-1)+1);
    end
    for m=1:n-1
       Lu(n+1,m)=Lu(n+1,m)-Lu(n-1,m)*(n-1)/((n-1)+1);
    end
end
Lu
end

Friends, I kept thinking about the problem of Schordinger equation with a series solution. I realized that it is not necessary that we force an orthogonal series with undetermined coefficients into Schordinger equation. Keeping series orthogonal all the time is  messy and requires a lot of work. We can try to use just a series with independent set of basis and force it into Schordinger equation and try to find its solution by finding values of undetermined coefficients. So I basically used a series as
[$]R(r)=\text{a0}+\text{a1} r+\text{a2} r^2+\text{a3} r^3+\text{a4} r^4+\text{a5} r^5+\text{a6} r^6+\text{a7} r^7[$]
and 
[$]T(\text{theta})=\text{b0}+\text{b1} \cos (\text{theta}) +\text{b2} \cos ^2(\text{theta})+\text{b3} \cos ^3(\text{theta})+\text{b4} \cos ^4(\text{theta})+\text{b5} \cos ^5(\text{theta}) \\ +\text{b6} \cos ^6(\text{theta})+\text{b7} \cos ^7(\text{theta})[$]
and
[$]F(\text{phi})=c \exp (i \, m \, \text{phi})[$]

I forced the product  [$]R[r] \, T[theta]\, F[phi][$] in the radial single electron Schordinger equation and took derivatives on the above series. derivatives of T(theta) required some extra manipulation to keep everything in powers of Cos(x) as sine terms were converted into cos terms and we kept a polynomials in cos(x) after differentiation of the equation.
I think each independent basis or all the products of independent basis should satisfy the Schordinger equation individually when we collect all the relevant terms with a certain basis or products of basis. I did all the calculations with mathematica and after taking derivatives, I used mathematica command "Collect" to get the expression in powers of r and cos(theta) and exponential terms which are independent basis for the problem. It is a long expression and I am trying to make something out of it but I have started to realize that it can be very meaningful and may lead to solution of Schordinger equation.
