Two Dimensional Z-Series/Hermite-Series:
Case 1:  Random variable X indepenent and Random Variable Y dependent and Conditional on X
Suppose we have two non-Gaussian continuous random variables, X and Y and we can make observations of their joint realizations. We suppose that X is the independent random variable and Y is the dependent random variable. This dependence might follow from causality but would usually be arbitrary for the analytics and we could possibly reverse the dependent and independent random variables and successfully re-do the analytics. 

We can calculate the univariate Z-series/Hermite-series of independent random variable X as

\[\,X(Z_x)\,=\, ah_0 \, +\, ah_1 \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, ah_2 \,H_2(Z_x) \,+\, ah_3 \,H_3(Z_x) \,+\,\ldots\]

Even though we can make a univariate Z-series/Hermite-Series of Y, this would not be helpful from two dimensional perspective. We just define a conditional version of Y as

\begin{align}

\left[Y|X\right](Z_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,&=\, \Big[\, cH_{0,0} \, + \, cH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, cH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{1,0} \, + \, cH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, cH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{2,0} \, + \, cH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, cH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align}

\begin{align}

\[ \,W(Z_y \, , \, Z_x)\, =\,\left[Y|X\right](Z_y \, , \, Z_x)\,\,X(Z_x)\,\]

Friends, In our 2D Framework, there are two basic building blocks/variables. The first variable is univariate Z-series of independent variable X that is mapped on a single independent Gaussian \(Z_x\) . And the second variable is Y which is dependent on both \(Z_x\) and \(Z_y\) and has a 2D Z-series.

The univariate Z-series of X is given as (equivalently, we could have specified Hermite-series)

\[\,X(Z_x)\,=\, a_0 \, +\, a_1 \,Z_x \,+\, a_2 \,{Z_x}^2 \,+\, a_3 \,{Z_x}^3+\, \ldots \]

This can be written more briefly as

\[\,X(Z_x)\,\,=\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, a_{m}\,{Z_{x}}^m  \] 

The bivariate conditional Hermite-Series of X conditional on Y is given as (please note that \(b_{m,n}\) are numerical coefficients). In matlab indices of arrays start at one so in actual programming the indices below are offset by one.
By construction different underlying Gaussians that appear in 2D Z-series are orthogonal and independent.

\begin{align}
\left[Y|X\right](Z_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,&=\, \Big[\, b_{0,0} \, + \, b_{0,1} \,Z_x \,+\, b_{0,2} \, {Z_x}^2 \,\, + \, b_{0,3} \,{Z_x}^3\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, b_{1,0} \, + \, b_{1,1} \,Z_x \,+\, b_{1,2} \, {Z_x}^2 \, + \, b_{1,3} \,{Z_x}^3\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, Z_y \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, b_{2,0} \, + \, b_{2,1} \,{Z_x} \,+\, b_{2,2} \, {Z_x}^2 \, \, + \, b_{2,3} \,{Z_x}^3\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, {Z_y}^2 \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 


\[\,\left[Y|X\right](Z_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,=\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, b_{n,m}\,{Z_{y}}^n\,{Z_{x}}^m\,\] 

Expected Value of a 2D Z-series is given as
 
\[\,E\left[Y|X\right](Z_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,=\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, b_{n,m}\,E[{Z_{y}}^n]\,E[{Z_{x}}^m]\,\] 

For example pth moment of 2D Z-series [Y|X\right](Z_y \, , \, Z_x) is found by repeated series multiplication of Y by itself and then taking expectation over all Gaussians at the final series.

\[\,E\left[Y|X\right]^p(Z_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,=\, E\left[ {\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, b_{n,m}\,{Z_{Y}}^n\,{Z_{X}}^m\,\right)}^p\right] \] 

Cross moments of X and Y are calculated as repeated series products with themselves to find appropriate powers of X and Y as series  and then a final cross product between these series followed by taking expectation over powers of two Gaussians in the 2D Series.

\[\,E\left[Y^p\,X^q\, \right](Z_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,=\, E\left[ {\left(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\, b_{n,m}\,{Z_{Y}}^n\,{Z_{X}}^m\,\right)}^p   \, {\left( \sum\limits_{l=0}^{L}\, a_{l}\,{Z_{x}}^l \, \right)}^q  \right] \] 

Since all of these calculations are series multiplications on which we later take expectations when necessary, I will take a brief stop to explain the matlab function that handles 2D Series multiplications. I have attached this program at the end.

Programming Notes:

The name of this function that handles multiplications of two 2D Z-series and takes expectation of the product is function "SeriesProduct2DAlgorithmicNew".

Here is the header of the function:
function [cc,EX] =SeriesProduct2DAlgorithmicNew(aa,bb,SeriesOrder10,SeriesOrder01,SeriesOrder20,SeriesOrder02)

It multiplies two 2D or 1D Z-series. aa and bb are both two dimensional arrays that carry the Coefficients of each of the two 2D Z-series that are  being multiplied. Let us have two Z-series. 

\[\,Y(Z_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,=\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{5}\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{7}\, aa_{n,m}\,{Z_{y}}^n\,{Z_{x}}^m\,\] 

The above series has OrderY=5 and OrderX=7. So SeriesOrder10=OrderY+1=6  And  SeriesOrder01=OrderX+1=8

We want to multiply it with another Z-series that is just univariate and is given as: \[\,X(Z_x)\,\,=\, \sum\limits_{m=0}^{5}\, bb_{m}\,{Z_{x}}^m  \]

This univariate Z-series has SeriesOrder20= OrderY+1 = 0+ 1=1  And  SeriesOrder02= OrderX+1 = 5+ 1=6

We input these arguments  \(aa_{n,m}\), \(bb_{m}\), SeriesOrder10, SeriesOrder01, SeriesOrder20, SeriesOrder02 into the above function to get the output 2D Z-series and its expectation.

The Series with coefficients cc that would be output will have SeriesOrder30=SeriesOrder10 + SeriesOrder20 -1  And  SeriesOrder03=SeriesOrder01 + SeriesOrder02 -1 . Its coefficients range then would  be in matlab notation as  cc(1:SeriesOrder30, 1:SeriesOrder03)

The output variable cc contains coefficients of the resulting product 2D Z-series and output variable EX will give expectation of the resulting product 2D Z-series.
