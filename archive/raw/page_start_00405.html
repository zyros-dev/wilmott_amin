Now you can download the new better written version of my paper here at SSRN: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm ... id=2872598
...
system of three non-linear second order ordinary differential equations.
Ok, we still have no clear statement (that I could find) that indicated when one might want to use this method over any of the many others out there, but I think I understand what is going on now--at least to the point where the equivalence with the perturbative approach is easy to show.
It wasn't clear to me in the last version of the paper whether there was some approximation going on to obtain the series expansion, but it is clear to me now that there isn't.  Equation (5) will (formally) solve the ode -- just by the fundamental theorem of (integral) calculus.

Where I was starting was an iteration of the solution into itself, like:
\[ y(t)=y(t_0)+\epsilon \int_{t_0}^tdsf\left(s,y(t_0)+\epsilon \int_{t_0}^sduf(u,y(u))\right). \]
Then you keep iterating forever... and you get the perturbative solution by performing a Taylor series in [$]\epsilon[$].  However, this is actually equal to Equation (5) from the paper (putting the [$]\epsilon[$]'s in):
\[ y(t)
=y(t_0)
+\epsilon\int_{t_0}^tdsf\left(s,y(t_0)\right)
+\epsilon^2\int_{t_0}^tds
\int_{t_0}^s du
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(s,y(u))
f\left(u,y(u))\right) \]

Now you play the same game with the integrand in the last term on the right (like Equation (6) from the paper), which leaves this term but with [$]y(t_0)[$], and brings the next order term multiplied by [$]\epsilon^3[$], and so on.  Series expansion in [$]\epsilon[$].

So my conclusion at this point is: For ode's the method is equivalent to a very well known series solution, and therefore adds nothing new.

(Of course all of the above assumes really `nice' functions, but that's ok with me as a start -- if it doesn't work there it isn't likely to work anywhere.)

P.S. I will leave the ``my degree is bigger than yours'' discussion to the rest of you. 
How does the above (regular, not singular) perturbation expansion tackle my counterexample?
