I am copying a part of my previous post in which I have modified the equation somewhat.

Please recall that in order to construct a Z-series, we mapped a sorted array of data values of Y on a discrete Z-grid. We did a regression of data Y on Z-grid to find one dimensional constant coefficients of the Z-series. However, we can make this local expansion in terms of Taylor series around each data point. For example if there are P data points and we have an underlying Z-grid of P points, we can write the derivatives of local Z-series around each pth data point as

\[ Y_p(Z_y)\,=\,a_{0,p}\,+\,a_{1,p}\, Z_{y,p}\,+\,a_{2,p}\, {Z_{y,p}}^2\,+\,a_{3,p}\, {Z_{y,p}}^3\,+\,a_{4,p}\, {Z_{y,p}}^4\,+\,a_{5,p}\, {Z_{y,p}}^5\,\]

So all the p data values take p different coefficients on the Z-grid of P values in total where p=1,..,P . 

So all these coefficients and their derivatives are locally stochastic and can be regressed on \(Z_x\). We can also understand that \(\,a_{1,p}\,\) is associated with first local derivative of \(Y_p\) with respect to \(Z_{y,p}\). Similarly  \(a_{n,p}\) is associated with nth local derivative of  \(Y_p\) with respect to \(Z_{y,p}\). We can either regress   \(a_{n,p}\) on corresponding values of \(Z_{x,p}\) or find cross moments between  \(a_{n,p}\) and \(X_p\) to find out its Z-series coefficients with respect to \(Z_x\) in the form of a Z-series. We can separate out derivatives of local coefficients and find their moments with X or use regression and in this way we can separately find Z-series of each coefficients term separately by separating the data into derivatives.

Friends, I am working on solution of 2D conditional Z-series of random variable Y given 1D Z-series of random variable X from cross moments of X and Y. I have not completed everything but I have a reasonably well thought out strategy that I want to share in details here. 

Before I describe the strategy, I want to mention that it is based on hermite-correlations of hermite polynomials of \(Z_y\) underlying random variable Y and hermite polynomials of \(Z_x\) underlying random variable X. Especially for higher polynomials, it is difficult to find these correlations perfectly correctly especially since when calculated from Z underlying a Z-series, usually the higher polynomials might not have proper variance and due to this there might be small spurious  correlations between hermite polynomials  of different order (across two random variables) so this part has to be tackled with care. One way to do this would be to back out hrermite correlations from the cross moments themselves. I am working on this but all of this can take more than a week and therefore I am sharing the details of the solution strategy as I understand with people who have been following my research.

Again we are trying to find solution of 2D conditional Z-series(that implicitly gives the conditional distributions) of random variable Y given 1D Z-series of random variable X from cross moments of X and Y. We are given univariate Z-series of both random variable X and random variable Y.

I am not giving introduction to 1D Z-series and hermite-series and other basics. For that please see my paper  https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4933283

We can have two hermite-series of two different random variables of possibly different order given as

\[V(Z_v)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,H_n(Z_v)\,\]

\[U(Z_u)\,=\,\sum\limits_{m=0}^{M}\,b_m\,H_m(Z_u)\,\]

if correlations between nth hermite is given as  \(\rho_n=<H_n(Z_v),H_n(Z_u)>\), we have that (if N<M, in case M<N, we will replace N with M in formula below) then cross moments between V and U (here only first cross moment given)

\[E \left[VU\right]\,=\,\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,b_n\,\rho_n\, \,n! \]

For higher order cross moments, we would have to first expand the Z-series polynomial to appropriate power,  convert to hermite-series and then apply the above formula.

If needed, we can back out hermite correlations from the above cross-moments.

Our 2D conditional Hermite series of Y given X is of the form

\begin{align}
\left[Ydecorr|X\right](\tilde{Z}_y \, , \, Z_x)\, \,&=\, \, \Big[ \, b_{0,0} \, + \, b_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, b_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, b_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \\
&+\, \, \Big[ \, b_{1,0} \, + \, b_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, b_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, b_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \,H_1(\tilde{Z}_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, b_{2,0} \, + \, b_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, b_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, b_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(\tilde{Z}_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, b_{3,0} \, + \, b_{3,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, b_{3,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, b_{3,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_3(\tilde{Z}_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

In the above hermite-series, the first row gives conditional dependence in the form of univariate hermite polynomials of \(Z_x\) and this goes to conditional calculation of mean. The first column gives conditional dependence on univariate polynomial of  \(\tilde{Z}_y\). This first row and first column are more important and once hermite-correlations between hermite polynomials of \(Z_x\) and \(Z_y\) are known, we can calculate both this first row and first column more easily. Please note that \(Z_y\) and  \(\tilde{Z}_y\) are different. The first one is associated with univariate Z-series of Y and is correlated with \(Z_x\) and the second  \(\tilde{Z}_y\) consists of decomposition of \(Z_y\) that is orthogonal to \(Z_x\). 

We start with hermite-series of Y and X as 

\[Y(Z_y)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,H_n(Z_y)\]
\[X(Z_x)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,b_n\,H_n(Z_x)\]

We will first turn this Hermite-series of Y into a Hermite-series conditional on X and then we will recursively add the result of (cross-moments) of all powers of Y and alter the first power so that it properly retrieves correlations associated with all higher moments. We start with taking correlations in the first moment and univariate Hermite-Series of Y given as  \(Y(Z_y)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,H_n(Z_y)\) is converted into first moment conditional form as

 \[Y(Z_y)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,\sqrt{1-\,{\rho_n}^2}\,H_n(\tilde{Z}_y)\,+\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,\rho_n \,H_n(Z_x)\]
\[=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,c_n\,\,H_n(\tilde{Z}_y)\,+\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,d_n\, \,H_n(Z_x)\]

The above is first order conditional form in which we want to recursively add the effect of higher order hermite correlations from higher moments one by one. I describe that in next post.

Since this method is easier to follow, some friends would prefer it on the full blown 2D Z-series solution. I am sure to come back later in a few days and present a solution to 2D Z-series problem as well but today I will only present the solution to finding conditional distribution from the simpler method. I am sure this simple method can also be used with multivariate conditional densities when a random variable is conditional on several other random variables. But I am presenting only two variable version here and will come to multivariate version of this simple method later after working out the details.

The very simple idea behind the new method is to find conditional moments of Y given X and then use these conditional moments in a one dimensional setting to construct the 1D conditional Z-series. These conditional moments of Y given X can be found easily by knowledge of Z-series/Hermite-series of Y and knowledge of all the hermite correlations.

For example 1D Hermite series of Y and X is given as

\[Y(Z_y)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,H_n(Z_y)\]
\[X(Z_x)\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,b_n\,H_n(Z_x)\]
 and correlation between respective hermite polynomials is given as
\[\rho_n=<H_n(Z_y),H_n(Z_x)>\]

We can find expected value of nth conditional moment of Y given X after raising Z-series of Y to nth power and then substituting the correlations. The expectation of correlated part is the nth conditional moment of Y given X.

First expected moment of Y given X is given as

\[E\left[Y|X=X_0\right]\,=\,\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\,a_n\,\rho_n\, \big[ H_n(Z_x) | Z_x=Z_{x_0}\,\big]\]

Similalrly, we can find expected values of all higher moments after calculating their Z-series. If the Z-series of mth moments is given as
\[Y^m(Z_y)\,=\,\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N*m}\,c_n\,\, H_n(Z_y)\]

Then the mth conditional moment of Y given X is calculated as

\[E\left[Y^m|X=X_0\right]\,=\,\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N*m}\,c_n\,\rho_n\, \big[ H_n(Z_x) | Z_x=Z_{x_0}\,\big]\]

We can simply use these univariate moments to find the conditional density of Y given X using the method we have discussed earlier especially in my paper https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4933283 to calculate the conditional density.

With some hack, the method can be extended to multivariate conditional expectations but I will write about them later tonight or tomorrow.

Friends, conditional expectation of moments of Y given correlations with several random variables  is a problem just like conditional regression in which effective correlation is used (controlling for the effect of correlations with other random variables) for calculation of regression coefficients. I had written a post about hermite regression with correlations earlier here (Post 1732) : https://forum.wilmott.com/viewtopic.php?t=99702&start=1725#p874542 We will have to use the matrix formula for calculations of effective correlations separately for each hermite polynomial.

Very very briefly, Then the mth conditional moment of Y given K random variables \(X_1, X_2, X_3, \ldots \,,X_K\) is calculated by an equation of the form

\[E\left[Y^m|X_1=X_{1_0},X_2=X_{2_0}, \ldots, X_K=X_{K_0} \right]\,=\,\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N*m}\, \sum\limits_{k=1}^{K} c_n \,\,\beta_{n,k}\, \big[ H_n(Z_{k}) | Z_{k}=Z_{k_0}\,\big]\] 

Please let me note that \(\beta_{n,k}\) are not hermite correlations as described earlier and are effective correlations controlling for the effect of other correlations and have to be found from matrix inverse of Hermite correlations matrix as described in post 1732 above. Please also note that there is a different set of hermite correlations matrix associated with each order of hermite polynomial that has to be inverted separately for finding effective correlations for that polynomial of all explaining Z_k 's.

Again all these conditional moments can be used to determine the univariate conditional density/distribution of Y given K random variables \(X_1, X_2, X_3, \ldots \,,X_K\).
