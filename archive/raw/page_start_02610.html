Friends, we have to move to real stocks data in a few days so I had been brainstorming about various indicators in the markets and other things related to algorithmic trading. 


We would be thinking about algorithmic trading and divide the whole thing into three parts.

1. Know your Market Type.
2. What type of strategies to use for various market types.
3. What are the most suitable instruments for particular type of market types and particular market strategies.


We will first work for more than a month mostly trying to understand the market type so I will give very brief thoughts about it. These ideas are initial and tentative and once we deal with real data many of these things would slightly change. But these are ideas from initial brainstorming as we embark on the project.

Know Your Market Type.

I want to make a multi-dimensional indicator for the market.  We will calculate these indicators and their variances. First dimension could be whether market is bearish or bullish on a scale of -1 to +1. Second dimension could be whether market is low volatility or high volatility on a scale of zero to 1. Third dimension would be whether market is low mean-reverting or high mean reverting on similar scale possibly zero to one. There could be other dimensions like volume and liquidity in both sides of the market. 

We will use various indicators including our recent work on moments to give a single statistic to all of the above dimensions related to market type and also calculate variance of each statistic.

As a background and in order to get oriented, I would request friends to read the follwing two posts: https://forum.wilmott.com/viewtopic.php?t=99702&start=2385#p882431  and https://forum.wilmott.com/viewtopic.php?t=99702&start=2385#p882455


let us suppose we have financial data of equities on a 15 sec grid (My data is for 41 working days from May and June of 2021). We have a universe of M stocks and The price of mth stock at time t is given as [$]X_m(t)[$]. We convert these stock values into log returns so they are easy to use in our set up.

[$]R_m(t)\,=\, \log(X_m(t))\,-\, \log(X_m(t-1))[$]

We can make very intelligent indicators using log returns from universe of M stocks.

We can make an indicator for market across the M stocks over four 15 second periods as


[$]IM_{state}(t)\, =\, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, w_{m} \, \Large{ \frac{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{Lag-1} \, R_m(t-k)\,}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{Lag-1} \,{\Big[ {R_m(t-k)}\,-\,\sum\limits_{k=0}^{Lag-1} \, R_m(t-k)\,/Lag \,\Big]}^2}}}[$] 


The above indicator has sum of returns of each stock in numerator and square root of their variance in denominator. But while making a single statistic for market state, we would also have to alter the weights of the stocks and make them inversely proportional to square root of variance of statistic of each stock in the sample so that low standard deviation stock statistics get high weight and high standard deviation statistics of stocks get a low weight when we form a single market indicator. This is done by defining  [$]w_m[$]  as


[$]\, w_m \, =\, \LARGE{ \frac{ \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{Lag-1} \,{\Big[ {R_m(t-k)}\,-\,\sum\limits_{k=0}^{Lag-1} \, R_m(t-k)\,/Lag \,\Big]}^2}}}{ \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{Lag-1} \,{\Big[ {R_m(t-k)}\,-\,\sum\limits_{k=0}^{Lag-1} \, R_m(t-k)\,/Lag \,\Big]}^2}}}}[$]  

We can write the above indicator in shorthand as

[$]IM_{state}(t)\, =\, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, w_{m} \, \Large{ \frac{E \left[ R_m\right]\,}{\sqrt{\, Var\left[R_m\right]}}}[$] 

and 

[$]\, w_m \, =\, \LARGE{ \frac{ \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\, Var\left[R_m\right]}}}{ \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\, Var\left[R_m\right]}}}}[$]  

We can have indicators for variance, skew and kurtosis of the market based like just the above indicator. For example indicator for market variance could be given as

[$]IM_{var}(t)\, =\, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, w_{2,m} \, \Large{ \frac{Var \left[ R_m\right]\,}{\sqrt{\, Var\left[Var\left[R_m\right]\right]}}}[$] 

and 

[$]\, w_{2,m} \, =\, \LARGE{ \frac{ \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\, \,Var\left[Var\left[R_m\right]\right]}}}{ \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\,Var\left[ Var\left[R_m\right]\right]}}}}[$]

Similarly for skew and kurtosis, we could have

[$]IM_{skew}(t)\, =\, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, w_{3,m} \, \Large{ \frac{Skew \left[ R_m\right]\,}{\sqrt{\, Var\left[Skew\left[R_m\right]\right]}}}[$] 

and 

[$]\, w_{3,m} \, =\, \LARGE{ \frac{ \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\, \,Var\left[Skew\left[R_m\right]\right]}}}{ \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\,Var\left[ Skew\left[R_m\right]\right]}}}}[$]

and

[$]IM_{kurtosis}(t)\, =\, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, w_{4,m} \, \Large{ \frac{Kurtosis \left[ R_m\right]\,}{\sqrt{\, Var\left[Kurtosis\left[R_m\right]\right]}}}[$] 

and 

[$]\, w_{4,m} \, =\, \LARGE{ \frac{ \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\, \,Var\left[Kurtosis\left[R_m\right]\right]}}}{ \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\,Var\left[ Kurtosis\left[R_m\right]\right]}}}}[$]

and for the intensity of mean reversion, we want to use a special indicator
 

[$]IM_{MR}(t)\, =\, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, w_{5,m} \, \Large{ \frac{   \frac{Kurtosis \left[ R_m\right]\,}{Var \left[ R_m\right]\,}}{\sqrt{\, Var\left[\frac{Kurtosis \left[ R_m\right]\,}{Var \left[ R_m\right]\,}\right]}}}[$] 

and 

[$]\, w_{5,m} \, =\, \LARGE{ \frac{ \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\, Var\left[\frac{Kurtosis \left[ R_m\right]\,}{Var \left[ R_m\right]\,}\right]}}}{ \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, \sqrt{\, Var\left[\frac{Kurtosis \left[ R_m\right]\,}{Var \left[ R_m\right]\,}\right]}}}[$]

We can also calculate variances of above indicators in both time and across the stock spectrum and use associated standard deviations for confidence limits.

Friends, these are tentatively brainstormed form of indicators and are framed analogously to our first order indicator to market state and these indicators might undergo many changes as we play with data and our final version might be very different.

In the main indicator equation, we have taken  \(Var \left[ R_m\right]\,\) but we are already dealing with log-returns. Recalling the definition of \(R_m\), we have 

\[R_m(t)\,=\, \log(X_m(t))\,-\, \log(X_m(t-1))\]

So Variance of the market is obviously better reflected by second moment of log-returns as opposed to variance of log-returns. We also have to consider that calculating variance takes out one degree of freedom out and calculating variance of variance takes out two degrees of freedom out in a small sample. So a better suggested form of market indicator could be one of the following

\[IM_{var}(t)\, =\, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, w_{2,m} \, \Large{ \frac{E \left[ {R_m}^2\right]\,}{\sqrt{\, Var\left[{R_m}^2\,\right]}}}\] 

where
\[ E \left[ {R_m}^2\right]\,=\,\frac{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{Lag-1} \, {R_m}^2(t-k)\,}{\, Lag} \]
and
\[ Var \left[ {R_m}^2\right]\,=\,\frac{\sum\limits_{k=0}^{Lag-1} \, \left[{R_m}^2(t-k)\,-\,E \left[ {R_m}^2\right]\, \right]^2}{\, Lag}\]
and 
\[\, w_{2,m} \, =\, \LARGE{ \frac{ \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\, \,Var\left[ {R_m}^2\,\right]}}}{ \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\,Var\left[{R_m}^2\,\right]}}}}\]


Similarly, we would like to slightly alter the previous indicators of skew and kurtosis as

and 

\[\, w_{3,m} \, =\, \LARGE{ \frac{ \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\, \,Var\left[E\left[{R_m}^3\right]\right]}}}{ \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \,\, \frac{\,1\,}{\sqrt{\,Var\left[ {R_m}^3\,\right]}}}}\]

and

and

and for the intensity of mean reversion, we want to use a special indicator

Another idea is to calculate "occupation time probability" of the market over some pre-specified time. We could divide the stock axis between maximum and low (over our time range interval) into an equi-spaced grid or equi-spaced bin. We suppose that there are N intervals of time \(\Delta t\). We start from starting time and add 1/N in the appropriate bin based on the values of X at the end of first interval. If this value of  stock X at the end of first interval is \(X_1\), we add 1/N in the grid cell/bin in which \(X_1\) lies. And so for all values of \(X_n(t_n)\) at the end of time \(t_n\) we continue to add weight 1/N in the appropriate bin based on which bin \(X_n\) falls in. So this way, we would have a density of relative time spent in each grid cell/bin over the time involved and we could also graph relative time spent VS stock value anywhere between high and low.

We could also calculate occupation time density of stocks on all sort of recent time intervals from a few days to 5-10 minutes.

Friends, I have been thinking more about determining mean-reversion in the market. I have some raw thoughts and I think lot of this would have to be finessed when I would deal with actual data.

First we have to take a toy example of addition of variance of a stochastic process across two periods. Suppose we call first interval part of stochastic process as \(X_{t_1}\) and the stochastic process across second part as \(X_{t_2}\). If we add variance across two parts of the stochastic process the resulting formula is

\[ Var \left[\,X_{t_1}\,+\,X_{t_2}\,\right]\,=\,Var\left[X_{t_1}\right] \,+\,Var\left[X_{t_2}\right] \,+2\,\rho_{X_{t_1},X_{t_2}}\,\,Var\left[X_{t_1}\right] \,\,Var\left[X_{t_2}\right] \, \]

If we can devise a numerical algorithm that can calculate all of the variances \(Var \left[\,X_{t_1}\,+\,X_{t_2}\,\right]\,\), \(Var \left[\,X_{t_1}\,\right]\,\) and  \(Var \left[\,X_{t_2}\,\right]\,\), we can calculate the correlation and associated mean reversion. Specifically if market is highly mean-reverting and correlation would be significiantly smaller smaller than zero and in that case  \(Var \left[\,X_{t_1}\,+\,X_{t_2}\,\right]\,\), would be significantly smaller than sum of the two variances given by \(Var \left[\,X_{t_1}\,\right]\,\) and  \(Var \left[\,X_{t_2}\,\right]\,\) and vice versa. 

We can devise a numerical method around above algorithm that finds mean reversion by calculating variances in multiple intervals and their sum as described in the above post. Most important thing would be that we would have to aggregate across all stocks to find an average mean reversion indicative of mean-reversion regime and and strength of mean reversion prevailing in the whole market. We would also have to determine mean-reversion on different scales.

Friends, even though first order statistics that I called Market State that we calculated last year and gave a reference now in first two posts of this page, worked excellently, it is not possible to make calculation of descriptive statistic of higher market moments without including hermite correlations and hermite polynomials (or some other appropriate form of correlations) and we simply cannot do univariate type of additions for higher moments analogously to what we did for first order moment. 

I intend to calculate market variance and standardized market skew and standardized market kurtosis and these three higher moments when combined with our earlier calculated market state statistic, should be reasonably enough to know our market type and comparisons across similar statistics of each stock. For example when standardized skew is above or below a threshold with significant value of market state  in that direction (and reasonably high standardized kurtosis), we could tell that market is trending. Friends, this post is about calculation of moments of a weighted basket of stocks that we would need in our analytics. When data is given, we need to find all correlations and expectations involved from the data to find data moments. 

For example, please consider three random variables W, X and Y.

\[E{\left[W\,+\,X\,+\,Y\right]^2} \,=\,E\left[W^2 \,\right]\, + 2 E\left[W X\,\right]\, + E\left[X^2\,\right]\, + 2 E\left[W Y\,\right]\, + 2 E\left[X Y\,\right]\, + E\left[Y^2\,\right]\,\]

and 

\[E{\left[W\,+\,X\,+\,Y\right]^3} \,=\,E\left[W^3\,\right]\,+3 E\left[W^2 X\,\right]\,+3 E\left[W X^2\,\right]\,+E\left[X^3\,\right]\,+3 E\left[W^2 Y\,\right]\,+6 E\left[W X Y\,\right]\,\\+3 E\left[X^2 Y\,\right]\,+3 E\left[W Y^2\,\right]\,+3 E\left[X Y^2\,\right]\,+E\left[Y^3\,\right]\,\]

and so on for higher moments.

All the expectations of variables and products of variables on the Right Hand Side of above equations can be calculated from data observations as summations over data observations divided by number of observations. When above variables are changing all the time, in order to calculate exact values of moments, we need to find all the above expectations from the given relevant data observations involved. Any pre-calculated expectations or correlations would alter the calculated moment values on LHS of above equations from true moments corresponding to data observations. We can find these expectations from data to calculate the exact values of moments on LHS of above equations.

In context of stock trading, we would like to find the above moments and involved expectations from data of most recent observations as we did earlier for finding market state.

The example of profits from GOOG is rather unrealistic since you cannot trade a lot of hundred shares (I just specified a commission of .35 cents which might not apply in the case of smaller lots.) Especially market impact is not accounted for. And markets now might have moved from dynamics several years ago when I bought this data. GOOG had a large share price and trading it in sizable lots would really move its market, so this has to be accounted for.
So please try the program with your own version of market (constituent stocks in the calculation of market), and your own likely commissions and you could possibly add impact in the profit calculation equations to account for all these variables.

For a stock to be viable for fast trading profit per trade should be a bit larger than one cent if you could easily trade hundred shares lots. The only requirement is that two way trade profits in the market exceed the commissions. IB charges .35 cents, so if you can maintain more than seventy cents in the live market or maintain per trade profits larger than any other relevant commission structure, you would be making money.
