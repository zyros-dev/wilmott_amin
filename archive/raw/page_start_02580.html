Friends, here is the result of my research today. I was able to get my program to work for applying multi-dimensional Newton Root Search for finding multi-dimensional coefficients backed out from Z-series of  X^2.

I calculated jacobian matrix for multidimensional Newton in the usual way. However instead of original Newton method as given below

\[X_{n+1}\,=\,X_n\,-\, {J_F(X_n)}^{-1}\,F(X_n) \]

I replaced it with Newton in least square sense 

\[X_{n+1}\,=\,X_n\,-\, \left[ \,{J_F(X_n)}^T\,J_F(X_n)\right]^{-1}\,{J_F(X_n)}^T\,F(X_n) \]

but it was still not working since there were zeros on some diagonal entries and I changed the matrix inversion to as in ridge regression as given below

\[X_{n+1}\,=\,X_n\,-\, \left[ \,{J_F(X_n)}^T\,J_F(X_n) \, + \alpha I \right]^{-1}\,{J_F(X_n)}^T\,F(X_n) \]

and then it started to converge when I applied it to calibrate it to Z-series of \(X^2\).

I still have to add the code for calibration to \(X^3\) and \(X^4\) as I had originally intended but I am sure that it will only make the problem solution more robust. Dynamic Coefficients Hermite Polynomial Series Models For Time Series Evolution of Random Variables

 In this brief note we want to discuss hermite polynomial series representation of random variables where coefficients of hermite polynomials are also represented by another hermite polynomial series for each coefficient.

We give some example of some practical dynamic hermite series coefficients models below  

\[\, X \,=\, a_{0}(t)\, + \, \, a_{1}(t)\, \, Z\, + \, \, a_{2}(t)\,\, \left( Z^2\,-\,1\,\right) + \, \, a_{3}(t)\, \, \left( Z^3\,-\,3 \,Z\,\right)    \] 
where
\[\, a_{0}(t)\, =\, \kappa_1\, \left(a_{00} \,-\,a_{0}(t-1)\, \right)\,+\, \sigma_0 \,Z_0\]
\[\, a_{1}(t)\, =\, \kappa_2\,\left(a_{10} \,-\,a_{1}(t-1)\, \right)\,+\, \sigma_1 \,Z_1\]
\[\, a_{2}(t)\, =\, \kappa_3\,\left(a_{20} \,-\,a_{2}(t-1)\, \right)\,+\, \sigma_2 \,Z_2\]
\[\, a_{3}(t)\, =\, \kappa_4\,\left(a_{30} \,-\,a_{3}(t-1)\, \right)\,+\, \sigma_3 \,Z_3\]

We have specified dynamics to coefficients of only first four polynomials in the Z-series but coefficients with dynamics may increase or decrease as we learn from the data.  All of these \(Z_0\, \,Z_1, \, Z_2,\,Z_3 \) could possibly be positively or negatively correlated with each other but could also be correlated with \(\bar{Z}_0\, \,\bar{Z}_1, \, \bar{Z}_2,\, \bar{Z}_3 \) which are noises in dynamic coefficients of hermite series of another random variable.

Estimation Strategy For Dynamic Coefficients Hermite Polynomial Series Models For Time Series Evolution of Random Variables

As we understand, the estimation of a dynamic coefficients model is not possible without specifying variability in moments. However many (single statistic of) moments we may specify, it will always give us a time homogenous static/stationary distribution with more precision therefore dynamic coefficients model estimation could only be possible if we can capture the systematic variability in moments of the time series. 

With this understanding, we can specify a simple estimation methodology in which we have a sliding window in time and we re-estimate moments of a time series (or innovations of this time series) with each sliding movement of this window. And also estimate the coefficients of the accompanying Z-series/Hermite series. We can then map the time series of coefficients of Z-series on simple Ornstein-Uhlenbeck like autoregressive processes. Since all coefficients are found in the same order in time, we can also find correlations among driving noises of different auto-regressive equation of coefficients across one time series and across various different time series of coefficients of different random variables.

Any sliding window method could give rise to correlations due to overlap of data so we would have to understand what are correlations due to overlapping data and what are true correlations after accounting for the overlap of the data in the sliding window.

We would also like to estimate dynamics of daily changes in the random variables in the financial markets from the most recent intra-day data of the market.

I have this simple idea that we could estimate a set of moments \(M_1\) over N intervals first, and then calculate another related set of moments \(M_2\) over \(\text{N+1}\) intervals and then we might be able to retrieve pure moments associated with \(\text{(N+1)th}\) interval using moments calculus over set of moments \(M_1\) and \(M_2\) but this is just a rudimentary idea and I need to see if it can work. If it does work, we can possibly find Z-series associated with \(\text{(N+1)th}\) interval directly from moments of \(\text{(N+1)th}\) interval but we might need some regularization and there might be some other caveats and I can only be sure if this idea works well in practice.

With the above simple methodology, we aim to estimate dynamic coefficients Z-series of various variables in the financial markets. I will come up with simple matlab programs in a few days.

We give some example of some practical dynamic hermite series coefficients models below  



\[\, X \,=\, a_{0}(t)\, + \, \, a_{1}(t)\, \, Z\, + \, \, a_{2}(t)\,\, \left( Z^2\,-\,1\,\right) + \, \, a_{3}(t)\, \, \left( Z^3\,-\,3 \,Z\,\right)    \] 

where

\[\, a_{0}(t)\,| \,a_{0}(t-1) \, =\, \kappa_1\, \left(a_{00} \,-\,a_{0}(t-1)\, \right)\,+\, \sigma_0 \,Z_0\]
\[\, a_{1}(t)\,| \,a_{1}(t-1) \,  =\, \kappa_2\,\left(a_{10} \,-\,a_{1}(t-1)\, \right)\,+\, \sigma_1 \,Z_1\]
\[\, a_{2}(t)\,| \,a_{2}(t-1) \,  =\, \kappa_3\,\left(a_{20} \,-\,a_{2}(t-1)\, \right)\,+\, \sigma_2 \,Z_2\]
\[\, a_{3}(t)\,| \,a_{3}(t-1) \,  =\, \kappa_4\,\left(a_{30} \,-\,a_{3}(t-1)\, \right)\,+\, \sigma_3 \,Z_3\]

However, since we would like to use our model in a lot of advanced applications where we might need even more precise analytics so I suggest that we have a general form of transition dynamics of all of parameters as

\[\, \Delta \theta_t \,=\, \theta_t\,- \,\theta_{t-1} \, =\, g(\theta_{t-1}) \,+\,f(\theta_{t-1}) \,Z_{\theta}\]

We can easily find the distribution of \(\theta\) and \(\Delta \theta_t \,\) from the time series of theta as we calculated yesterday and expand the above equation around median \(\theta_0\) of the distribution of \(\theta\) as

\[\, \Delta \theta_t \,=\, \theta_t\,- \,\theta_{t-1} \, =\, g(\theta_{t-1}) \,+\,f(\theta_{t-1}) \,Z_{\theta}\]
or more explicitly
\[\, \Delta \theta_{t_n} \,=\, g(\theta_0) \, + \, g'(\theta_0) \,(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)+ .5 \, g''(\theta_0) \,{(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)}^2\, + \\\, \left[ \, f(\theta_0) \, + \, f'(\theta_0) \,(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)+ .5 \, f''(\theta_0) \,{(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)}^2\,\right] \, Z_{\theta}\ \\
 \,=\, g_0 \, + \, g_1 \,(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)+ .5 \, g_2 \,{(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)}^2\,+\, \left[ \, f_0 \, + \, f_1 \,(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)+ .5 \, f_2 \,{(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)}^2\,\right] \, Z_{\theta}\]

In the above equation \(\theta_0\) is median of distribution of \(\theta\) and can be found by choosing the middle element in sorted values of \(\theta_t\).

We can find the above equation of transition of dynamic coefficients \(\theta_{t_n}\) by calculating moments of \(\Delta \theta_{t}\)  as 

\[\, \mu_k\,=\,\frac{1}{N}\, \sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\, {\left[\Delta \theta_{t_n} \right]}^k \]

We have to find six values of transition parameters \(g_0,\,g_1,\,g_2,\,f_0,\,f_1,\,f_2\) so that for six moments calculated above and given by \(\mu_k\,\) of index k as \(k\,=\,1,\,2,\,\ldots,6\), the equation below holds (We solve six moment equations to find six parameters given by \(g_0,\,g_1,\,g_2,\,f_0,\,f_1,\,f_2\)

\[\, \mu_k\,=\,\frac{1}{N}\, \sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\, {\left[\, g_0 \, + \, g_1 \,(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)+ .5 \, g_2 \,{(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)}^2\,+\, \left[ \, f_0 \, + \, f_1 \,(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)+ .5 \, f_2 \,{(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)}^2\,\right] \, Z_{\theta} \right]}^k \]

MATH AI Analytics: Applying a Function on a Different Function (Composition of Functions) To Follow a Third Target Function. 

Ok, coming towards the topic. Here is our setting. We have a function at hand denoted as \(F(X)\) and we have a target function denoted as \(G(X)\) . We suppose both of \(F(X)\) and \(G(X)\) are known to us or we can possibly calculate them easily by other methods. We want to apply a function \(V(.)\) on \(F(X)\) so that it starts to perfectly follow the target function \(G(X)\). We also suppose that functions are smooth and continuous and their derivatives of all orders of interest exist and can also be measured for both \(F(X)\) and \(G(X)\). 

We have to find the function V(.) so that the composition of functions equation below is perfectly satisfied.

\[V(F(X))\,=\,G(X)\]

Our strategy is simple, We apply Taylor series in X on both sides of the above equation and equate equation of each derivative on both sides separately. This way we can find all derivatives of \(V(.)\) of various orders of interest and be able to reconstruct required function \(V(.)\) to take \(F(X)\) to target function \(G(X)\) from its derivatives.

We expand \(G(X)\) as a Taylor series as

\[G(X)\,=\,G(\text{X0}) \,+(X-\text{X0}) G'(\text{X0})\, + \frac{1}{2} (X-\text{X0})^2 G''(\text{X0})\,+\frac{1}{6} G^{(3)}(\text{X0}) (X-\text{X0})^3 \,\\+ \frac{1}{24} G^{(4)}(\text{X0}) (X-\text{X0})^4+O\left((X-\text{X0})^5\right)\]

We also expand the composition of \(V(.)\) and \(F(.)\) as a Taylor series as

\[V(F(X))\,=\, V(F(\text{X0}))+(X-\text{X0}) F'(\text{X0}) V'(F(\text{X0}))+\frac{1}{2} (X-\text{X0})^2 \left(F''(\text{X0}) V'(F(\text{X0}))+F'(\text{X0})^2 V''(F(\text{X0}))\right)\\+\frac{1}{6} (X-\text{X0})^3 \left(F^{(3)}(\text{X0}) V'(F(\text{X0}))+V^{(3)}(F(\text{X0})) F'(\text{X0})^3+3 F'(\text{X0}) F''(\text{X0}) V''(F(\text{X0}))\right)\\+\frac{1}{24} (X-\text{X0})^4 \left(F^{(4)}(\text{X0}) V'(F(\text{X0}))+3 F''(\text{X0})^2 V''(F(\text{X0}))+V^{(4)}(F(\text{X0})) F'(\text{X0})^4 \\+4 F^{(3)}(\text{X0}) F'(\text{X0}) V''(F(\text{X0}))+6 V^{(3)}(F(\text{X0})) F'(\text{X0})^2 F''(\text{X0})\right)+O\left((X-\text{X0})^5\right)\]

Matching powers of  \((X-\text{X0})\) on both sides of equation, we get the following equations that we solve one by one as

\[\,G(\text{X0}) \,=\,V(F(\text{X0}))\]

\[(X-\text{X0}) G'(\text{X0})\,=\,(X-\text{X0}) F'(\text{X0}) V'(F(\text{X0}))\] From where it follows
\[ V'(F(\text{X0}))\,=\, \frac{G'(\text{X0})\,}{\, F'(\text{X0})} \]

Matching 2nd power of  \((X-\text{X0})^2\), we get

\[\frac{1}{2} (X-\text{X0})^2 G''(\text{X0})\,=\,\frac{1}{2} (X-\text{X0})^2 \left(F''(\text{X0}) V'(F(\text{X0}))+F'(\text{X0})^2 V''(F(\text{X0}))\right)\] From where it follows \[ V''(F(\text{X0}))= \,\frac{G''(\text{X0})\,-\left(F''(\text{X0}) V'(F(\text{X0})) \right) }{F'(\text{X0})^2}\]

Matching power of  \((X-\text{X0})^3\), we get

\[\frac{1}{6} G^{(3)}(\text{X0}) (X-\text{X0})^3 \,=\,\frac{1}{6} (X-\text{X0})^3 \left(F^{(3)}(\text{X0}) V'(F(\text{X0}))+V^{(3)}(F(\text{X0})) F'(\text{X0})^3+3 F'(\text{X0}) F''(\text{X0}) V''(F(\text{X0}))\right) \] From where it follows  \[  V^{(3)}(F(\text{X0}))  \,=\,\frac{G^{(3)}(\text{X0})  \,-\, \left(F^{(3)}(\text{X0}) V'(F(\text{X0})) +3 F'(\text{X0}) F''(\text{X0}) V''(F(\text{X0}))\right)} {F'(\text{X0})^3} \] 

Matching power of  \((X-\text{X0})^4\), we get

\[ \frac{1}{24} G^{(4)}(\text{X0}) (X-\text{X0})^4 \,=\, \frac{1}{24} (X-\text{X0})^4 \left(F^{(4)}(\text{X0}) V'(F(\text{X0}))+3 F''(\text{X0})^2 V''(F(\text{X0}))+V^{(4)}(F(\text{X0})) F'(\text{X0})^4 \\+4 F^{(3)}(\text{X0}) F'(\text{X0}) V''(F(\text{X0}))+6 V^{(3)}(F(\text{X0})) F'(\text{X0})^2 F''(\text{X0})\right)\] From where we get
\[ V^{(4)}(F(\text{X0})) \, =\,\frac{ \left[ G^{(4)}(\text{X0})  \,-\,  \left(F^{(4)}(\text{X0}) V'(F(\text{X0}))+3 F''(\text{X0})^2 V''(F(\text{X0}))+4 F^{(3)}(\text{X0}) F'(\text{X0}) V''(F(\text{X0})) \\+6 V^{(3)}(F(\text{X0})) F'(\text{X0})^2 F''(\text{X0})\right) \right]}{F'(\text{X0})^4} \]

So this way, we can calculate various different derivatives of \(V(.)\) and then reconstruct V(.) from there. Here we list the above calculated four derivatives of again, if we use the notation \(Y=F(X)\), then we can write, as

\[\, \,V(Y0)=V(F(\text{X0}))\,=\,G(\text{X0})\]
\[ V'(\text{Y0})\,=V'(F(\text{X0}))\,=\, \frac{G'(\text{X0})\,}{\, F'(\text{X0})} \]

Dynamic Coefficients Z-series Model and its Estimation

Among other possible variants, one form of practical dynamic hermite series coefficients models is given below  
\[\, X \,=\, a_{0}(t)\, + \, \, a_{1}(t)\, \, Z\, + \, \, a_{2}(t)\,\, \left( Z^2\,-\,1\,\right) + \, \, a_{3}(t)\, \, \left( Z^3\,-\,3 \,Z\,\right)    \] 
where
\[\, a_{0}(t)\,| \,a_{0}(t-1) \, =\, \kappa_1\, \left(a_{00} \,-\,a_{0}(t-1)\, \right)\,+\, \sigma_0 \,Z_0\]
\[\, a_{1}(t)\,| \,a_{1}(t-1) \,  =\, \kappa_2\,\left(a_{10} \,-\,a_{1}(t-1)\, \right)\,+\, \sigma_1 \,Z_1\]
\[\, a_{2}(t)\,| \,a_{2}(t-1) \,  =\, \kappa_3\,\left(a_{20} \,-\,a_{2}(t-1)\, \right)\,+\, \sigma_2 \,Z_2\]
\[\, a_{3}(t)\,| \,a_{3}(t-1) \,  =\, \kappa_4\,\left(a_{30} \,-\,a_{3}(t-1)\, \right)\,+\, \sigma_3 \,Z_3\]

we have a general form of transition dynamics of all of parameters as

\[\, \Delta \theta_t \,=\, \theta_t\,- \,\theta_{t-1} \, =\, g(\theta_{t-1}) \,+\,f(\theta_{t-1}) \,Z_{\theta}\]

We can easily find the distribution of \(\theta\) and \(\Delta \theta_t \,\) from the time series of theta as we calculated earlier. 

We can write Z-series representation of theta as

\[\theta(Z_{\theta}) \,=\, b_0 \, + \, b_1\,{Z_{\theta}}\,+\,b_2  \,{Z_{\theta}}^2\,+\,b_3\,{Z_{\theta}}^3\]

and we can also write unconditional Z-series representation of  \(\Delta \theta \,\) 

\[\Delta\theta(Z_{\Delta\theta}) \,=\, c_0 \, + \, c_1\,{Z_{\Delta\theta}}\,+\,c_2  \,{Z_{\Delta\theta}}^2\,+\,c_3\,{Z_{\Delta\theta}}^3\]

The above two equations would be used to find correlations and later to find conditional expectations of  \(E\left[\Delta\theta_t \,|\, \theta_{t-1} \right]\) 

 But at the same time, we suppose that conditional (on one period earlier interval start value of \(\theta\)) transition model form of distribution of  \(\Delta\theta \) could be given as  where we have divided \( \Delta \theta_t\) into two parts, the first part that is perfectly correlated with \(\theta_{t-1}\) and the second part that is orthogonal (but not necessarily independent of \(\theta_{t-1}\)  or 


\[\, \Delta \theta_t \,=\, \theta_t\,- \,\theta_{t-1} \, =\, E\left[\Delta\theta_t \,|\, \theta_{t-1} \right] \,+\, \bar{ \Delta \theta_t } \\
=\, g(\theta_{t-1}) \,+\,f(\theta_{t-1}) \,Z_{\bar{\Delta\theta}} \]


We want to notice that first drift part \(g(\theta_{t-1})\) is perfectly correlated wtih  \(\theta_{t-1}\)  while \(f(\theta_{t-1})\) in noise part also depends on \(\theta_{t-1}\) but the whole second term above is orthogonal to \(\theta_{t-1}\). 

We can write the form of Z-series of \(g(\theta)\) and \(f(\theta)\) , since they are one-dimensional functions of \(\theta\), as 

\[\tilde{g}(Z_{\theta}) \,=\, d_0 \, + \, d_1\,{Z_{\theta}}\,+\,d_2  \,{Z_{\theta}}^2\,+\,d_3\,{Z_{\theta}}^3\]
\[\tilde{f}(Z_{\theta}) \,=\, e_0 \, + \, e_1\,{Z_{\theta}}\,+\,e_2  \,{Z_{\theta}}^2\,+\,e_3\,{Z_{\theta}}^3\]

Which means that our conditional (on one period earlier interval start value of \(\theta\)) transition model form of distribution of  \(\Delta\theta\) now becomes.
\[\, \Delta \theta_t \,=\, \theta_t\,- \,\theta_{t-1} \, =\, E\left[\Delta\theta_t \,|\, \theta_{t-1} \right] \,+\, \bar{ \Delta \theta_t } \\
=\, g(\theta_{t-1}) \,+\,f(\theta_{t-1}) \,Z_{\bar{\Delta\theta}} \\
=\,\left[\, d_0 \, + \, d_1\,{Z_{\theta}}\,+\,d_2  \,{Z_{\theta}}^2\,+\,d_3\,{Z_{\theta}}^3\right]\,+\,\left[ \, e_0 \, + \, e_1\,{Z_{\theta}}\,+\,e_2  \,{Z_{\theta}}^2\,+\,e_3\,{Z_{\theta}}^3\right] Z_{\bar{\Delta\theta}}\]

We can directly calculate \( g({\theta}) \,=\, E\left[\Delta\theta_t \,|\, \theta_{t-1} \right]\) using conditional expectation as given in reference below and then  

 \[\bar{\Delta\theta_t} \,=\, \Delta\theta_t\, -\, E\left[\Delta\theta_t| \,\theta_{t-1} \right] \,\]

For the above equation to hold, We apply this equation on data observations of \(\Delta \theta_t\) with t-th observation pair given as \(\Delta\theta_{t}\) and \(\theta_{t-1}\). We first invert Hermite-Series equation of \(\theta_t\) to find \(Z_{\theta_t}\) and then use the following equation for each t-th observation (For details see my reference paper on Z-series below)

\begin{equation}\label{4.4.3}
 \bar{\Delta\theta_t} \,=  \, \Delta\theta_t\,-\,-\, E\left[\Delta\theta_t| \,\theta_{t-1} \right] \,\\
\,=  \, \Delta\theta_t\,-\,\left[ \, d_0 \, + \, d_1\,{Z_{\theta}}\,+\,d_2  \,{Z_{\theta}}^2\,+\,d_3\,{Z_{\theta}}^3\right]
\end{equation}

This way we get data values of \(\bar{\Delta\theta_t} \,\) which is the orthogonal (but not independent) part of the model equation where we want to estimate the remaining part of model as

\[\,  \bar{ \Delta \theta_t } =\,\, f(\theta_{t-1}) \,Z_{\bar{\Delta\theta}} \\
=\,\,\left[ \, e_0 \, + \, e_1\,{Z_{\theta}}\,+\,e_2  \,{Z_{\theta}}^2\,+\,e_3\,{Z_{\theta}}^3\right] Z_{\bar{\Delta\theta}}\]


We can estimate the above equation by calculating moments of \(\bar{\Delta \theta}_{t}\)  as 

\[\, \mu_k\,=\,\frac{1}{N}\, \sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\, {\left[\bar{\Delta \theta}_{t_n} \right]}^k \]

In order to estimate the above equation, we will have to first back out \(Z_{\theta_{t_n}}\) associated with t-th observation of \(\theta_{t_n}\) and then use the equation 

\[\, \mu_k\,=\,\frac{1}{N}\, \sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\, {\left[ \left(\, e_0 \, + \, e_1 \, Z_{\theta_{t_{n-1}}}\,+ \,e_2 \,{\, Z_{\theta_{t_{n-1}}}\,}^2\,\right)\, Z_{\bar{\Delta\theta}} \right]}^k \]

The values \(\mu_k\) on RHS of above equation are moments that have been calculated from the data of \(\bar{\Delta\theta_t} \,\), and we have to estimate \((e_0,\, e_1,\, e_2)\) by matching the moments to RHS values. 

or more explicitly we can convert above drift and noise Z-series into a Taylor series in parameters \(\theta_t\) to find the true solution in original variables. We will have to use our inverse function composition to find coefficients of Taylor series below

\[\, \Delta \theta_{t_n} \,=\, g(\theta_0) \, + \, g'(\theta_0) \,(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)+ .5 \, g''(\theta_0) \,{(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)}^2\, + \\\, \left[ \, f(\theta_0) \, + \, f'(\theta_0) \,(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)+ .5 \, f''(\theta_0) \,{(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)}^2\,\right] \, Z_{\theta}\ \\
 \,=\, g_0 \, + \, g_1 \,(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)+ .5 \, g_2 \,{(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)}^2\,+\, \left[ \, f_0 \, + \, f_1 \,(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)+ .5 \, f_2 \,{(\theta_{t_{n-1}}\,-\, \theta_0)}^2\,\right] \, Z_{\theta}\]

In the above equation \(\theta_0\) is median of distribution of \(\theta\) and can be found by choosing the middle element in sorted values of \(\theta_t\).

This way we can fit the more general model to data of dynamic coefficients. Our ornstein-uhlenbeck type earlier model is also nested by and is a special case of this more general model. \subsection{Conditional Expectation of a Non-Gaussian Random variable Y given Another Correlated Non-Gaussian Random variable X}
\label{sec4.3}

In this section, we describe conditional expectation of a non-Gaussian random variable Y given the value of another correlated non-Gaussian random variable X. Orthogonality property of Hermite polynomials and decomposition of variance into orthogonal parts means that conditional expectation of a random variable Y with respect to other random variables X can be divided into separate covariance/correlation based relationships between Hermite polynomials of the same order of random variables X and Y . Further implying that the total conditional expectation of a random variable Y based on another correlated random variable X can be described by sum of separate conditional expectations of each Hermite polynomial of random variable Y with respect to Hermite polynomials of the same order of the random variable X . 

The conditional expectation of nth Hermite polynomial based term (with coefficient \(ch_n\)) of the random variable Y given nth polynomial term of X is calculated in terms of covariance \(Cov_{XY,n}\) between nth polynomials of both random variables X and Y and variance \(Var_{X,n}\) of the nth Hermite polynomial of explanatory variable X as 

\begin{equation}\label{4.3.1}
E[Y_n\,|\, X_n]\,= \, E[\,\,ch_n\,H_n(Z_Y)\,|\,bh_n.\,H_n(Z_X)]\, =\,\frac{Cov_{XY,n}}{Var_{X,n}}\,bh_n \, H_n(Z_X) 
\end{equation}

while expectation of variance associated with the conditional expectation \(E[Y_n\,|\, X_n]\,\) is given as

\begin{align}\label{4.3.2}
 & Var[Y_n\,|\, X_n]\,= Var[\,\,ch_n \, H_n(Z_Y)\,|\,bh_n\,H_n(Z_X)]\,=\,Var_{Y,n}\,-\frac{{Cov_{XY,n}}^2}{Var_{X,n}}\,\\ &=(1\,-\, {\rho_{XY,n}}^2) \, Var_{Y,n}\,\, =(1\,-\, {\rho_{XY,n}}^2) \, {ch_n}^2\, n! \,
\end{align}

We can write sum of conditional expectations of all Hermite polynomials of target variable Y with respect to explanatory variable as

\begin{equation}\label{4.3.4}
 \sum\limits_{n=1}^{N} E[\,\,ch_n \, H_n(Z_Y),|\,bh_n\,H_n(Z_X)]\,=\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\frac{Cov_{XY,n}}{Var_{X,n}}\,\,bh_n \, H_n(Z_X) 
 \end{equation}

But we know that conditional expectation of Y is equal to sum of conditional expectations of each orthogonal polynomial of Y including the zeroth hermite polynomial which is the unconditional mean of Y itself, so we can write

\begin{equation}\label{4.3.5}
E[Y \, | \, X]=\mu_Y+ \sum\limits_{n=1}^{N} E[\,\,ch_n \, H_n(Z_Y)\, | \,\,bh_n \,H_n(Z_X) ]
\end{equation}

which means that 

\begin{equation}\label{4.3.6}
E[Y\, | \, X]=\mu_Y+\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\frac{Cov_{XY,n}}{Var_{X,n}}\,\,bh_n \, H_n(Z_X) 
\end{equation}

and expected value of total variance, which is variance associated with part of random variable Y that has not been explained by conditioning on random variable X, associated with conditional expectation of Y given X \(E[Y\, | \, X]\) is given as

\begin{align}\label{4.3.7}
& Var[Y\, | \, X]=\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,Var_{Y,n}\,-\frac{{Cov_{XY,n}}^2}{Var_{X,n}}\, =\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,(1\,-\, {\rho_{XY,n}}^2) \, Var_{Y,n}\,\, \\ &= \,\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,(1\,-\, {\rho_{XY,n}}^2) \, {ch_n}^2\, n!\, 
\end{align}
In the above equation, We have given the formulas for conditional expected value and the expected value of variance of conditional expectation of the random variable Y given random variable X. 

Even though above formula is true for expectation of variance associated with \(E[Y|X]\), the coefficients of nth Hermite polynomial in conditional distribution equation of Y given X are not given by \(\, \sqrt{\,1\,-\,{\rho_{XY,n}}^2\,} \,\, ch_n\). We find true conditional distribution equation for Y when its unexplained variance is independent of X in section(\ref{sec4.5}) and when its variance structure is dependent on X in section(\ref{sec4.6}) 



\subsection{Decomposing the non-Gaussian Random variable Y into a part that is perfectly Correlated with Random variable X and another part that is orthogonal to X}
\label{sec4.4}

In order to convert a random variable Y into two parts with first part fully correlated with X and another part orthogonal to X, we remove the correlation effect of random variable X from Y, where Hermite series of X and Y are given by Eqs(\ref{4.1},and \ref{4.2}) and correlation between their Hermite polynomials are given by Eq(\ref{4.1.5}).

Correlation effect of X on Y is given by conditional expected value of \(E\left[Y|X\right]\). And we represent the orthogonal part of Y as \(\tilde{Y}\). So Y can be decomposed into two parts as

\begin{equation}\label{4.4.1}
 Y \,=\,\, E\left[Y|X \right] \,+ \,\tilde{Y}
\end{equation}

So orthogonal part of Y denoted as \(\tilde{Y}\) is given by

\begin{align}\label{4.4.2}
 & \tilde{Y} \,=\, Y\, -\, E\left[Y|X \right] \, \\ & =  \, Y\,-\,\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N} \, E \left[\,\,ch_n \, H_n(Z_Y)\,|\,H_n(Z_X) \right]\\ \, &=  \, Y\,-\,\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,\frac{Cov_{XY,n}}{Var_{X,n}}\,bh_n \, H_n(Z_X) \\ \, &=  \, Y\,-\,\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,\rho_{XY,n} \,ch_n \, H_n(Z_X)
\end{align}

We apply the above equation on data observations of Y with pth observation pair given as \(Y_p\) and \(X_p\). We first invert Hermite-Series equation of \(X_p\) to find \(Z_{X_p}\) and then use the following equation for each pth observation 

\begin{equation}\label{4.4.3}
 \tilde{Y_p} \,=  \, Y_p\,-\,\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,\rho_{XY,n} \,ch_n \, H_n(Z_{X_p})
\end{equation}

function [bnm] = EstimateDynamicCoeffParameters(X)

Ndata=length(X)-1;
Order=7;


%Calculate transition dX
for nn=1:Ndata
    dX(nn)=X(nn+1)-X(nn);
end

%Below Calculate Z-series of X and dX.

[CoeffX,ZX,RSquared1] = CalculateActualZSeriesFromData(X,Order);
[CoeffdX,ZdX,RSquared2] = CalculateActualZSeriesFromData(dX,Order);



Ymu1(1:Ndata,1)=dX(1:Ndata);

[W1] = EvalHermiteArrayH0(ZX(1:Ndata),Order);
        %Below, do the regression to find the coefficients of part of dX
        %random variable that is correalted with X.
coeff1=inv(W1'*W1)*(W1'*Ymu1);
        %Below, we evaluate the correalted part of dX random variable
        %with X by summing over all the hermite polynomials out
        %to Order.
for hh=0:Order 
            %Below dXcorr continues to sum corelated part over all hermites of X
            
      dXcorr(1:Ndata,1)=dXcorr(1:Ndata,1)+coeff1(hh+1,1).*W1(1:Ndata,hh+1);
            
end

%Below, we calculate orthogonal part of DX that is uncorrelated with dX

dXdecorr(1:Ndata,1)=Ymu1(1:Ndata,1)-dXcorr(1:Ndata,1);


%Below calculat moments of correalted (with X)  part and orthogonal (with respect to X) part of dX
