I will take the threads where we left (as this post has to be read in the context of past few posts) and describe how to convert univariate moments' series of Y into conditional moments of Y conditional on  K random variables \(X_1, X_2, X_3, \ldots \,,X_K\). 

We suppose that the Hermite-series of mth power of Y (mth moment is its expectation) is given as

\[Y^m(Z_y)\,=\,\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N*m}\,c_n\,\, H_n(Z_y)\]

and we want to find the mth conditional moment of Y given some realization K random variables \(X_1, X_2, X_3, \ldots \,,X_K\).

We define the correlation matrix of nth hermite polynomial of K explaining  random variables \(X_1, X_2, X_3, \ldots \,,X_K\) as 
\[\textbf{R}_n \,=\,
 \begin{bmatrix}
         \rho_{X_1 X_1,n} & \rho_{X_2 X_1,n} & \cdots & \rho_{X_K X_1,n}\\
         \rho_{X_1 X_2,n} & \rho_{X_2 X_2,n} & \cdots & \rho_{X_K X_2,n}\\ 
         \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\ 
         \rho_{X_1 X_K,n} & \rho_{X_2 X_K,n} & \cdots & \rho_{X_K X_K,n} 
     \end{bmatrix}
\]

While the vector of correlations of nth hermite polynomial of Y with nth hermite polynomial of each of the K explaining  random variables \(X_1, X_2, X_3, \ldots \,,X_K\) is given as

 \[\textbf{r}_{XY,n}\,=\begin{bmatrix}
          \rho_{X_1 Y,n} \\
 \rho_{X_2 Y,n}  \\ \cdots \\ \rho_{X_K Y,n}
     \end{bmatrix}\]

Then vector of effective correlation coefficients \(\beta_{n,k}\)  associated with kth random variable \(X_k\) and nth hermite polynomial that explains the nth hermite polynomial of Y is given as

\[
\begin{bmatrix}
          \beta_{n,1} \\
 \beta_{n,2}  \\ \cdots \\ \beta_{n_K}
     \end{bmatrix}
=
{\begin{bmatrix}
         \rho_{X_1 X_1,n} & \rho_{X_2 X_1,n} & \cdots & \rho_{X_K X_1,n}\\
         \rho_{X_1 X_2,n} & \rho_{X_2 X_2,n} & \cdots & \rho_{X_K X_2,n}\\ 
         \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\ 
         \rho_{X_1 X_K,n} & \rho_{X_2 X_K,n} & \cdots & \rho_{X_K X_K,n} 
     \end{bmatrix}}^{-1}
\,
\begin{bmatrix}
          \rho_{X_1 Y,n} \\
 \rho_{X_2 Y,n}  \\ \cdots \\ \rho_{X_K Y,n}
     \end{bmatrix}
\]

The above equation is equation of standardized regression that is done on correlations. Inverse of variance-covariance matrix of regressors is replaced by inverse of correlation matrix of regressors \({\textbf{R}_n}^{-1} \). And covariance vector between regressand and regressors is replaced by the respective correlation vector \(\textbf{r}_{XY,n}\) making the standardized regression equation as 

\[ \boldsymbol{\beta}_n ={\textbf{R}_n}^{-1} \, \textbf{r}_{XY,n}\]

Then the mth conditional moment of Y given K random variables \(X_1, X_2, X_3, \ldots \,,X_K\) is calculated by an equation of the form

\[E\left[Y^m|X_1=X_{1_0},X_2=X_{2_0}, \ldots, X_K=X_{K_0} \right]\,=\,\,\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N*m}\, \sum\limits_{k=1}^{K} c_n \,\,\beta_{n,k}\, \big[ H_n(Z_{k}) | Z_{k}=Z_{k_0}\,\big]\] 

Again all these conditional moments can be used to determine the univariate conditional density/distribution of Y given K random variables \(X_1, X_2, X_3, \ldots \,,X_K\).

I suggest again as I did in a previous post (post 2096: https://forum.wilmott.com/viewtopic.php?p=889657#p889653) that the hermite correlations used in the calculation of conditional moments are backed out from the cross moments between the respective random variables.

Conditional Expectation of A Non-Gaussian Random Variable Given several Non-Gaussian Correlated Random variables: Variance Form.

Suppose we know univariate hermite series of target variable Y and K explanatory variables each of which are represented as \(X_k\,,k\,=\,1, \ldots,K\). We define some basic relationships again for clarity

\begin{equation}\label{5.1.1}
Y(Z_Y)\,=\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, c_n\, H_n(Z_Y) \, =\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, Y_n 
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{5.1.2}
X_k(Z_{X_k})\,=\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, b_{k,n}\, H_n(Z_{X_k})\, =\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, X_{k,n} 
\end{equation}

We would have one variance matrix \(E[X^t\,X]\) for each order of hermite polynomial. We define Variance  which is defined as reference of Eqs(88-90)

\begin{equation}\label{5.1.3}
\boldsymbol{Var_{\boldsymbol{X},n}}\,=\,\begin{bmatrix}  Var_{X_{1},n} & Cov_{X_{1}X_{2},n} & ....& ..... &  Cov_{X_{1}X_{K},n} \\ 
Cov_{X_{2}X_{1},n} & Var_{X_{2},n} & ....& ..... &  Cov_{X_{2}X_{K},n} \\ .... & .... & ....& .... & ....\\ Cov_{X_{k}X_{1},n} & Cov_{X_{k}X_{2},n} & ....& .... &  Cov_{X_{k}X_{K},n} \\  ... & ... & ... & ... & ... \\ 
Cov_{X_{K}X_{1},n} & Cov_{X_{K}X_{2},n} & .... & .... &  Var_{X_{K},n} 
\end{bmatrix}   
\end{equation}


While array of covariances of \(X_k\,,k\,=\,1, \ldots,K\) with Y for nth order hermite polynomial is defined as

\begin{equation}\label{5.1.4}
\boldsymbol{cov_{\boldsymbol{XY},n}}\,=\,\begin{bmatrix}  Cov_{X_{1}Y,n} \\  Cov_{X_{2}Y,n} \\ 
....\\
Cov_{X_{k}Y,n} \\ .... \\ Cov_{X_{K}Y,n}
\end{bmatrix}   
\end{equation}

We also define

\begin{equation}\label{5.1.5}
\boldsymbol{x_n}\,=\,\begin{bmatrix}  X_{{1},n} \\  X_{{2},n} \\ 
....\\
X_{{k},n} \\ .... \\ X_{{K},n}
\end{bmatrix}   
\end{equation}

then conditional expected value associated with nth hermite polynomial term of Y is given as
\begin{equation}\label{5.1.6}
E \left[\,Y_n|\, X_{k,n}\,(k\,=\,1, \ldots,K) \right]\,= \boldsymbol{{x_n}^T \, Var_{{X},n}^{-1} \,cov_{\boldsymbol{XY},n} }\,
\end{equation}

Then conditional expected value of Y given \(X_k\,,k\,=\,1, \ldots,K\) is summation of its conditional expected value over all Hermite polynomials and is given as
\begin{equation}\label{5.1.7}
E \left[\,Y|\, X_k\,(k\,=\,1, \ldots,K) \right]\,=\mu_Y\,+\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\, \boldsymbol{{x_n}^T \, Var_{{X},n}^{-1} \,cov_{\boldsymbol{XY},n} }\,
\end{equation}

Friends, I want to mention something about my previous research about finding the densities of SDEs given the parameters of the SDEs. The parameters of the SDEs are not inherently random and do not have an automatic Hermite-series/Z-series. So I made bounds on SDE parameters and then randomly generated combinations of SDE parameters within those bounds and then generated their Hermite-series. For each randomly generated pair of parameters, I simulated the SDE and found Z-series associated with its density. Then I did a full-span Hermite (and also Least Squares) regression of coefficients of the Z-series of SDEs on their associated random coefficients. And the results were astounding. The first three or four parameters of the Z-series were found by regressions with more than .999 R-Squared. And all of the densities of SDEs generated by findding Z-series coefficients from above mentioned regression were indistinguishable from the ones generated by direct monte carlo simulation. The R-squared of last few coefficients was slightly smaller than 95% but I attributed that to noise in input associated with imprecision associated with direct monte carlo simulations(monte carlo random imprecision).  

I want to mention that Z-series coefficients are non-linear functions of moments. And when I look back in retrospect now, I can see that if  I had done above regressions on moments of SDEs, it would have been a lot easier (as it would most likely require not necessarily full-span polynomials.) than regression on Z-series coefficients(as these coefficients (used in regressions of that previous research) are non-linear functions of moments). Moments would yield the same Z-series coefficients and directly regressing the moments would take a whole layer of complexity out of the problem and make the problem far easier.

Again, in retrospect, I feel that it would have been much easier(but slightly slower) if I had directly regressed moments of the simulation results of randomized SDE parameters instead of Z-series coefficients of the simulated SDEs. 

Seeing in context of conditionality in data, parameters of the SDE are not directly random and are not random data and therefore do not have a hermite-series or Z-series directly unless we generate a random sample of them. However for random data that can directly be converted into hermite-series, we do not need any special training and we can directly hermite-regress the moments of the resulting variable on the moments of its explaining random variables. However depending upon the nature and complexity of the problem, for some problems we might have to add various types of cross-products of explanatory variables (through adding hermite polynomials of hermite-series of these cross-products) ,especially the first order cross-products, as regressors in the hermite regression to get very good results. For most difficult problems, we might have to do full-span regressions. By full-span regressions, I mean that hermite-polynomials of Hermite-series of all possible orders of cross-products are included in regression. Even though most researchers would take time to find out which cross-products (of various orders) are more effective (after their hermite polynomials are included in regressions) in parsimoniously predicting the desired random variable especially for scientific problems where an underlying true solution might exist.

Again, in my earlier SDE densities from SDE parameters research project, I regressed coefficients of Hermite-series of SDE densities on respective hermite-Series of SDE parameters. But neither coefficients of Hermite-Series of SDE densities nor SDE parameters are random per se and randomized data had to be generated to find their Hermite-Series. On the other hand, when we choose to regress moments of regressand on the hermite polynomials of random data, both the regressand and the regressors are random data and their hermite-series is directly known and therefore we do not need any special randomization. Only thing needed is proper choice of regressors by also including Hermite-Series of appropriate products of regressors.

For friends who have taken interest in my research, I would like to mention that I have primarily worked with densities of continuous random variables that can be represented by normal random variable and its hermite polynomials. But there are several  large classes of continuous random variables that cannot be described by hermite polynomials of normal random variables. With hermite polynomials, we can work with (among others) gamma, and chi-squared random variables and most of their more advanced modifications. 

But many densities that do not have a tail like for example beta density (among many others) and these (beta-like and other similar) densities can be easily described by an expansion in Legendre polynomials of uniform random variables. Similarly sinusoidal type random variables and processes have their own underlying orthogonal polynomials with associated standard distribution. All these different orthogonal polynomials in respective standard distributions of associated standard random variables can be used for different classes of random variables and our equations of conditional expectation for these random variables would be just like what I have earlier described with hermite polynomials of standard normal replaced by Legendre polynomials of uniform random variables or other appropriate polynomials (for sinusoids for example) of their associated standard random variable, and as a result, the correlations would be between orthogonal polynomials of the same order.

My research has been primarily in finance where normal-like densities (like gamma density, generalized gamma density, chi-squared density, non-central chi-squared, generalized chi-squared and others similar tailed densities) are usually used that can be described by hermite polynomials so I primarily worked with hermite polynomials only.

I recall I even wrote a small algorithm on this forum that could construct orthogonal polynomials for any given continuous distribution of random variables.

All the ideas we used for hermite polynomials of normal density to generalize relevant random variables can also be applied to other classes of orthogonal random variables (of their associated underlying standard random variable class) which can then be used  to represent their relevant appropriate classes of random variables.

If some friends are interested in developing orthognal polynomials of some other standard base class of random variables to represent different general classes of random variables and want to transfer the infrastructure I have developed for hermite polynomials of normal random variables to other orthogonal polynomials(of their standard underlying RV density), please let me know and I would love to help (obviously without any fee for academic friends).
