Friends, again there is some bad news and some more good news. The bad news is that it may not be easily possible to advance stochastic integrals of SDEs with method of matching cumulants. In theory method should work perfectly but there are a few practical problems. First of all, I was doing simulations of SDEs in Bessel coordinates but when I convert a Z-series (in powers of Z) representation of the SDE from Bessel Coordinates to original coordinates, many times the new Z-series in original does not faithfully represent the SDE variable in original coordinates. I have been converting it into original coordinates earlier by mapping Z-series  in Bessel coordinates to the Z-variable grid and then converting every grid point to original coordinates and it works perfectly well for all different SDEs around one and between one and zero for all typical parameters of the SDEs. However directly converting Z-series(in powers of Z) in Bessel coordinates to Z-series in original coordinates does not work very well usually. It might be possible to get a Z-series conversion into original coordinates if we were using larger number of powers of Z in Bessel representation as we are only using first five powers in our representation. But including larger powers in Z-series make the code too cumbersome.
Another problem is that higher cumulants increase very fast in original coordinates while in Bessel coordinates they remain relatively smaller. This results in our newton method to find roots for cumulant matching strategy converging very quickly for Bessel coordinates but diverging(or converging far less quickly) for original coordinates. 
In order to find the densities of stochastic integrals of SDEs my first line of attack was to simply convert into a Z-series in original variables and calculate cumulants and then match cumulants just as we did for the original SDE to find the density of stochastic integrals. But this does not seem practical anymore.
Now the good news. We have been working with different methods of simulating the SDEs for several years now (I posted code for higher order monte carlo simulations in 2018 as I recall) and I have gained a lot of insights into various methods of simulation of SDEs. Now when I was looking for alternative methods for simulation of densities of stochastic integral of SDEs, I thought again to use transition probabilities framework. And when I thought again, I was embarrassed at some of the unintelligent mistakes I had earlier made when I tried this before. And I am very sure with a corrected version of transition probabilities method and a long stepping of original SDE (of Delta t=1/16 years or 1/32 years at worst), we would be able to get very fast methods for simulating the densities of integrals of SDEs.
