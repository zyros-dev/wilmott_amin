Friends, I am writing corrected version of the introduction again. The older introduction in post#2636 remains but it is understood that it has some errors. The version below is corrected version. Specifically in the article with errors, Bayes law could be not be applied in reverse direction since due to correlations and dependent variance, one of the random variables Y and X would have to be dependent on both \(Z_x\) and \(Z_y\) so Bayes law cannot be applied in both directions on X and Y with existing \(Z_x\) and \(Z_y\). Principles of Bayes law remain valid but we would have to change the defintion of \(Z_x\) and \(Z_y\)
For example applying Bayes law as
\[ \,W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, =\,\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\,X(Z_x)\,\]
would mean that X is independent variable dependent only on \(Z_x\) and it is Y that is dependent on \(Z_x\) and \(Z_y\) and we would have to do joint density analytics with the same thing in mind.
Applying Bayes law in other direction 
\[ \,W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, =\,\left[X|Y\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\,Y(Z_y)\,\]
would imply that Y is independent variable solely dependent on \(Z_y\) while it is X that is both dependent on  \(Z_x\) and \(Z_y\) and we would have to do joint density analytics with the same assumptions.

It will not be possible to apply Bayes law in both directions with the same defintion of \(Z_x\) and \(Z_y\). However, we can construct decorrelated versions of X and Y where Bayes law could apply in both directions with same definition of \(Z_x\) and \(Z_y\)
 

Two Dimensional Z-Series/Hermite-Series:
Case 1:  Random variable X indepenent and Random Variable Y dependent and Conditional on X
Suppose we have two non-Gaussian continuous random variables, X and Y and we can make observations of their joint realizations. We suppose that X is the independent random variable and Y is the dependent random variable. This dependence might follow from causality but would usually be arbitrary for the analytics and we could possibly reverse the dependent and independent random variables and successfully re-do the analytics. 

We can calculate the univariate Z-series/Hermite-series of independent random variable X as

\[\,X(Z_x)\,=\, ah_0 \, +\, ah_1 \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, ah_2 \,H_2(Z_x) \,+\, ah_3 \,H_3(Z_x) \,+\,\ldots\]

Even though we can make a univariate Z-series/Hermite-Series of Y, this would not be helpful from two dimensional perspective. We just define a conditional version of Y as

\begin{align}

\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \,&=\, \Big[\, cH_{0,0} \, + \, cH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, cH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{1,0} \, + \, cH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, cH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{2,0} \, + \, cH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, cH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 



Bivariate Joint Hermite-Series of X and Y is denoted by a random variable W in the form of a product (of joint realizations of X and Y) and is given as (please note that \(aH_{m,n}\) are merely numerical coefficients while \(H_n(.)\) are hermite polynomials)

\begin{align}

W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, = \, X\, Y\,&=\, \Big[\, aH_{0,0} \, + \, aH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, aH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, aH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, aH_{1,0} \, + \, aH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, aH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, aH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, aH_{2,0} \, + \, aH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, aH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, aH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

The following version of conditional Hermite-Series would hold

\[ \,W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, =\,\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\,X(Z_x)\,\]

Now, we want to do analysis of \(\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \). It is composed of three parts. The first part of Y is directly correlated with X and appears on first row in the formula of \(\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \). The second part of Y is that has variance independent of X and that appears in the first column in the formula of \(\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \). The third part of Y has variance that is dependent on X and this part appears below the first row and towards left of the first column. rewriting the formula of   \(\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \), we have

\begin{align}
\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \,&=\, \Big[\, cH_{0,0} \, + \, cH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, cH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{1,0} \, + \, cH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, cH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{2,0} \, + \, cH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, cH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

The first row \( \Big[\, cH_{0,0} \, + \, cH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, cH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big]\) has part of Y that is directly correlated with X. 
And first column given as 

\begin{align}
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{1,0} \,  \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{2,0} \, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{3,0} \, \Big] \, H_3(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

is independent of X while the remaining body given as

\begin{align} 
&+\, \Big[  \, cH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, cH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[  \, cH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, cH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[  \, cH_{3,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{3,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, cH_{3,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_3(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

is the part whose variance systematically depends upon X.


We should be not be able to retrieve one dimensional marginal Hermite-Series from Bivariate joint Hermite-Series after integrating over the second variable. This is because we are integrating over Z_x that is found as underlying in both variables X and Y as they are correlated. The following are true only when X and Y are independent.

\[\,Y(Z_y)\,=\,\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\, \,W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, dZ_x\]
\[\,X(Z_x)\,=\,\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\, \,W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, dZ_y\]

Another simple solution to finding two dimensional joint distribution would be to find the univariate Z-series of random variable X and Y independently as
\begin{equation}\label{1}
\,X(Z_x)\,=\, ah_0 \, +\, ah_1 \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, ah_2 \,H_2(Z_x) \,+\, ah_3 \,H_3(Z_x) \,+\,\ldots
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{2}
\,Y(Z_y)\,=\, bh_0 \, +\, bh_1 \,H_1(Z_y) \,+\, bh_2 \,H_2(Z_y) \,+\, bh_3 \,H_3(Z_y) \,+\,\ldots  
\end{equation}

Now we calculate correlations between hermite polynomials of the same order as
\begin{equation}\label{3}
\rho_{XY,n} \, = \,  \frac{\sum\limits_{p=1}^{P}\, H_n(Z_{X,p}) \, \, H_n(Z_{Y,p}) \,}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{p=1}^{P} \, {H_n(Z_{X,p})}^2 \,\sum\limits_{p=1}^{P} \, \, {H_n(Z_{Y,p})}^2 \,}}
\end{equation}

 Now, we substitute correlations in the definition of dependent random variable which is Y to get
\begin{align}\label{4}
\,Y(Z_x,Z_y)\,&=\, bh_0 \, + \, bh_1\, \left[\rho_{XY,1} \,H_1(Z_X) \,+\,\sqrt{1-\,{\rho_{XY,1}}^2 \,} \,H_1(Z_y) \right]\,+\, bh_2 \,\left[\rho_{XY,2} \,H_2(Z_X) \,+\,\sqrt{1-\,{\rho_{XY,2}}^2 \,} \,H_2(Z_y) \right]\,\,\\
&+\, bh_3 \,\left[\rho_{XY,3} \,H_3(Z_X) \,+\,\sqrt{1-\,{\rho_{XY,3}}^2 \,} \,H_3(Z_y) \right]\,\,+\,\ldots 
\end{align}

Now we do a series multiplication of above equation (4) with equation (1) to find a value of 
\begin{equation}\label{5}
W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, = \, X(Z_x)\, Y(Z_x\,,\,Z_y)\
\end{equation}

We can possibly find the value of \(\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\) from 

\begin{equation}\label{6}
W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, =\,\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,\,X(Z_x)\,
\end{equation}

by using


\begin{equation}\label{7}
\,\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, =\,\frac{\,W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\,}{\,X(Z_x)\,}
\end{equation}

But again, this fails to properly capture the dependence of variance of Y on X. The above expression only gives us orthogonal variance that is independent.

Friends, regressions given in post 2637 are perfectly right. Only thing we need to determine is how to associate \(Z_{x_p}\) with \(X\) and \(Z_{y_p}\) with \(Y\). Once we have properly made this association, our regressions would be exactly the same as in post 2637.

Let us try again.

In order to associate data observations of first variable \(X_p\) with its underlying Gaussian, \(Z_{X_p}\), we simply sort the observations \(X_p\) and associate them with discretized Gaussian density \(Z_{X_p}\) just as we did for univariate densities.

Now we are left with associating the observations of second variable \(Y_p\) with \(Z_{Y_p}\). This is more difficult.

First we decorrelate the observations \(Y_p\) by removing the effect of \(X_p\). For this we will have to remove the correlated hermite polynomials of \(Z_{X_p}\). This can also be done by a univariate regression of the observations \(Y_p\) on hermite polynomials of joint observations of \(X_p\) given by \(H_n(Z_{X_p})\). Suppose decorrelated (and de-meaned) observations are denoted as \(\bar{Y}_p\). These observations would have zero mean. 

In order to find dependence structure of variance of observations \(\bar{Y}_p\) on \(Z_{X_p}\), we regress \({ \bar{Y}_p}^2\) on hermite polynomials of \(Z_{X_p}\) along with a constant. We call this variance regression as  \(Var\left[\bar{Y}\right](Z_{X_p})\) which would be an expression in hermite polynomials of  \(Z_{X_p}\).

Now  the decorrelated \(\bar{Y}\), standardized by square root of variance \(Var\left[\bar{Y}\right](Z_{X_p}) \), should be distributed in an increasing order i.e. the standardized values  \(\frac{\bar{Y}}{\sqrt{Var\left[\bar{Y}\right](Z_{X_p})}}\) could be sorted and assigned to discretized density of \(Z_{Y_p}\) in increasing order.

Least Squares Regression Logic To Calculate Joint Bivariate Hermite Series of \(XY=W(Z_y,Z_x)\) and Conditional Hermite Series \([Y|X](Z_y,Z_x)\) 


Bivariate Joint Hermite-Series of X and Y is denoted by a random variable W in the form of a product (of joint realizations of X and Y) and is given as (please note that \(aH_{m,n}\) are merely numerical coefficients while \(H_n(.)\) are hermite polynomials)

\begin{align}

W(Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, = \, X\, Y\,&=\, \Big[\, aH_{0,0} \, + \, aH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, aH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, aH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, aH_{1,0} \, + \, aH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, aH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, aH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, aH_{2,0} \, + \, aH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, aH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, aH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

One dimensional Hermite Series of X is given as

\[\,X(Z_x)\,=\,g(Z_x)\,=\, ah_0 \, +\, ah_1 \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, ah_2 \,H_2(Z_x) \,+\, ah_3 \,H_3(Z_x) \,+\,\ldots\]

We can write the first equation as

\begin{align}

 \, g(Z_x)\, Y\,&=\, \Big[\, aH_{0,0} \, + \, aH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, aH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, aH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, aH_{1,0} \, + \, aH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, aH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, aH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, aH_{2,0} \, + \, aH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, aH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, aH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

Dividing both sides by \(g(Z_x)\), we get

\begin{align}

 Y\,&=\, \Big[\, aH_{0,0} \,\frac{1}{g(Z_x)}\, + \, aH_{0,1} \frac{H_1(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\,+\, aH_{0,2} \, \frac{H_2(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\, \,\, + \, aH_{0,3} \frac{H_3(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, aH_{1,0} \, \,\frac{1}{g(Z_x)}\,+ \, aH_{1,1} \frac{H_1(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\,+\, aH_{1,2} \, \frac{H_2(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\, + \, aH_{1,3} \,\frac{H_3(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, aH_{2,0} \, \,\frac{1}{g(Z_x)}\,+ \, aH_{2,1} \, \,\frac{H_1(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\,\,+\, aH_{2,2} \,\frac{H_2(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\, \, + \, aH_{2,3} \frac{H_3(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

From the above regression, we get coefficients \(aH_{n,m}\) of hermite-series of \(W=XY\) as found in very first equation at start of the post.

The above equation is written again below emphasizing the dependence on pth joint observations as in one line of regression

\begin{align}

 Y_p\,&=\, \Big[\, aH_{0,0} \,\frac{1}{g(Z_{x,p})}\, + \, aH_{0,1} \frac{H_1(Z_{x,p})}{g(Z_{x,p})}\,+\, aH_{0,2} \, \frac{H_2(Z_{x,p})}{g(Z_{x,p})}\, \,\, + \, aH_{0,3} \frac{H_3(Z_{x,p})}{g(Z_{x,p})}\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, aH_{1,0} \, \,\frac{1}{g(Z_{x,p})}\,+ \, aH_{1,1} \frac{H_1(Z_{x,p})}{g(Z_{x,p}/)}\,+\, aH_{1,2} \, \frac{H_2(Z_{x,p})}{g(Z_{x,p})}\, + \, aH_{1,3} \,\frac{H_3(Z_{x,p})}{g(Z_{x,p})}\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_{y,p}) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, aH_{2,0} \, \,\frac{1}{g(Z_{x,p})}\,+ \, aH_{2,1} \, \,\frac{H_1(Z_{x,p})}{g(Z_{x,p})}\,\,+\, aH_{2,2} \,\frac{H_2(Z_{x,p})}{g(Z_{x,p})}\, \, + \, aH_{2,3} \frac{H_3(Z_{x,p})}{g(Z_{x,p})}\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_{y,p}) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

But we have simultaneously done regression for conditional value of \([Y|X](Z_y,Z_x)\) and if we denote

\begin{align}

\left[Y|X\right](Z_x \, , \, Z_y)\, \,&=\, \Big[\, cH_{0,0} \, + \, cH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, cH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{1,0} \, + \, cH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, cH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_1(Z_y) \, \\
&+\, \Big[ \, cH_{2,0} \, + \, cH_{2,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{2,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, \, + \, cH_{2,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \, H_2(Z_y) \, \\
&+\,\,\,\ldots\, \\
\end{align} 

then for first row of both conditional and joint equations
\[\Big[\, cH_{0,0} \, + \, cH_{0,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{0,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \,\, + \, cH_{0,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big] \\
= \Big[\, aH_{0,0} \,\frac{1}{g(Z_x)}\, + \, aH_{0,1} \frac{H_1(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\,+\, aH_{0,2} \, \frac{H_2(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\, \,\, + \, aH_{0,3} \frac{H_3(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\,\,+\,\ldots\,\Big]  \]
The above equation can be solved in many ways to get  the values of \(cH_{0,m}\)

From second row of both bivariate Z-series

\[\, \, \Big[ \, cH_{1,0} \, + \, cH_{1,1} \,H_1(Z_x) \,+\, cH_{1,2} \, H_2(Z_x) \, + \, cH_{1,3} \,H_3(Z_x)\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big] \,\\
=\Big[ \, aH_{1,0} \, \,\frac{1}{g(Z_x)}\,+ \, aH_{1,1} \frac{H_1(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\,+\, aH_{1,2} \, \frac{H_2(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\, + \, aH_{1,3} \,\frac{H_3(Z_x)}{g(Z_x)}\,\,+\,\ldots\, \Big]  \]

The above equation can be solved in many ways to get  the values of \(cH_{1,m}\)

So we can repeat for all rows to get the values of all coefficients \(cH_{n,m}\)

This way we get very stable regressions. For many SDEs for which nothing was working earlier yesterday for several hundred thousands of paths, I found excellent match (with regression as described in this post) to moments as paths increased beyond 20,000. And all the conditional and joint Hermite-series are fully consistent with each other in conditional sense by construction.

function [Coeff,Zy] = CalculateHermiteSeriesFromData01(Yin,Order,ZI)

Ndata=length(Yin);

MeanYin=sum(Yin(1:Ndata))/Ndata;

YmVar=sum(Ym(1:Ndata).^2)/Ndata;
Y(1:Ndata)=Ym(1:Ndata)/sqrt(YmVar);



% [Zn] = StandardNormalIdealDesity02(Ndata,1);
% %[ZI] = CalculateZI(Zn);
% 
% 
% ZI=Zn;
%Below W is the design matrix for least squares regression i.e the matrix
%that contains all the hermite polynomials calculated at ideal normal
%density grid of size Ndata. 
[W] = EvalHermiteArrayH0(ZI,Order);

%We first sort the data so that we can assign one appropriate Z-grid
%point(and associated hermite polynomials calculated at that grid point)
%to each data element. This ideal normal density Z-grid is
%constructed so that its size is equal to data observations count. We
%suppose that first derivative of random variables with repsect to Z
%does not switch sign meaning that increasing values of data
%observations have to aligned with increasing values of ideal normal
%density grid.
[Ys,I]=sort(Y(1:Ndata));
%In the line below, the data sort is reversed so that now the ideal 
%normal density grid elements assigned to various observations of random 
%variable face it on data observations index. For details, Please look 
%on interent the definition of matlab sort command with role of "I" 
%in output and how it alters the index.   

%%Zs1(I(1:Ndata))=ZI(1:Ndata);

%Below the values to be regressed facing the design matrix are assigned
Ymu(1:Ndata,1)=Ys(1:Ndata);
%Below, implement least squares regression.
coeff=(W'*W)\(W'*Ymu);
%Store in coefficietns array of all random varaibles
Coeff(1:Order+1)= coeff(1:Order+1,1);
%Store the associated Z in Z-array of all coefficients.   
Zy(I(1:Ndata))=W(1:Ndata,2);



Coeff(1:Order+1)=Coeff(1:Order+1).*sqrt(YmVar);
Coeff(1)=Coeff(1)+MeanYin;

end
