An explanation here for friends who are also working on the programs.

1.   The matlab function in post  2983    named  Calculate2DZSeries0 is the simplest with only the problem of signs fixed.

When I was earlier working with the problem two months ago and I had retrieved the original density of X from Y through correlations (that seemed to work with TT1=TT2), I had noticed that adding hermite correlations of  calculated orthogonal part  \(\left[ Y \perp X \right]\) with X improved the estimate of original density.  \(\left[ Y \perp X \right]\) is supposedly orthogonal to X and no correlations should exist but small correlations exist in reality and adding them to \(\left[Y|X\right]\), improved the estimate of \(\left[Y|X\right]\). This hermites correlations is what we are adding as an input variable YxCoeffH2 in the calibration function.

Here I added this correlation to \(\left[Y|X\right]\) in first line of the function where  \(\left[Y|X\right]\,\)=rho(nn).*YCoeffH(nn+1)
.
.
Code: Select all
ch(nn+1)=rho(nn).*YCoeffH(nn+1)+(YxCoeffH2(nn+1));
.
.
I noticed that adding the above correlations removes the problem of spikes at the start of the density and mostly improves the shape and fit of the density close to zero. But sometimes it throws an already good density a bit away. 

More work has to be done on this. Please experiment with different weights on YxCoeffH2(nn+1) in the above copied line of code. I took in gamma since I noticed that weight had to be higher for .95 and smaller for .5 . But this is just developing and we would have a clear picture in a few days.

Meanwhile, the main program given in post 2985 keeps the mean accurate. This is because when YxCoeffH2 is added to \(\left[Y|X\right]\), it slightly alters the mean and this issue is fixed in the main program in post 2985.

Friends, I think I have an appropriate solution to the given problem that only requires simultaneous solution of linear equations using linear algebra.

Here are the basics. Every  \(\left[ Z_x\,+\,Z_{\tilde{y}}\right]^n\) is expanded with binomial theorem and each of the (n+1) expansion terms has one correlation coefficient, so binomial expansion of \(\left[ Z_x\,+\,Z_{\tilde{y}}\right]^n\) has n+1 correlation coefficients. There are not two correlation coefficients(that is why I am not indicating any correlation in compact power equation), rather there are n+1 correlation coefficients and binomial expansion of each nth power term has different correlation coefficients. So binomial correlation coefficients of each of the  \(\left[ Z_x\,+\,Z_{\tilde{y}}\right]^K\) would be very different from binomial correlation coefficients of \(\left[ Z_x\,+\,Z_{\tilde{y}}\right]^N\) for all values of \(K\,=\,1,2,\,\ldots,N-1\).

We impose (N+1) linear constraint equations on Nth power binomial expansion to find N+1 correlation coefficients.

1st constraint is that correlation of   \(\left[ Z_x\,+\,Z_{\tilde{y}}\right]^N\)  with \({Z_y}^N\)  is one.
second constraint is that if \(K\,=\,1,2,\,\ldots,N-1\) then for each of the N-1 values of K, the joint correlation of   \(\left[\left[ Z_x\,+\,Z_{\tilde{y}}\right]^N\,\left[ Z_x\,+\,Z_{\tilde{y}}\right]^K\right]\)  with \({Z_y}^{(N+K)}\) is again one. Please note that all of the  \(\,\left[ Z_x\,+\,Z_{\tilde{y}}\right]^K\) in above joint correlation have their own K+1 correlation coefficients in their expansion found earlier with a similar method on lower index K.
The last and most important constraint is that correlation between  \(H_N(Z_x)\) with \(H_N(Z_y)\)   is also retrieved from the same sets of values of \(\left[ Z_x\,+\,Z_{\tilde{y}}\right]^N\) and \(\left[ Z_x\,+\,Z_{\tilde{y}}\right]^K\) that have already been found and one missing degree of freedom would be solved with this equation of correlation between hermite polynomials.

All of these constraints can be framed in the form of linear equations that have to be solved recursively with above constraints for each value of N starting from one. In order to find N+1 correlation coefficients associated with binomial expansion of  \(\left[ Z_x\,+\,Z_{\tilde{y}}\right]^N\) , we would need to solve N+1 linear equations simultaneously.
