\subsection{Calculating the true distribution structure in Uni-variate Conditional Expectation of a Correlated Random variable when its variance is dependent on Explanatory Variable }
\label{sec4.4}

While calculating conditional expectation in previous post, we have assumed that variance of Y is independent of X which is most likely not the case. For example consider an SDE-like dependence equation of a random variable

\[ \,Y \,= \, \mu(X) \, + \, \sigma(X) \, W \]

where W would usually be a non-Gaussian random variable which is assumed to be independent of  \(\sigma(X) \,\) and \( \mu(X) \) and \( \sigma(X) \) are some functions of a random variable X. The first term \(\, \mu(X) \,\) should be perfectly captured by correlations in our conditional expectation equation but we see that variance term \(\,\,\sigma(X) \, Z\,\, \), in equation that describes Y, even though orthogonal to X  is not independent of X. So different realizations of X over its density should also affect and alter the distribution of variance of Y and we would have to do more analytics to calculate the effect of X on variance of Y for a right and proper calculation of conditional distribution of Y.
In order to find the true distribution associated with univariate conditional expectation of a random variable, we again perform the de-correlation operation discussed in previous subsection on jointly observed values of data sample of X and Y, we can find that \(\tilde{\tilde{Y}}\) represents the true residual part of Y that has not been explained by the variable X. 

This decorrelated value can be written as an equation as
\[ \tilde{\tilde{Y}} \,=\, Y\, -\, E\left[Y|X \right] \, \]

From section (), we already know that we can find a Z-series of \(\sigma(X(Z))\) through Taylor Series on Z-series of X as given in subsection()   

\[\sigma(X(Z_X))=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, d_n\, H_n(Z_X)\]

if \(\mu_{\sigma(X),n}\,\) represents nth moment of \(\sigma(X)\), We can find moments of \( \sigma(X)\) as 

\[\mu_{\sigma(X),n}\, =\, E\left[\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, d_n\, H_n(Z_X) \right]^n \]

Now we see that Z-series structure of \( \tilde{\tilde{Y}} \) with dependency structure included in equation is given as

\[ \tilde{\tilde{Y}} \,=\, \, \sigma(X) \,\,W\,=\, \, \sigma(X) \,\, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\, \tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\, H_m(Z_Y) \ \, \]
The random variable \(\,W\,\)  with Hermite Series representation given as \(\, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\, \tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\, H_m(Z_Y) \ \,\) with as yet undetermined coefficients is supposed to capture the distribution structure of non-Gaussian noise in the variance equation. And if \(\mu_{\tilde{\tilde{Y}},n}\,\) represents nth moment of \(\tilde{\tilde{Y}}\,\), this nth moment can be written because of independence between \(\,\mu_{\sigma(X),n}\,\) and  \(\, W \,\) as

\[\mu_{\tilde{\tilde{Y}},n}\, =\,\mu_{\sigma(X),n}\, \,\mu_{W,n}\, \, \]

Since \(\mu_{\tilde{\tilde{Y}},n}\,\) can be easily calculated from data of decorrelated values \(\tilde{\tilde{Y}} \), and we can also easily calculate the moments \(\mu_{\sigma(X),n}\,\) as earlier describe,  we get the equation for moments of non-Gaussian noise W as

\[ \, \mu_{W,n} \, \,= \, \frac{\mu_{\tilde{\tilde{Y}},n}\,}{\,\mu_{\sigma(X),n}}\,\]

Once we have found the moments of non-Gaussian noise given as  \(\, \mu_{W,n} \, \), we can use these moments with multidimensional Newton root search method to estimate coefficients  \(\tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\,\)  as  described earlier in section (3.) using the equations

\[ E \, \left[\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\, \tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\, H_m(Z_Y)\right]^n \,=\,\, \mu_{W,n} \]

Then conditional distribution of Y would be given as

\[ Y\, | \, X=\, E \left[ Y|X \right] + \, \tilde{\tilde{Y}}(Z_Y)\,\]

\[ Y\, | \, X=\mu_Y+\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\frac{Cov_{XY,n}}{Var_{X,n}}\,b_n \, H_n(Z_X)+\,\, \left[ \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, d_n \, H_n(Z_X) \right] \, \left[\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\, \tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\, H_m(Z_Y)\right] \]

\[ Y\, | \, X=\mu_Y+\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,\rho_{XY,n} \, c_n \, H_n(Z_X)+\,\, \left[ \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, d_n \, H_n(Z_X) \right] \, \left[\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\, \tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\, H_m(Z_Y)\right] \]

Again  once coefficients in above equation have been calculated from data, we can use it for conditional distribution of random variable Y as single (or more) values of X become available just as easily as we did for correlation based conditional distribution.

Friends, I want to emphasize that once we have been given two two distributions, and we are asked to find conditional/transition distribution between them. Important thing is to know that choice of conditional/transition distribution between two fixed distributions is not unique at all and there are so many ways we can find conditional/transition distributions between two densities to achieve perfectly the same structure of both given fixed distributions.  
So if probability density of first random variable X is given as \(P_X(X)\) and probability density of another dependent correlated variable Y is given as \(P_Y(Y)\)  then P(Y|X) is given as so that 

\[P_Y(Y)\,=\, \int \, P(Y|X) \, P_X(X) \, dX\]   Eq(1)

Now this choice of \(P(Y|X) \) is not unique at all and there are so many possible forms of \( P(Y|X)\)  so that above relationship between \(P_Y(Y)\) and  \(P_X(X)\) is perfectly satisfied.
So we can have an independent variance in our conditional probability distribution of  P_1(Y|X) and we could also have another conditional probability distribution P_2(Y|X)  where we have specified a functional dependence of conditional variance of Y on X and both conditional distributions P_1(Y|X) and P_2(Y|X) would perfectly satisfy the relationship in Eq(1) because of non-uniqueness of conditional probability density of Y given X once both distributions of Y and X have been specified.
So important thing to know is that information relevant to problem at hand has to be embedded while calculating transition distributions/conditional distributions since it is equally possible to construct condtional distributions that are very ill suited for the problem at hand but they perfectly satisfy the conditional/transition distribution relationship given in Equation(1) between both the specified distributions.

\subsection{Calculating Parameters of an SDE that Define the true transition Probability distribution When starting distribution and end distribution of the SDE across a time interval are exogenously specified }
\label{sec4.4}

Since an SDE is driven by a Gaussian noise in volatility term and distribution of this Gaussian noise is already fixed, so all the burden of calibration to both starting and ending distribution across an interval falls on parameters of the SDE. In this subsection, we discuss numerical algorithm that has to be followed to calibrate parameters of an SDE so that its transition probability when applied to starting distribution, the distribution at the end of the interval is perfectly retrieved. 
In order to make the exposition more clear, we first define the equation for an SDE whose parameters have to be determined from start and end distributions across an interval. We define the SDE as

\[dX(t)\,=\,\left( \, \mu_1\, {X(t)}^{\beta_1} \,+\, \mu_2\, {X(t)}^{\beta_2} \, \right) \,dt\,+\, \sigma_0 \, {X(t)}^{\gamma} \, dZ(t) \, \]

We define 

\[\mu(X(t))\,=\,\, \mu_1\, {X(t)}^{\beta_1} \,+\, \mu_2\, {X(t)}^{\beta_2} \]
and 

\[\sigma(X(t))\,=\, \sigma_0 \,{X(t)}^{\gamma} \]

so 

\[X(t_2)\,=\,X(t_1)\,+  \mu(X_t)\,dt \,+\, \sigma(X(t)) \, dZ(t) \, \]

When distribution of \(X(t_2)\) and \(X(t_1)\) have been exogeneously specified, only free parameters that can calibrate the transition disribution \(P(X(t_2)|X(t_1))\) are parameters of the SDE given by \(\mu_1, \, \mu_2, \, \beta_1, \, \beta_2, \sigma_0, \,\) and  \(\gamma \)

We also want to emphasize since distribution of \(X(t_1)\) is known, we can easily find its Z-series representation.

\[X(t_1)\,=\,  \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N} c_n \, {Z_1}^n\]

We can also calculate Z-series of \(\mu(X(t))\,\) and \(\sigma(X(t))\,\) through Taylor series since their functional form is already known. We represent these Z-series as

\[\mu(X(t))\,=\,\,\mu(Z_1,\Theta) \]
and 
\[\sigma(X(t))\,=\,\,\sigma(Z_1,\Theta) \]
where \(\Theta\) signifies the dependence of drift and volatility functions on parameters of the SDE given as \(\mu_1, \, \mu_2, \, \beta_1, \, \beta_2, \sigma_0, \,\) and  \(\gamma \)

Now we discretize the SDE () to second order as 
\begin{multline*}
 \, X(t_2) \, - \, X(t_1) \, = \, \mu(X(t_1) \Delta t \, + \, \sigma(X(t_1)) Z \sqrt{\Delta t} \\ + \, \frac{\partial \mu(X(t_1))}{\partial X} \, \mu(X(t_1)) \, \frac{{\Delta t}^2}{2} \, + \, \frac{\partial \mu(X(t_1))}{\partial X} \, \sigma(X(t_1)) \, \left(1 \, - \, \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \right) \, Z \, {\Delta t}^{1.5} \\ +\, .5  \, \frac{\partial^2 \mu(X(t_1))}{\partial X^2} \, {\sigma(X(t_1))}^2 \, \frac{{\Delta t}^2}{2} \,  + \, \frac{\partial \sigma(X(t_1))}{\partial X} \, \mu(X(t_1)) \,  \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \right) \, Z \, {\Delta t}^{1.5} \, \\+ \, \frac{\partial \mu(X(t_1))}{\partial X} \, \sigma(X(t_1)) \, (Z^2\, - \, 1) \, \frac{\Delta t}{2}  +\, .5  \, \frac{\partial^2 \sigma(X(t_1))}{\partial X^2} \, {\sigma(X(t_1))}^2 \, \,  \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \right) \, Z \, {\Delta t}^{1.5} \,
 \end{multline*}

We rearrange the above equation as

\[\, X(t_2) \, - X_(t_1) \, = h_0(Z_1,\Theta) \, + \,h_1(Z_1,\Theta) \,Z \,+ \,h_2(Z_1,\Theta) \,(\,Z^2\,-\,1\,) \, \]

Please note that \(Z_1\) and \(Z\) in above equation are both independent of each other.
where  \( h_0(Z_1,\Theta) \) is Z-series representation of terms without any Z or \((\,Z^2\,-\,1\, )\) in them and is found from application of Taylor series on Z-series of X(t) 
\begin{multline*}
 \, h_0(Z_1,\Theta) \, = \, \, \mu(X(t_1) \Delta t \,+ \, \frac{\partial \mu(X(t_1))}{\partial X} \, \mu(X(t_1)) \, \frac{{\Delta t}^2}{2} \,+\, .5  \, \frac{\partial^2 \mu(X(t_1))}{\partial X^2} \, {\sigma(X(t_1))}^2 \, \frac{{\Delta t}^2}{2} \,
 \end{multline*}

 and   \(h_1(Z_1,\Theta)\) is Z-series representation of terms multiplying Z.
 \begin{multline*}
 h_1(Z_1,\Theta) \,=\, \sigma(X(t_1)) \sqrt{\Delta t} + \, \frac{\partial \mu(X(t_1))}{\partial X} \, \sigma(X(t_1)) \, \left( 1 \, - \, \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \right) \, {\Delta t}^{1.5}+ \, \frac{\partial \sigma(X(t_1))}{\partial X} \, \mu(X(t_1)) \,  \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \right) \, {\Delta t}^{1.5} \, \\+\, .5  \, \frac{\partial^2 \sigma(X(t_1))}{\partial X^2} \, {\sigma(X(t_1))}^2 \, \,  \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \right) \, {\Delta t}^{1.5} \,
 \end{multline*}

 
\[h_2(Z_1,\Theta) \,=\, \, \frac{\partial \mu(X(t_1))}{\partial X} \, \sigma(X(t_1)) \, \frac{\Delta t}{2} \]


For joint calibration of SDE transition probabilities to distribution of \(X(t_1)\) and \(X(t_2)\), we could alter the parameters of the SDE given as \(\mu_1, \, \mu_2, \, \beta_1, \, \beta_2, \sigma_0, \,\) and  \(\gamma \)
so that in equation
\[\, X(t_2) \,- \, X(t_1)\, = h_0(Z_1,\Theta) \, + \,h_1(Z_1,\Theta) \,Z \,+ \,h_2(Z_1,\Theta) \,(\,Z^2\,-\,1\,) \, \]

moments of LHS \(E \left[ { \left( \, X(t_2) \,-\, X(t_1) \right)}^n \right] \) in above equation perfectly match the moments of RHS calculated as 

\[ \, E \left[ \left( \, h_0(Z_1,\Theta) \, + \,h_1(Z_1,\Theta) \,Z \,+ \,h_2(Z_1,\Theta) \,(\,Z^2\,-\,1\,) \, \right)^n \right]\]

This can either be done by iterative optimization over parameters of  \(\Theta\)  for each iteration which we slowly alter the coefficients \(\mu_1, \, \mu_2, \, \beta_1, \, \beta_2, \sigma_0, \,\) and  \(\gamma \) and recalculate  \( h_0(Z_1,\Theta) \,, \,h_1(Z_1,\Theta) \,Z \, \) and \( \,h_2(Z_1,\Theta) \,(\,Z^2\,-\,1\,) \) and then find moments associated with RHS of Eq() and then check how well moments of RHS match with moments of terminal distribution of \(X_2\) while perturbing the SDE parametrs slowly in the direction of better fit and improving objective function.

 When close enough, we can also use multi-dimensional Newton method to find the parameters of the SDE.

\subsection{Calculating the true distribution structure in Uni-variate Conditional Expectation of a Correlated Random variable when its variance is dependent on Explanatory Variable }
\label{sec4.4}

While calculating conditional expectation in previous post, we have assumed that variance of Y is independent of X which is most likely not the case. For example consider an SDE-like dependence equation of a random variable

\[ \,Y \,= \, \mu(X) \, + \, \sigma(X) \, W \]

where W would usually be a non-Gaussian random variable which is assumed to be independent of  \(\sigma(X) \,\) and \( \mu(X) \) and \( \sigma(X) \) are some functions of a random variable X. The first term \(\, \mu(X) \,\) should be perfectly captured by correlations in our conditional expectation equation but we see that variance term \(\,\,\sigma(X) \, W\,\, \), in equation that describes Y, even though orthogonal to X  is not independent of X. So different realizations of X over its density should also affect and alter the distribution of variance of Y and we would have to do more analytics to calculate the effect of X on variance of Y for a right and proper calculation of conditional distribution of Y.

This de-correlated value can be written as an equation as
\[ \tilde{\tilde{Y}} \,=\, Y\, -\, E\left[Y|X \right] \, \]

\[\sigma(X(Z_X))=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, d_n\, H_n(Z_X)\]

if \(\mu_{\sigma(X),n}\,\) represents nth moment of \(\sigma(X)\), We can find moments of \( \sigma(X)\) as 

\[\mu_{\sigma(X),n}\, =\, E\left[\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, d_n\, H_n(Z_X) \right]^n \]

Now we see that Z-series structure of \( \tilde{\tilde{Y}} \) with dependency structure included in equation is given as

\[ \tilde{\tilde{Y}} \,=\, \, \sigma(X) \,\,W\,=\, \, \sigma(X) \,\, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\, \tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\, H_m(Z_Y) \ \, \]
The random variable \(\,W\,\)  with Hermite Series representation given as \(\, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\, \tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\, H_m(Z_Y) \ \,\) with as yet undetermined coefficients is supposed to capture the distribution structure of non-Gaussian noise in the variance equation. And if \(\mu_{\tilde{\tilde{Y}},n}\,\) represents nth moment of \(\tilde{\tilde{Y}}\,\), this nth moment can be written because of independence between \(\,\mu_{\sigma(X),n}\,\) and  \(\, W \,\) as

\[\mu_{\tilde{\tilde{Y}},n}\, =\,\mu_{\sigma(X),n}\, \,\mu_{W,n}\, \, \]

Since \(\mu_{\tilde{\tilde{Y}},n}\,\) can be easily calculated from data of de-correlated values \(\tilde{\tilde{Y}} \), and we can also easily calculate the moments \(\mu_{\sigma(X),n}\,\) as earlier describe,  we get the equation for moments of non-Gaussian noise W as

\[ \, \mu_{W,n} \, \,= \, \frac{\mu_{\tilde{\tilde{Y}},n}\,}{\,\mu_{\sigma(X),n}}\,\]

Once we have found the moments of non-Gaussian noise given as  \(\, \mu_{W,n} \, \), we can use these moments with multidimensional Newton root search method to estimate coefficients  \(\tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\,\)  as  described earlier in section (3.) using the equations

\[ E \, \left[\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\, \tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\, H_m(Z_Y)\right]^n \,=\,\, \mu_{W,n} \]

Then conditional distribution of Y would be given as

\[ Y\, | \, X=\, E \left[ Y|X \right] + \, \tilde{\tilde{Y}}(Z_Y)\,\]

\[ Y\, | \, X=\mu_Y+\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\frac{Cov_{XY,n}}{Var_{X,n}}\,b_n \, H_n(Z_X)+\,\, \left[ \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, d_n \, H_n(Z_X) \right] \, \left[\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\, \tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\, H_m(Z_Y)\right] \]

\[ Y\, | \, X=\mu_Y+\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,\rho_{XY,n} \, c_n \, H_n(Z_X)+\,\, \left[ \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, d_n \, H_n(Z_X) \right] \, \left[\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\, \tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\, H_m(Z_Y)\right] \]

Again  once coefficients in above equation have been calculated from data, we can use it for conditional distribution of random variable Y as single (or more) values of X become available just as easily as we did for correlation based conditional distribution.

After calculation of \(Y\, | \, X\) , we describe how to find the probability distribution \(p \left(X\, | \, Y\,=\,Y_0 \right) \)  of X given an observation of \(Y=Y_0\). For calculation of \(p \left( X\, | \, Y\,=\,Y_0 \right) \), we will use Bayes formula but First we define \(Z_{Y_0}\,|\, Z_{X_0} \) as the particular value of \(Z_Y\) that solves for \(Y=Y_0\) given \(X=X_0\) in Eq () above. \(Z_{Y_0}\,|\, Z_{X_0} \) is the particular value of \(Z_Y\) that equates the LHS of Eq() to \(Y_0\) given \(X=X_0\) and has to be backed out from the equation below as

\[ Y_0\, | \, X_0 =\mu_Y+\,\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\,\rho_{XY,n} \, c_n \, H_n(Z_{X_0})+\,\, \left[ \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\, d_n \, H_n(Z_{X_0}) \right] \, \left[\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\, \tilde{\tilde{c}}_m\, H_m(Z_Y)\right] \]

We invert above equation for \(Z_{Y}\) to find the value of \(Z_{Y_0}\,|\, Z_{X_0} \) since this is the particular value of \(Z_{Y}\) that relates the observation of \(X=X_0\) with observation of \(Y=Y_0\) through conditional expectation formula.
Below we calculate the probability of starting from a point \(Z_{X_0}\) associated with an arbitrary point \(X_0\) on density of X and going to point \(Y=Y_0\) on the density of second random variable.
\[p( \left(Y=Y_0 \right)\, \cap \, Z_{X_0} ) \, =\, p(Z_{Y_0}|Z_{X_0}) \, p(Z_{X_0}) \] 
\[=\, \phi(Z_{Y_0}|Z_X) \, \phi(Z_{X_0}) \] 

where \(\phi(X)\,=\, \frac{\exp \left(-.5\, X^2 \right)}{\sqrt{2\, \pi}} \) is symbol for normal density value at X.

 We drop the subscript zero on \(Z_{X_0}\) and simply write it as \(Z_{X}\) for the sake of integration
\[p(Y=Y_0)\,\, \, =\,\int\limits_{Z_X=-\infty}^{\infty} \phi(Z_{Y_0}|Z_{X}) \, \phi(Z_X) dZ_X \, \] 

We know from Bayes formula

\[p(Z_X\,|\, Y=Y_0)\,=\,\frac{p(\left(Y=Y_0 \right)\, \cap \, Z_{X} )}{p(Y=Y_0)}\]
so
\[p(Z_X\,|\, Y=Y_0)\,=\,\frac{\, \phi(Z_{Y_0}|Z_X) \, \phi(Z_{X}) }{\,\int\limits_{Z_X=-\infty}^{\infty} \phi(Z_{Y_0}|Z_{X}) \, \phi(Z_X) dZ_X }\]

further  since the probability densities of \(Z_x\) and \(X\) are related as

\[p_X(X) \,=\, p_Z(Z_X) \, \frac{d Z_X}{dX} \] we can find the desired conditional probability density of X as

\[p(X\,|\, Y=Y_0)\,=\,\frac{\, \phi(Z_{Y_0}|Z_X) \, \phi(Z_{X}) \, \frac{d Z_X}{dX}}{\,\int\limits_{Z_X=-\infty}^{\infty} \phi(Z_{Y_0}|Z_{X}) \, \phi(Z_X) dZ_X }\]
Equation(AA)
where 
\[ \frac{d X}{dZ_X}\,=\, \sum\limits_{n=0}^{N-1}\,(n+1)\, b_{n+1}\, {Z_X}^n \]

The above equation (AA) gives probability density of \(X|\, Y=Y_0 \) on an arbitrary value of random variable X (arbitrary point on density of X). In order to numerically construct a complete density, we would have to make a grid of points \(X_m\) on density of X and find the probability density on all grid points through the Equation(AA) above. Integration in denominator of Eq(AA) would have to be done on the grid of points \(Z_{X_m}\) associated with grid of \(X_m\) on density of X. Also not including \( \frac{d Z_X}{dX}\) in the denominator is not an error since integration in variable X or variable Z_x would give the same result since \(\int \, p(Z_X) \, dZ_X\,=\,\int \, p(X) \, dX\, \)   

If needed, we can represent the above equation of conditional probability \(p(X\,|\, Y=Y_0)\, \)as a Z-series by one of the methods discussed in section 3.
