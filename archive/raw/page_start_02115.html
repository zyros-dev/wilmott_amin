Friends, this is a simple program that multiplies a bivariate 2D Density with another Bivariate 2D Density.
You can multiply a variable repeatedly with itself to get its powers and also find expectations of those powers.
There is no bound on size of the series and their results per se but of course extremely high values of expectations of large powers of normal density may create problems in calculation of their expectations.
I have given the variables below to represent as they appear in program. Variables(X1,X2,X3) do not appear in program input rather coefficients(aa,bb,cc) are input to program.

Friends, I have written the code for calculation of moments and their derivatives with respect to coefficients for root search and I had been thinking about generating the initial value for multidimensional Newton Method. 
And I think I have a very good analytical strategy to get the initial transition/conditional probabilities that might possibly work very well in its own right even when used only alone.
Here I will summarize my strategy for friends. 
We have two distributions Y and X and we want to calculate conditional/transition probabilities from each point of Y to various points of X(So that we attain the marginal probability of X when we sum over transition probabilities).
First I want to run a hermite polynomials regression from the Z-series density of Y to Z-series density of X. This will give us the correlated part of X in terms of coefficients in Y(Zy). We call this part of density of X as XCorr and it will be a function of Zy.
We can represent X as a sum of first part that is correlated with Y and a second part that is uncorrelated with Y.

[$]X(Z_{x1})\, = \, XCorr(Z_y) \, + \, XUncorr(Z_{x2}) \,[$]

The univariate Z-series of X on LHS is already calculated from data. On RHS, in first term the Zy based Z-series of XCorr(Zy) would come from the hermite polynomial regression expression and will be known from there. And then We will use moment subtraction(just like moment addition we have used so many times) to subtract the density of XCorr(Zy) from complete X(Zx1) to get the Z-series density of uncorrelated part XUncorr(Zx2). 

We define another variable XX as 

[$]\, XX \, = \, \frac{XUncorr}{Y} \, [$] 

and find its nth raw moment as  (due to no correlation, raw moments on LHS would be found from division of raw moments of XUncorr and Y)

[$]\, \mu_n(XX)\, = \, \frac{\mu_n(XUncorr)}{\mu_n(Y)}\,[$]

From these moments we will get the Z-series density of XX(Zx). We will do this Z-series construction from the earlier method that constructs the Z-series density once raw  moments are known.

Now the expression for transition/conditional probabilities of X from Y would be given as

[$]X(Z_y,Z_x)=\, XCorr(Z_y) \, +\, XX(Z_x) \,* \,Y(Z_y) \,[$] 

I am quite sure that above 2D Z-series would be extremely close to true 2D conditional Z-series.
