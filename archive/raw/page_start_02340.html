I am still trying to see how to comprehensively approach this but some basic ideas are here.

I think we have to think about average correlation, point correlation and density of correlation.

Average Auto-Correlations between hermite polynomials = [$] \Large{\frac{ \sum\limits_{t=0}^{t=T-1}\, H_n(t)\, H_n(t+1)}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{t=0}^{t=T-1} \, H_n(t)^2 \, \sum\limits_{t=0}^{t=T-1} \, H_n(t+1)^2\,}}}[$] 
Since this is auto-correlation, we can write the average correlation as
[$]\approx  \, \Large{\frac{ \sum\limits_{t=0}^{t=T-1}\, H_n(t)\, H_n(t+1)}{\sum\limits_{t=0}^{t=T-1} \, H_n(t)^2 \,}}[$] 

While as a start, we can think of point auto-correlation at a certain time as

[$]\approx  \, \Large{\frac{ \, H_n(t)\, H_n(t+1)}{\sum\limits_{t=0}^{t=T-1} \, H_n(t)^2 \,}}[$] 

The above formula might have some problems with normalization as sometimes it will lead to numbers greater than one but I think it can be fixed(more on it later after I could verify in my experiments) but I think it can nevertheless be useful.
From the above point correlations, we can calculate the density of correlation and average correlation over the density would be equal to average correlation calculated from  the first formula.

I think we have to move from average correlations to point correlations so we can introduce dynamic correlation framework as opposed to static correlations we usually use in our analytics.

[$]\approx  \, \Large{\frac{ \sum\limits_{t=0}^{t=T-1}\, H_n(t)\, H_n(t+1)}{\sum\limits_{t=0}^{t=T-1} \, H_n(t)^2 \,}}[$] 

While as a start, we can think of point auto-correlation at a certain time as

[$]\approx  \, \Large{\frac{ \, H_n(t)\, H_n(t+1)}{\sum\limits_{t=0}^{t=T-1} \, H_n(t)^2 \,}}[$] 

The above formula might have some problems with normalization as sometimes it will lead to numbers greater than one but I think it can be fixed(more on it later after I could verify in my experiments) but I think it can nevertheless be useful.
From the above point correlations, we can calculate the density of correlation and average correlation over the density would be equal to average correlation calculated from  the first formula.

I think we have to move from average correlations to point correlations so we can introduce dynamic correlation framework as opposed to static correlations we usually use in our analytics.

Friends, let us suppose we have financial data of equities on a 15 sec grid (My data is for 41 working days from May and June of 2021). We have a universe of M stocks and The price of mth stock at time t is given as [$]X_m(t)[$]. We convert these stock values into log returns so they are easy to use in our set up.

[$]R_m(t)\,=\, \log(X_m(t))\,-\, \log(X_m(t-1))[$]

You can use your own definition of returns and possibly label above as  [$]R_m(t-1)\,[$] or divide by time increment etc. 

From the moments of above returns, we make a Z-series/Hermite-series representation of returns of each asset. I made a hermite series of returns from previous day and used it for next day (but that was because I was a bit lazy) and you can add information to Z-series from running day at several discrete points in time. This Z-series expansion of returns is given as

[$]R_m(t)\,=\, a_0\, +\, a_1 \, Z_m(t)\,+\, a_2 \, {Z_m(t)}^2\,+\, a_3 \, {Z_m(t)}^3\,+\, \ldots[$]

During the market time on 15 seconds grid, whenever we notice a new return [$]R_m(t)[$] in the market, we immediately know the associated [$]Z_m(t)[$] in the market. 
For positive returns, the [$]Z_m(t)[$] would likely be positive and for negative returns [$]Z_m(t)[$] would likely be positive. Similarly for very large positive returns [$]Z_m(t)[$] would likely be very large positive and vice versa. 

We can make very intelligent indicators from knowledge of [$]Z_m(t)[$] itself and also from its values from universe of M stocks. For example market continues to fluctuate between positive and negative states and these states last from half a minute to 2-3 minutes. Market acts like a sinusoidal with a lot of noise in it. So if we want to know the current state of the market, we can make an indicator for that by summing all the [$]Z_m[$] across the M stocks over 3-4 15 second periods as

[$]IM_{state}\, =\, \sum\limits_{k=0}^{3} \, \sum\limits_{m=1}^{M} \, w_{m,k}\, Z_m(t-k)\,[$] 

The above indicator will describe the current state of the market (that is reasonably fleeting and will change in a minute or two).
